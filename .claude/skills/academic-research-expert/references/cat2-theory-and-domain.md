## Persona 5: Senior Professor of Institutional Sociology and Organisational Behaviour

**Guards:** construct precision, and the claim to contribute *to* institutional theory, not just apply it *to* Brisbane.

**Signature question:** "Decoupling of what from what, for whom, under what pressure, and how would you know?"

### Genealogy the candidate must command [C unless tagged]
- **Founding works (1977):** Meyer & Rowan; Zucker.
- **Isomorphism:** DiMaggio & Powell (1983).
- **Diffusion:** Tolbert & Zucker (1983).
- **Legitimacy:** Suchman (1995).
- **Logics:** Thornton, Ocasio & Lounsbury (2012).
- **Institutional work:** Lawrence & Suddaby (2006).
- **Complexity:** Greenwood et al. (2011).
- **Micro-foundations:** Powell & Colyvas (2008).
- **Scott,** *Institutions and Organizations*, 4th ed. (2014), is the latest [V]. Citing the 3rd as current is an error.
- **Boxenbaum & Jonsson (2017),** in the SAGE Handbook of Organizational Institutionalism, 2nd ed. [V]. Cite this rather than the 2008 chapter.
- **Critique:** Alvesson & Spicer (2019), on the theory's "mid-life crisis" of over-reach and tautology [V].

### Ask the candidate to name the lineage
"Decoupling" means different things in different traditions:
- **Meyer & Rowan:** a structural buffer.
- **Brunsson:** talk–decision–action inconsistency.
- **Oliver:** strategic avoidance.
- **Logics work:** one option alongside selective coupling.
- **Bromley & Powell; Wijen:** a means–ends gap.

### Core debate [V at record or abstract level]
- **Bromley & Powell (2012), *AMA* 6(1), doi 10.1080/19416520.2012.684462:** distinguish policy–practice decoupling from means–ends decoupling. Quote the definitions from the original.
- **Wijen (2014), *AMR* 39(3), 302–323:** opacity pushes rule-makers toward rigid rules that secure compliance but limit goal achievement. So fixing policy–practice decoupling can widen means–ends decoupling.
- **Haack & Schoeneborn (2015):** ask whether decoupling is "becoming decoupled" from institutional theory. Wijen's rejoinder argues coupling should be the focus.
- **Kern, Laguecir & Leca (2018), *OS*:** power and politics. Institutionally complex contexts are propitious for decoupling.
- **de Bree & Stoopendaal (2020), *OS*:** inspections that exposed inconsistencies led to recoupling.
- **Athanasopoulou et al. (2025), *JMS*:** experimental implementation restrains means–ends decoupling; consistency-oriented implementation fosters it.
- **Kaufmann, Krlev & Brown (2024/25), *OS*:** systemic opacity in impact measurement raises the risk of decoupling.
- **Cho et al. (2015), *AOS*:** organised hypocrisy operationalised through talk, decisions and actions in public documents.
- **Okhmatovskiy & David (2012):** the specificity of a governance code as an indicator of ceremonial adoption.
- **[C]:** Crilly et al. (2012); Tilcsik (2010); Hallett (2010) on recoupling; Dick (2015); Power (1997); Espeland & Sauder (2007).
- **Olympic applications:** no explicit means–ends decoupling study of Olympic delivery was found. Treat this as a hypothesis to test with a systematic Scopus or Web of Science search, not a confirmed gap. Check that **Geeraert & Gauthier (2018)** exists before citing it.

### Operational scheme: the commitment chain [I]
For each stated end E, record:
- its institutional source;
- the stated means M;
- implementation traces I (procurement, budget papers, annual reports, minutes, RTI, Hansard);
- outcome provisions O;
- the causal theory C linking M to E.

Classify each chain:
- **Policy–practice decoupling:** M or E is stated, but I is absent.
- **Means–ends decoupling:** I is present and compliant, but O measures only outputs or proxies, or C is missing or implausible, or no continuing organisation owns E.
- **Coupling:** M, I and O align, with feedback.
- **Selective coupling:** some components are coupled, while legacy sits in a unit without decision rights.

**Ceremonial indicators:**
- boilerplate lifted from IOC templates;
- aspirational wording with no owner, budget or date;
- output-only KPIs and no baseline;
- evaluation deferred until after the Games;
- legacy bodies without budget authority;
- unexplained target drift;
- commitments that appear only in front-stage genres.

**Substantive indicators:**
- budget lines and contract clauses;
- baselined targets;
- owners whose role continues past 2032;
- independent assurance with consequences;
- documented revision after evaluation.

Order document versions in time: 2021 candidature → Host Contract → 100 Day Review (2025) → 2032 Delivery Plan. Use the constructs as sensitising concepts and for pattern matching, not as a closed codebook.

### Mechanism hypotheses for Brisbane [I]
- Goal proliferation across the IOC, Commonwealth, State, council and First Nations commitments.
- Causal opacity in the tourism ends.
- Measurement proxies.
- Temporary organisations that dissolve before the ends mature.
- Audits and reviews that either recouple to the ends or tighten coupling to budget-and-time proxies.

Keep three outcomes distinct: recoupling to ends, tight coupling to proxies (Sauder & Espeland, 2009), and re-legitimation without changed practice. **Loose coupling** (Weick, 1976; Orton & Weick, 1990) is a structural property. It is not legitimacy-driven decoupling.

### Safeguards
- A construct glossary.
- A **decoupling claim protocol:** pressure → audience → adoption → separation → rivals ruled out.
- A search for negative cases.
- Reflexive notes on the candidate's own stance toward the Olympics.

### Questions to put to the candidate
- State the decoupling claim in one sentence. Name its form, and say why it isn't the others.
- For each end, give the document, clause and date.
- Who is the legitimating audience? Show evidence for that audience that is independent of the adoption itself.
- Show a coupled chain for contrast.
- Rule out the rival explanations.
- What does a temporary, multi-level, pre-event setting teach institutional theory that firm-level CSR studies cannot?

### Red flags
- Treating any gap as decoupling, without first ruling out incapacity, shocks, cost escalation or re-prioritisation.
- Tautology.
- Treating decoupling as greenwashing.
- Judging means against ends the researcher prefers.
- Reading talk as practice.
- Ignoring power.
- Assuming coupling is always good.
- Slipping between field, organisation and programme levels.
- Treating decoupling as a state rather than a process.
- Edition errors.
- "Testing" decoupling theory with a single pre-event case.

### Candidate contributions [I]
- Means–ends decoupling in temporary, multi-organisation delivery systems (Lundin & Söderholm, 1995, on temporary organisations, is a possible link).
- Promissory, pre-event decoupling.
- Formal reviews as triggers of either recoupling or re-legitimation.

**Confidence:** moderate to high for the 2012–2025 debate; moderate for the canon; low for the claim that no Olympic decoupling study exists.

## Persona 6: Senior Academic Theorists (theory fusion and mechanisms)

**Owns:** which theories may be combined, whether their assumptions are compatible, which constructs bridge them, which mechanisms are specified, and whether the result reaches Level 10 originality.

### Verified anchors
- **Oswick, Fleming & Hanlon (2011):** one-way borrowing of theory yields few original ideas; argues for two-way blending.
- **Okhuysen & Bonardi (2011):** judge a combination by how close the phenomena are and how compatible the assumptions are. The content is recalled and needs checking [U].
- **Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl (2021):** theory takes propositional, process, configurational, perspectival and meta-theorising forms.
- **Polzer et al. (2016):** the closest template. Read public administration paradigms as institutional logics, and distinguish layering from blending. Found layering in Austrian parliamentary debates on budget reform.

### Public administration as layered repertoires, not eras [C]
- **NPM:** Hood (1991).
- **Target gaming:** Bevan & Hood (2006).
- **NPG:** Osborne (2006), rooted in institutional and network theory. This is the main justification for fusing PA with organisational sociology.
- **Public value:** Moore (1995); Rhodes & Wanna's (2007) Australian critique.
- **Network governance:** Provan & Kenis (2008); Goodwin & Grix (2011) in sport.
- **Metagovernance:** Sørensen & Torfing (2009).
- **Collaborative governance:** Ansell & Gash (2008); Emerson, Nabatchi & Balogh (2012).
- **Accountability:** Bovens (2007); Koppell (2005) on "multiple accountabilities disorder".
- **Agencies and depoliticisation:** Pollitt et al. (2004); van Thiel (2004); Flinders & Buller (2006).
- **Pollitt (2001):** talk, decisions, practice and results. This is the closest PA counterpart to decoupling.
- **The three institutionalisms:** Hall & Taylor (1996).

**Expected Brisbane hybrid:** NPM-style statutory delivery authorities, inside Commonwealth–State–Council agreements, under the IOC's contractual metagovernance.

### Mechanism families [C]
- **Analytical sociology:** Hedström & Swedberg; Hedström & Ylikoski (2010); Coleman's boat.
- **Field accounts:** Gross (2009); Davis & Marquis (2005).
- **Context and process tracing:** Falleti & Lynch (2009); Beach & Pedersen; Collier (2011).
- **Critical realism and historical institutionalism:** Bhaskar, Archer, Sayer; Pierson; Capoccia & Kelemen (2007); Mahoney & Thelen (2010) on displacement, layering, drift and conversion.
- **Structuration bridge:** Barley & Tolbert (1997).

**Recommended stance:** critical realism as the meta-theory; historical institutionalism to sequence the bid, award, delivery and post-Games phases; institutional and PA theory as the substantive content.

### Six candidate mechanisms (hypotheses to trace, not findings) [I]
1. **Output-contract substitution:** venues on time and on budget stand in for legacy ends.
2. **Temporal compression and sunset:** delivery dominates, and legacy drifts after the delivery bodies wind up.
3. **Depoliticised accountability:** accountability shifts to forums that judge means, not ends.
4. **Metagovernance-induced ceremonial conformity** to the IOC's requirements.
5. **Layering** of legacy institutions onto a delivery logic.
6. **Network diffusion of responsibility,** leaving "orphaned ends".

Following Falleti & Lynch, the thesis must say which Australian features are part of a mechanism, not just background: federal fiscal relations, Westminster ministerial responsibility, statutory authorities, electoral cycles.

### Commensurability
Fusing institutional theory with NPM is the hardest pairing: calculating actors versus a logic of appropriateness. NPG, network governance and historical institutionalism fuse more easily.

Four justifications for a combination: ontological mapping (Polzer); shared ancestry in Simon, March and Selznick; dividing the explanatory labour by level or time; a meta-theoretical umbrella.

**Combination protocol:** state the explanandum → list each theory's assumptions → define the bridging constructs (legitimacy, performance regime, accountability forum, structural disaggregation) → choose the mode of combination and the form of theory → set boundary conditions → specify the documentary traces → say what changes in each parent theory.

### Seven-item contribution test
1. A theory delta: "before, theory said X; now we know Y."
2. At least one mechanism, specified with its entities, activities and conditions.
3. Boundary conditions that are themselves theorised.
4. Portability to Expos, Commonwealth Games, city deals, megaprojects and disaster-recovery authorities.
5. Two-way blending.
6. A problematised assumption. Example: decoupling theory implicitly assumes a stable, permanent organisation.
7. A recognisable artefact: a process model, typology or set of propositions.

"Application only" looks like theories listed side by side and then used as labels.

**Candidate contributions:**
- Temporal means–ends decoupling in time-bound, special-purpose governance.
- Nested metagovernance.
- A typology of legacy-governance arrangements.
- A process model of legacy commitments moving through layering, drift and conversion.

### Red flags
- "Decoupling" used for any plan–outcome gap.
- Paradigms treated as eras ("Brisbane 2032 is NPG").
- Six or more theories with no integration section.
- Mechanisms inferred from strategy documents alone.
- Teleological legacy narratives.
- Claims about legacy outcomes before the Games.
- Mega-event exceptionalism.
- An untheorised Australian context.
- A "new paradigm" overclaim, when Australian examiners expect a bounded contribution.

**Confidence:** low to moderate on verification; nearly everything here is [C].

## Persona 7: Mega-Event Governance Experts

**Role:** the "delivery realist". Ask of every claim: which document, which version, which party is bound, who bears the risk? Is it a promise, a plan or an outcome?

### Contractual web [V]
- The Brisbane 2032 Olympic **Host Contract** was executed on **21 July 2021** by the IOC, Brisbane City Council, the State of Queensland and the Australian Olympic Committee.
- **The Commonwealth is not a party.** Its role sits in a separate intergovernmental agreement.
- The Organising Committee joined the contract by joinder on **19 May 2022**.
- **QAO, *Preparing for the Brisbane Games*** (Report 5: 2024–25, 13 Dec 2024): no consolidated budget; the State carries the greatest contractual and financial exposure and underwrites the OC; conclusions given at **limited assurance**. It also found the governance structure well designed, with "no evidence … ineffective".
- Host Contract clause content (the IOC's contribution, surplus sharing, termination, disputes) was **not retrieved** [U].

### Brisbane 2032 chronology, as at 25 October 2026 [V unless tagged]

| Date | Event | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2024 | Quirk 60-day review recommends a ~$3.4bn Victoria Park stadium and finds QSAC "does not represent value for money"; Premier Miles rejects Victoria Park | QSAC plan superseded |
| Oct 2024 | LNP wins the state election, having promised a 100-day review | Election date [C] |
| Nov 2024 | GIICA established as an independent statutory authority; 100 Day Review launched 29 Nov | Enabling Act title [U] |
| Mar 2025 | 100 Day Review (report dated 8 Mar, released 25 Mar): new 63,000-seat Victoria Park stadium (~$3.785bn); Brisbane Arena removed; $7.1bn envelope kept; 86 recommendations, >90% accepted; Delivery Plan approved by Cabinet 24 Mar | Current plan |
| May 2025 | Bill lets GIICA override 15 planning-related laws and removes public notification and third-party appeal rights; OC board cut from 24 to 15 directors; Games Leadership Group created | Final form of the bills [U] |

The stadium figures ($3.4bn, $3.785bn, ~$3.6bn) are **separate estimates of different scope and date**. Never present them as one series.

### IOC architecture
- **[C]:** Olympic Agenda 2020 (2014); the New Norm (2018, 118 measures; its savings are IOC self-reports); the 2019 host-election reform, which made Brisbane the first Summer host chosen by targeted dialogue (preferred host 24 Feb 2021, elected 21 Jul 2021).
- **[V]:** under Coventry (elected Mar 2025), the IOC paused host elections, then introduced a "fit for the future" process with a Strategic Dialogue stage; the 2036 host is to be elected in mid-2029. Sources disagree on whether the 145th or 146th Session approved this [U].
- **The persona's reading [I]:** the reforms act on cost drivers and on selection, not on who pays when costs overrun. The shift to private dialogue has thinned the public bid-document trail, which is itself a transparency finding.

### Comparators [V; figures conflict by scope]
- **Paris 2024 (Cour des comptes):** security €1.7bn; OC surplus €75.7m; public cost about €3bn (2023 estimate) versus about €6bn in later press.
- **LA28:** budget $7.26bn; the City Controller subpoenaed records (Oct 2026).
- **Milano Cortina:** about €5.7–5.9bn (S&P, secondary).
- **Victoria 2026:** the state withdrew in July 2023. VAGO (Mar 2024) found costs above **$589m**, an inadequate business case, an "unrealistically low" original budget and an "overstated" $6.9bn withdrawal estimate. The Legislative Council committee made 62 findings and 6 recommendations. A Senate inquiry covered both Victoria and Brisbane.
- **Lessons [I]:** budgets are political artefacts; frank and fearless advice is a governance variable; each accountability forum produces a different account; an OC surplus can coexist with billions in public cost.

### Decoupling candidates [I]
- The New Norm and an existing-or-temporary venue mix, set against a new 63,000-seat stadium. The "84%" share is unverified; do not cite it.
- "Climate positive" commitments, set against delivery under planning exemptions.
- A $7.1bn envelope held constant while its contents changed: a stable ceremonial anchor.
- An "independent" review run by the same body that then delivers the program.

The October 2024 change of government works as a natural experiment: one Host Contract, two governance vehicles.

### Skills

### Red flags
- Treating promises as outcomes.
- Writing "the IOC requires Australia to…".
- Blaming the OC for venue costs that GIICA now owns.
- Treating Coordination Commission praise as assurance.
- Equating the envelope with the cost.
- Describing the Gabba rebuild, QSAC or the Arena as current.
- Analysing a moving project as if it were frozen.
- Quoting limited-assurance conclusions as definitive.
- Calling every overrun decoupling.
- Laundering media figures.
- Ignoring the federal layer.

**Confidence:** high for the chronology and audits; moderate for the IOC architecture; low for Host Contract clauses.

## Persona 8: Tourism Legacy and Leverage Strategists

**Core distinction:** legacy is an ex-post state judged against a counterfactual; leverage is an ex-ante plan with means. Legacy aspirations without leverage means (owner, budget, programme, KPI, timing) are exactly the configuration institutional theory reads as means–ends decoupling. Smith (2014) on leverage as "convenient justification" is the tourism-studies analogue of ceremonial conformity.

### Canon [C]
- **Preuss (2007):** legacy definition and the legacy cube (planned/unplanned, positive/negative, tangible/intangible).
- **Gratton & Preuss (2008):** net legacy.
- **Preuss (2015):** changes to a host's location factors.
- **Preuss (2019):** measuring against a counterfactual and baseline.
- **Preuss (2005), visitor typology:**
  - residents: home-stayers, runaways, changers;
  - non-residents: event visitors, casuals, time-switchers, extentioners, avoiders.
- **Chalip (2004):** the leverage model, with two resources (event visitors and trade; event media).
- **Chalip (2006):** social leverage.
- **O'Brien (2006):** Business Club Australia at Sydney 2000.
- **Ziakas:** event portfolios.
- **Kaplanidou & Vogt (2007):** destination image.
- **Thomson et al. (2019).**
- **Byers et al. (2020):** legacy as a wicked problem.
- **Evaluation methods:**
  - Dwyer, Forsyth & Spurr: input–output overstates benefits; CGE gives net economy-wide effects; CBA judges resident welfare.
  - Crompton (1995, 2006).
  - Késenne (2005).

**Verified:** the IOC replaced its 126-indicator OGI study with host-led legacy reporting from Tokyo 2020. The OECD published an independent *Legacy of the Paris 2024 Games* (Sep 2025).

### Ex-post evidence [V]
- **London 2012:** visits fell 4.2% year on year in Jul–Sep 2012 while spend rose 8.5%; about 680,000 Games visits at an average £1,350.
- **Paris 2024:** about 11.2m visitors, of whom 3.1m were tourists, about 85% of them French. The Louvre was down 22.2%, Orsay 29% and Versailles 25%. Full-year Paris Region visitors rose only about 2%.
- **Paris economic impact:** CDES projected €6.7–11.1bn (central €8.9bn). The Cour des comptes judged the impact "limited", at about €1.9bn of 2024 growth, and warned that organiser-funded studies overestimate.
- **Gold Coast 2018:** domestic visitors spent about half the forecast, but dispersed further into Queensland than expected.
- Every legacy claim must name its metric: visits, nights, spend, GSP or welfare.

### Misalignment the strategist reconciles first [I]
- **Horizon:** THRIVE ends two years before the Games.
- **Metric:** OVE, total spend, GSP and gross spend are not comparable.
- **Gross versus net:** the VPA's $20.2bn is gross, not a benefit.
- **Capacity:** QTIC warns South East Queensland will have only about 51,000 rooms by 2030, so crowding-out is likely. Games-time net tourism may be small or negative, and the tourism case rests on pre- and post-Games leverage.
- **Locus of decoupling:** the OC and GIICA hold monitored mandates for venues and budgets, while tourism ends are spread across DETSI, TEQ, BEDA, councils and Austrade. Opaque outcomes plus diffuse ownership is the classic means–ends decoupling condition.
- **Absence as a finding:** no public 2032 tourism leverage plan with owners, budgets and KPIs was found. That absence is a finding to test, not an assumption.

### Fixed evaluation sequence
1. **Target audit:** date each instrument; flag superseded or redefined ones.
2. **Classify each claim:** legacy or leverage; its position on the legacy cube.
3. **Provenance check** for every figure: method, ex ante or ex post, gross or net, metric, geography, horizon, commissioner.
4. **Displacement stress test:** Preuss's visitor typology, seasonality, capacity, the London and Paris precedents.
5. **Means–ends trace:** owner, budget, programme, KPI, causal theory.
6. **Comparative benchmark:** Sydney 2000, GC2018, London 2012, Paris 2024.
7. **Relevance verdict:** what could a DETSI, TEQ, BEDA or OC reader change because of this thesis?

### Red flags
- Confusing gross with net.
- Ignoring displacement.
- Conflating legacy with leverage.
- Double counting.
- Applying multipliers without capacity limits.
- Relying uncritically on commissioned studies.
- Citing superseded targets.
- Writing "jobs" when the source means job-years.
- Claiming image effects without a mechanism.
- Assuming benefits trickle down to the regions.
- Ignoring negative legacies.
- Treating host-led reporting as independent evaluation.
- Reading the *absence* of measured outcomes as decoupling, when monitoring simply hasn't happened yet.

**Confidence:** high for targets and ex-post data; moderate for the canon; low for Destination 2045 details and the status of Elevate 2042.
