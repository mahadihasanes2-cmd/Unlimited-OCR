# Chapter blueprints

Use these when drafting or reviewing a chapter. Each blueprint gives the chapter's job, the personas who lead, what the chapter must contain, and the failure examiners notice first.

The university's thesis rules override this structure. The likely shape is a topic-based or traditional-complex hybrid, with analytic chapters organised by **mechanism**, not by document type.

## Abstract (about 300 words; check the university limit)
- **Leads:** Chair 1 and the Editing Supervisor.
- **Moves** (Kamler & Thomson's "tiny text"):
  1. **Locate:** mega-event governance; the gap between legacy promises and delivery.
  2. **Focus:** the research question.
  3. **Report:** case, corpus, cut-off date, analytic approach and main findings.
  4. **Argue:** the contribution to institutional theory, then to mega-event and tourism studies.
- **Must** use the same contribution sentence as the introduction and conclusion.
- **Failure:** a topic summary with no claim, or a contribution that differs from the one in the conclusion.

## 1. Introduction
- **Leads:** Chair 1 and the Senior Editor (diagnosis).
- **CARS moves** (Swales):
  1. Establish the territory: Olympic delivery and the IOC reforms.
  2. Establish the niche: means–ends decoupling has not been theorised for temporary, multi-level, pre-event delivery systems. Phrase this as a gap the candidate's systematic search has confirmed, not an assertion.
  3. Occupy the niche: research questions, case, approach, contribution, thesis map.
- **Must** state:
  - the scope condition (prospective or designed-in decoupling up to the cut-off date);
  - the working definitions of decoupling, legacy and leverage.
- **Failure:** "fills a gap" framing, or a contribution that is only an application of theory.

## 2. Literature review (one or two chapters)
- **Leads:** Institutional Sociologist, Senior Theorists, Governance Expert, Tourism Strategist.
- **Organise by argument, not by author.** Possible strands:
  1. neo-institutional theory and the lineages of decoupling;
  2. public administration repertoires and the governance of delivery bodies;
  3. mega-event governance and cost and risk;
  4. legacy versus leverage.
- **End each strand** with what remains unresolved, and why it matters for the research questions.
- **Use current editions** (see `verification-register.md`).
- **Failure:** "he said, she said" listing; theory that is described but never used later; superseded editions.

## 3. Theoretical or conceptual framework
- **Leads:** Senior Theorists, Institutional Sociologist, Methodologist.
- **Must** contain:
  - the combination protocol: assumptions, bridging constructs, mode and form of theory, boundary conditions;
  - the commitment-chain scheme (end, means, implementation, outcome provision, causal theory);
  - the mechanism hypotheses, stated as things to trace;
  - indicators that would count *against* decoupling.
- **Failure:** six or more theories with no integration; paradigms treated as eras.

## 4. Methodology
- **Leads:** Methodologist, Chair 2, Document Analyst, RTA Consultant, Data Consultant.
- **Sequence:**
  1. Paradigm statement (critical realism or constructionism, with reasons).
  2. Case design: subject and object, embedded, longitudinal, phases, cut-off date.
  3. Corpus design: six tiers, sampling frame, appraisal (Scott; AACODS), register and provenance.
  4. Analysis: the two-layer design, a framework "map" plus RTA "meaning", with the orientation declared on the four RTA continua.
  5. Quality: use the criteria-to-technique table; drop saturation, member checking and inter-rater reliability, and say why.
  6. Reflexivity and positionality.
  7. Ethics: exemption determination under the 2025 National Statement, RTI handling, Indigenous data.
  8. Data management and AI use.
- **Failure:** language from the wrong paradigm; COREQ; an unbounded case; ethics hand-waving.

## 5–7. Analytic or findings chapters (organised by mechanism)
- **Leads:** RTA Consultant, Document Analyst, Institutional Sociologist, Editing Supervisor.
- **Each chapter needs:**
  - one central claim;
  - themes named as claims, each with a central organising concept;
  - extracts from several genres and periods, each with its document ID;
  - ledger evidence: end, means, activity, outcome;
  - rival explanations considered;
  - disconfirming evidence;
  - every finding framed as "decoupling to date".
- **Toulmin-map** the chapter's 3–5 claims.
- **The panel coaches; the candidate interprets.** Do not generate themes from the candidate's data.
- **Failure:** chronology instead of analysis; topic summaries; talk read as practice; outcome claims before 2032.

## 8. Discussion
- **Leads:** Senior Theorists, Chair 1, Committee Reviewer.
- **Speak back to each parent theory**, two-way: what changes in decoupling theory, and what changes in public-administration and mega-event theory.
- **Specify:**
  - the mechanism (entities, activities, conditions);
  - the boundary conditions;
  - portability to other megaprojects.
- **Pass the seven-item contribution test** (`cat2-theory-and-domain.md`).
- **Include practical implications** for DETSI, TEQ, GIICA, the OC and the IOC. These are secondary to the scientific contribution.
- **Failure:** restating findings; claiming a new paradigm.

## 9. Conclusion
- **Leads:** Chair 1 and the Senior Editor.
- **Bunton's field-oriented moves:**
  1. restate the purpose;
  2. consolidate the findings;
  3. state the contribution, using the same sentence as the abstract;
  4. state the limitations (documents only, prospective scope, cut-off date);
  5. future research (post-2032 recoupling, comparative cases).
- **No new material.**
- **Failure:** a contribution statement that has drifted from the one in the introduction.

## Front and back matter
- **Writing Specialist and Data Consultant:**
  - an acknowledgment of editing, if any, using IPEd wording;
  - an AI-use declaration that truthfully describes the panel's role;
  - a document-corpus appendix that matches the reference list;
  - a glossary of defined terms.
