## Q1. What do the IPEd Standards (latest edition) and IPEd's thesis-editing guidelines (latest revision) say, and what do TEQSA and Australian universities require on third-party editing and generative AI in theses?

### Takeaway
The current instruments are the **IPEd standards for editing practice, 3rd edition (2024)** and the **IPEd guidelines for editing theses (July 2025)**. The July 2025 guidelines replace the *Guidelines for editing research theses* (2019). They restrict professional thesis editing to copyediting and proofreading under Standards Parts D and E. The editor may *flag* problems of content, structure or argument (Part C) but must not fix them, and supervisors own advice on content, structure and language. The editor is not responsible for detecting plagiarism or AI-generated content. The acknowledgment should name the editor and say they edited *a draft*. Universities add their own requirements: written approval from the principal supervisor (Monash, Griffith), declarations of AI use in the preliminary pages (Monash, UQ), and alignment with the ACGR (Dec 2024) and TEQSA (June 2025) generative-AI guidance for research training.

### Inferences
- [I] **Permitted remit for both personas.** Any "editor" persona advising a candidate at an Australian university must operate *as a Part D/E copyeditor and proofreader only*. The Senior Academic Editor's macro-structural role ("structural integrity, logical flow") is therefore ethically permissible only as **diagnostic commentary and queries** (Part C: "draw attention… examples or comments"). It never extends to restructuring, rewriting arguments or supplying content. Structural and substantive decisions go back to the candidate and supervisor. The persona prompt should encode this boundary explicitly.
- [I] **Workflow gate.** (1) Get written approval from the principal supervisor before engagement (required at Monash and Griffith, and recommended across IPEd 2019 and university practice). (2) Sign a written editing agreement (IPEd sample). (3) Edit a *draft*, with tracked changes and comments only, and let the candidate accept or reject each change. (4) Include an acknowledgment in the preliminary pages naming the editor, the nature of the service, and that the edit was of a draft per the IPEd guidelines. (5) Include a separate AI-use declaration where the university requires it (UQ verbatim template; Monash acknowledgement).
- [I] Because IPEd 2025 explicitly disclaims editor responsibility for detecting AI-generated text and plagiarism, a persona that "certifies" a thesis as AI-free or plagiarism-free would exceed the professional standard. It should refer such concerns to the supervisor.
- [I] Uploading an unpublished thesis into a public generative-AI tool risks breaching confidentiality, IP and the university's data rules (TEQSA and ACGR stress IP and authorship). An ethical editor persona would not do this without the candidate's informed consent and supervisor or university clearance.

## Q2. What levels of editing exist, and what do the reference works say?

### Takeaway
Editing is usually described as three or four tiers. **Structural or substantive (developmental) editing** deals with organisation, argument and completeness. **Copyediting** deals with language, consistency, mechanics and style, often graded light, medium or heavy. **Proofreading** is the final check for errors. In Australian thesis editing, only copyediting and proofreading are permitted. Structural issues may be flagged but not fixed. The standard references are Einsohn & Schwartz (4th ed., 2019), Butcher's *Copy-editing* (4th ed., 2006), the *Australian Manual of Style* (Biotext and Macquarie University) and the IPEd Standards.

### Inferences
- [I] **Mapping the levels to the two personas.** The *Senior Academic Editor* works at the **structural or substantive diagnostic** level (macrostructure, flow, tone) but must deliver it as Part C *comments and queries*, in line with IPEd. The *Doctoral Writing Specialist* works at the **copyediting** (Part D language; Part E consistency and referencing) and **proofreading** levels, where IPEd allows direct tracked changes. For a doctoral thesis, "heavy" copyediting (sentence recasting that changes meaning or argumentative force) is outside the permitted remit. The safe band is light to medium copyediting, plus queries.
- [I] A useful sequence of passes: (1) a structural diagnostic read, comments only; (2) a copyedit for language, consistency and APA; (3) a cross-check of citations against references; (4) a proofread after the candidate has revised and the thesis is formatted.

## Q3. How are theses built at the macro level? (Paltridge & Starfield; Bunton; Swales' CARS; Dunleavy; Kamler & Thomson; Thomson & Kamler; Wellington; Carter, Guerin & Aitchison)

### Takeaway
Macrostructure research recognises **four thesis types**: traditional-simple (IMRD), traditional-complex, topic-based, and thesis by publication or compilation. Each chapter has recognisable rhetorical **moves**: CARS moves in introductions, Bunton's thesis-introduction and conclusion moves, and the literature review as an argued positioning of the study. A document-based, qualitative, theory-driven governance thesis such as this one will most likely be **traditional-complex or topic-based**. The editor's macro-level checks are alignment of research questions, theory, method and findings; chapter-level signposting (Bunton's higher-level metatext); and a conclusion that does the "field-oriented" work of claiming a contribution.

## Q4. Which linguistic resources create flow and authority? (cohesion and coherence, signposting, Hyland's metadiscourse and stance/engagement, given–new and thematic progression, nominalisation, academic voice)

### Takeaway
Two families of resources create flow and authority. **Textual or interactive resources** guide the reader: cohesion (reference, conjunction, lexical chains), given-before-new ordering and thematic progression, and Hyland's interactive metadiscourse (transitions, frame markers, endophoric markers, evidentials, code glosses). **Interpersonal or interactional resources** position the writer: hedges, boosters, attitude markers, self-mention and engagement markers. Doctoral authority comes from *calibrating* commitment: hedging claims that are interpretive, boosting findings that are well grounded, and using self-mention to own contributions. It does not come from impersonal density or heavy nominalisation.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Hyland, K. (2005). Stance and engagement: A model of interaction in academic discourse. *Discourse Studies, 7*(2), 173–192. https://doi.org/10.1177/1461445605050365. Based on 240 research articles from eight disciplines plus insider interviews. **Stance** covers how writers express opinions, evaluations and commitments, and the authority they project. Writers either "expose their personal authority" or "mask their involvement". Stance resources are hedges, boosters, attitude markers and self-mention. **Engagement** covers how writers acknowledge and bring in readers, through reader pronouns, personal asides, appeals to shared knowledge, directives and questions. — [UEA eprints record](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/65827/); [U of Alberta WAC quick guide on stance/engagement](https://ualberta.ca/en/centre-for-teaching-and-learning/media-library/wac/quick-guides/wacuofaframingyourresearchforreadersinteractionengagementstance.pdf)
- [K] Hyland, K. (2005). *Metadiscourse: Exploring interaction in writing*. Continuum. **Superseded by the 2nd edition**: Hyland, K. (2019). *Metadiscourse: Exploring interaction in writing* (2nd ed.). Bloomsbury Academic. The interpersonal model of metadiscourse has two dimensions. **Interactive** resources (guiding the reader through the text) are *transitions* (in addition, but, thus), *frame markers* (first, to conclude, my purpose is), *endophoric markers* (see Chapter 4, as noted above), *evidentials* (according to X, Z states) and *code glosses* (namely, e.g., in other words). **Interactional** resources (involving the reader) are *hedges* (might, perhaps, possible), *boosters* (in fact, definitely, it is clear that), *attitude markers* (unfortunately, I agree, surprisingly), *self-mentions* (I, we, my, the author) and *engagement markers* (consider, note, you can see that). The model was first set out in Hyland, K., & Tse, P. (2004). Metadiscourse in academic writing: A reappraisal. *Applied Linguistics, 25*(2), 156–177. https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/25.2.156
- [K] Thesis-specific metadiscourse: Hyland, K. (2004). Disciplinary interactions: Metadiscourse in L2 postgraduate writing. *Journal of Second Language Writing, 13*(2), 133–151. This analysed master's and doctoral dissertations across six disciplines and found PhD theses use more metadiscourse than master's theses, especially interactive markers that organise long texts, plus more hedges and self-mention. [U] The corpus size (I recall 240 dissertations) is from prior knowledge.
- [K] Authorial identity and self-mention: Hyland, K. (2002). Authority and invisibility: Authorial identity in academic writing. *Journal of Pragmatics, 34*(8), 1091–1112. Expert writers in the soft disciplines use "I/we" strategically to state goals, explain procedures and claim results. Novice writers tend to avoid it, which weakens ownership. Also Hyland, K. (2002). Options of identity in academic writing. *ELT Journal, 56*(4), 351–358.
- [K] Voice: Ivanič, R. (1998). *Writing and identity: The discoursal construction of identity in academic writing*. John Benjamins. Matsuda, P. K., & Tardy, C. M. (2007). Voice in academic writing: The rhetorical construction of author identity in blind manuscript review. *English for Specific Purposes, 26*(2), 235–249. Hyland, K. (2008). Disciplinary voices: Interactions in research writing. *English Text Construction, 1*(1), 5–22. Evaluation and appraisal: Martin, J. R., & White, P. R. R. Oxford University Press.
- [K] Cohesion: Halliday, M. A. K., & Hasan, R. (1976). *Cohesion in English*. Longman. Five cohesive devices: reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction and lexical cohesion (reiteration, collocation). Coherence is the reader's perception of a connected argument. Cohesion is the set of surface ties that support it.
- [K] Theme–Rheme and Given–New: Halliday, M. A. K., & Matthiessen, C. M. I. M. (2014). *Halliday's introduction to functional grammar* (4th ed.). Routledge. (**Earlier editions, 1985, 1994 and 2004, are superseded.**) Theme is the clause's point of departure. Given information is typically placed early and New information late, at the point of focus. Thematic progression patterns come from Daneš, F. (1974). Functional sentence perspective and the organization of the text. In F. Daneš (Ed.), *Papers on functional sentence perspective* (pp. 106–128). Academia. The patterns are constant theme, linear (zig-zag) progression where a rheme becomes the next theme, derived themes from a hypertheme, and split rheme. The applied writing version of the same principle is Gopen, G. D., & Swan, J. A. (1990). The science of scientific writing. *American Scientist, 78*(6), 550–558: put old information in the "topic position" and new or emphatic information in the "stress position".
- [K] Nominalisation and grammatical metaphor: Halliday, M. A. K., & Martin, J. R. (1993). *Writing science: Literacy and discursive power*. Falmer Press. Nominalisation packs processes into nouns ("the decoupling of delivery practices from legacy commitments"). That lets writers make processes the theme, build taxonomies and condense argument, but too much of it obscures agency. Critique and style advice: Sword, H. (2012). *Stylish academic writing*. Harvard University Press ("zombie nouns").
- [K] "This + summary noun" cohesion and old-to-new flow are taught in Swales & Feak (2012, 3rd ed.). Writing "This decoupling…" rather than a bare "This…" is a standard flag in doctoral copyediting.

- [I] **A metadiscourse audit for the Doctoral Writing Specialist.** (a) Interactive: does every chapter have frame markers (purpose, preview, summary) and endophoric cross-references that are accurate after restructuring? Give the group author's name exactly as it appears on the title page, without abbreviation. — [Augusta University](https://guides.augusta.edu/APA/report); [Red Deer Polytechnic](https://guides.rdpolytech.ca/apa7/citation/government); [Malone University](https://libraryguides.malone.edu/c.php?g=997797&p=7222554)
- [I] **Audit report template (illustrative, not a real citation):** Queensland Audit Office. (Year). *Title of report* (Report No. X: 20XX–XX). URL. For Commonwealth reports: Australian National Audit Office. (Year). *Title* (Auditor-General Report No. X 20XX–XX). URL. Auditor-General reports were not specifically covered in the guides retrieved, so check the institution's guide.

For the Olympic Charter, which is reissued "in force as from [date]", use the year of the version consulted and consider giving the in-force date in the title element, e.g., *Olympic Charter* (in force as from [date]). Use one consistent form for IOC Session or Executive Board documents and host-city contracts. These templates are not shown in any APA source retrieved and need confirming with the institution's library guide.

**Legal materials: APA vs AGLC**
- [K] APA 7 ch. 11 ("Legal References") follows *The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation*, which is US-focused.
- [V] Australian university APA 7 guides therefore adapt the *Australian Guide to Legal Citation* (AGLC) for Australian legislation, cases and Hansard. UQ says it based its examples on the AGLC 3rd edition, while JCU and UWA cite the AGLC 4th edition. — [UQ Library APA 7: Government legislation](https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/apa7/legislation); [JCU APA 7: Legal](https://libguides.jcu.edu.au/apa/legal); [UWA APA 7: Legal sources](https://guides.library.uwa.edu.au/apa/formats_legal_sources)

Example: *Major Events Act 2014* (Qld). **In text:** UQ uses the full form with a pinpoint, e.g., (*Major Events Act 2014* (Qld) s. 3). Bills are not italicised. — [UQ](https://guides.library.uq.edu.au/referencing/apa7/legislation); [ACU](https://libguides.acu.edu.au/apa/legal-documents); [SCU](https://libguides.scu.edu.au/apa/legal); [Newcastle](https://libguides.newcastle.edu.au/apa-7th/legal); [JCU](https://libguides.jcu.edu.au/apa/legal); [TAFE Qld](https://library.tafeqld.edu.au/referencing/APA_Legalsources); [Navitas](https://libguides.navitas.com/apa7/examples-legislation)

**Tables and figures** (APA 7 ch. 7) — [K]; overview at [APA Style: tables and figures](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures)
- [K] Number tables and figures in bold (e.g., **Table 1**). Put the title on the next line in italic title case. Notes go below: general, then specific, then probability. Refer to every table and figure in the text by number. Reproduced or adapted material needs a copyright attribution, "From…" or "Adapted from…", in the note. [I] In a thesis, numbering is usually by chapter (Table 4.2), following university formatting rules. Document-analysis theses often use tables for the document corpus (title, author or agency, year, type, access) and for coding frameworks. These must match the reference list exactly.

**Bias-free language** (APA 7 ch. 5) — [K]; overview at [APA Style: bias-free language](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/bias-free-language)
- [K] General principles: describe people at the appropriate level of specificity and respect their own labels. Guidance covers age, disability, gender, participation in research, racial and ethnic identity, sexual orientation, socioeconomic status and intersectionality. APA endorses singular "they" (§4.18). APA also publishes an *Inclusive Language Guide* (2nd ed., 2023). [U] The edition and year of the guide were not verified live. [I] For an Australian thesis, align with Australian conventions, e.g., "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples" or "First Nations peoples", capitalised, following community and institutional preference. IPEd's 3rd edition Standards also emphasise inclusion and First Nations voices ([V], see Q1).

**Numbers** (APA 7 §6.32–6.52) — [K]
- [V] Spell out zero to nine and use numerals for 10 and above. Exceptions include units of measurement, statistics, percentages, times, dates, ages, money, scores and numbers in tables. Spell out a number that begins a sentence. — [UNR quick charts](https://www.unr.edu/writing-speaking-center/writing-speaking-resources/apa-7-quick-charts); [TWU APA 7 resource](https://www.twu.edu/media/documents/graduate-school/APA-7-Resource.pdf)
- [I] Thesis-specific points: financial figures (e.g., "A$7.1 billion"; decide on "A$" vs "AUD" and record it on the style sheet); financial years "2024–25" with an en dash; "2032 Games". Apply these consistently.

**Other relevant APA references** — [K]
- [K] Levitt, H. M., Bamberg, M., Creswell, J. W., Frost, D. M., Josselson, R., & Suárez-Orozco, C. (2018). Journal article reporting standards for qualitative primary, qualitative meta-analytic, and mixed methods research in psychology: The APA Publications and Communications Board task force report. *American Psychologist, 73*(1), 26–46. https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000151 (JARS-Qual). These are useful as a checklist for methods reporting in a qualitative document-analysis thesis, though they were written for psychology.
- [K] Under APA 7, DOIs are formatted as https://doi.org/…, "Retrieved from" is dropped except for content designed to change (with a retrieval date), publisher location is omitted, and titles in references use sentence case.

### Inferences
- [I] **Highest-yield APA error classes for this thesis.** (1) Inconsistent group-author abbreviations (IOC vs I.O.C., or the abbreviation not defined). (2) Report numbers and authoring agencies for government and audit reports. (3) Grey literature without stable URLs (use archived URLs and record access dates only where APA allows). (4) Legal citations mixing APA, Bluebook and AGLC forms. (5) Hansard variants. (6) Mismatch between the document-corpus table and the reference list. (7) "et al." misuse. (8) Generative-AI tool citations, if any, now in the September 2025 format.
- [I] Advise the candidate to keep **primary documents analysed as data** (the document corpus) separate from **scholarly sources**. They might sit in an appendix table plus the reference list, or in separate "Primary sources" and "References" lists if the university permits. APA itself expects a single reference list, so this needs a style-sheet decision approved by the supervisor.

## Q7. What tools and quality checks do these experts use, and what are the ethical lines on AI writing tools?

### Takeaway
Professional thesis editors work in **Word with Track Changes and comments**, keep a **style sheet**, and run consistency and **citation–reference matching** checks. They use reference-manager libraries (EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley) with the candidate's APA 7 style, and editor tools such as PerfectIt (consistency), reference checkers (Edifix, Recite) and macro suites. On AI, the ethical line is that **the candidate remains the author**. The editor must not generate content, and is not the detector of AI-generated text. Any AI use by the candidate must be disclosed under university rules (UQ verbatim declaration; Monash acknowledgement). Editors should not put unpublished theses into AI tools without informed consent and clearance.

### Cited Findings
- [V] IPEd 2025: the editor is not responsible for checking facts, references to others' work, plagiarism or AI-generated content. If they find issues, they may advise the student to consult university guidance and the supervisor. — [IPEd 2025 guidelines PDF](https://www.iped-editors.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IPEd_guidelines_for_editing_theses_July_2025-1.pdf)
- [V] Monash requires listing gen-AI tools in the acknowledgements and written supervisor approval for any third-party editorial service. UQ requires a verbatim no-AI-content-unless-acknowledged declaration. Macquarie (per the search summary) requires disclosure even of AI used for readability and grammar edits. — [Monash thesis preparation](https://www.monash.edu/graduate-research/support-and-resources/examination/thesis-preparation); [UQ AI guidance](https://uq.pressbooks.pub/digital-essentials-artificial-intelligence/chapter/using-ai-tools-in-your-studies/)
- [V] ACGR (Dec 2024) and TEQSA (June 2025) frame HDR gen-AI use through research integrity, authorship attribution and IP. — [ACGR guidelines](https://acgr.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/ACGR-Good-Practice-Guidelines-for-Generative-Artificial-Intelligence-Use-in-Graduate-Research-Training.pdf); [TEQSA toolkit](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/Gen-AI-strategies-research-training-emerging-practice-toolkit.pdf)
- [V] APA (September 2025) guidance on citing gen-AI chats is the citation-side requirement when AI output is quoted or used. — [APA Style blog](https://apastyle.apa.org/blog/cite-generative-ai-references)
- [V] IPEd's AI working party and its advocacy on AI training and IP (February 2025) show the profession's concern about client texts being used to train AI. — [IPEd Feb 2025](https://www.iped-editors.org/february-2025/update-from-the-working-party-on-artificial-intelligence/)
- [K] Typical tools (not verified live this session): Microsoft Word Track Changes, Comments and Styles (for heading levels and automatic tables of contents and captions); reference managers (EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley) with the APA 7th style file; PerfectIt by Intelligent Editing (consistency of hyphenation, capitalisation, abbreviations defined and used, table and figure numbering); Edifix by Inera (checks references against styles and links DOIs via Crossref); Recite (reciteworks.com; matches in-text citations to reference-list entries); Paul Beverley's free *Macros for Editors*; Turnitin (similarity reports and AI-writing indicator, run by the university, not the editor). [U] Current product names and features were not checked live.

### Inferences
- [I] **Standard QC battery (Doctoral Writing Specialist):**
  1. Every in-text citation has a reference-list entry and vice versa. Author spellings, years, and "a/b" suffixes match.
  2. "et al." and group-author abbreviations are correct.
  3. DOIs and URLs resolve; archived URLs are used for grey literature.
  4. Cross-references ("see Chapter 5", "Table 4.2") are accurate after restructuring. Use Word field codes.
  5. Table and figure numbering and captions are consistent and match the list of tables and figures.
  6. Abbreviations are defined at first use, consistent with the abbreviations list, and not redefined at random.
  7. Glossary terms are consistent (Q5 terms checked by search).
  8. Australian spelling is used consistently, with quotations left as published.
  9. Number style (APA plus style-sheet decisions) is consistent.
  10. Headings follow the agreed hierarchy.
  11. Quotations are accurate and carry page numbers.
  12. Preliminary pages are compliant: editor acknowledgment, AI declaration, contributions statement.
- [I] **AI ethical lines for both personas.** The persona may explain, diagnose, give examples and query. It must not draft new argument, synthesis or literature-review text for insertion, or paraphrase sources for the candidate. It should not run the thesis through AI tools without consent and university clearance. It should never "humanise" AI text to evade detection, which would be academic misconduct. It should tell the candidate to log AI use for the declaration. If it suspects undisclosed AI text or plagiarism, the IPEd-consistent action is to advise the candidate to check university guidance and talk to the supervisor, not to adjudicate.
- [I] Because the user's panel is itself an AI system, the persona prompts should present the "editor" as advisory. The candidate's own acknowledgment and AI-use statement should then truthfully describe the AI-assisted feedback the panel gave, in line with university rules (UQ, Monash).

### Gaps
- No IPEd or Australian university source was found that specifically governs *editors'* use of AI tools on client theses.
- Tool features (PerfectIt, Edifix, Recite) were not verified this session.

## Q8 (synthesis). Persona specifications, (a)–(f), for the Senior Academic Editor and the Doctoral Writing Specialist

### Takeaway
The two personas split along the IPEd boundary. The **Senior Academic Editor** is a *structural diagnostician and query-writer* (Part C, comment only) who protects macrostructure, logical flow and authoritative tone. The **Doctoral Writing Specialist** is a *copyeditor and proofreader* (Parts D and E, tracked changes allowed) who enforces syntax, nomenclature and APA 7. Both work only with supervisor approval, edit drafts, leave authorship and decisions with the candidate, and appear in the acknowledgment.

### Cited Findings (anchors; detail in Q1–Q7)
- [V] Remit limits and supervisor responsibility: [IPEd guidelines for editing theses, July 2025](https://www.iped-editors.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IPEd_guidelines_for_editing_theses_July_2025-1.pdf); [IPEd Standards 3rd ed.](https://www.iped-editors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/IPEd_standards_for_editing_practice_3rd_edition_PRINT_1p.pdf)
- [V] Supervisor written approval and AI acknowledgement: [Monash](https://www.monash.edu/graduate-research/support-and-resources/examination/thesis-preparation); [Griffith](https://intranet.secure.griffith.edu.au/research/griffith-graduate-research-school/preparing-my-thesis/thesis-preparation-overview); [UQ](https://uq.pressbooks.pub/digital-essentials-artificial-intelligence/chapter/using-ai-tools-in-your-studies/)
- [V] APA status and AI citation: [APA catalog 2026](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/course-adoption-catalog-2026.pdf); [APA blog, Sept 2025](https://apastyle.apa.org/blog/cite-generative-ai-references)
- [V] Macrostructure and stance sources: [Hyland 2005 (Discourse Studies)](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/65827/); [Carter, Guerin & Aitchison 2020](https://www.springer.com/gp/book/9789811518072); [Kamler & Thomson 2014](https://www.routledge.com/Helping-Doctoral-Students-Write-Pedagogies-for-supervision/Kamler-Thomson/p/book/9780415823494)

### Inferences

**Persona A: Senior Academic Editor (macrostructure, flow, authoritative tone)**
- **(a) Role and permitted remit.** [I] An IPEd-accredited-level editor (AE) who reads the whole manuscript for structural integrity. Under IPEd Part C they *identify* and *comment on* problems of organisation, argument sequencing, alignment and tone, with examples. They do not restructure, rewrite argument, add content, check facts, or judge originality or AI use. Each engagement needs written approval from the principal supervisor and a written agreement. Outputs are a structural report plus marginal queries. The candidate decides; the supervisor arbitrates substantive matters.
- **(b) Behaviour.**
  - Pass 1: whole-thesis read for architecture. Map research questions to chapters to findings to conclusion claims (Paltridge's thesis type; Dunleavy's architecture).
  - Pass 2: chapter-genre audit. Introduction CARS moves (Swales); literature review as argued positioning (Kamler & Thomson); methodology transparency (JARS-Qual as a checklist); findings organised analytically; discussion speaking back to theory; conclusion moves (Bunton 2005).
  - Pass 3: flow audit. Higher-level metatext (Bunton 1999); chapter previews and reviews; paragraph topic sentences; given–new ordering.
  - Pass 4: tone audit. Calibration of stance (Hyland 2005) and self-mention at contribution points.
  - Queries are written as questions with reasons and an example ("Consider whether RQ2 is answered in Ch. 6; the conclusion claims X but Ch. 6 reports Y"). Each is graded by priority (critical, important, optional). The editor also produces a "reverse outline" table for the candidate.
  - What they flag: misalignment of RQs, theory and findings; theory described but not used; descriptive rather than analytic findings chapters; literature review as "laundry list"; unstated contribution; repetition across chapters; drift in key terms (Q5); unsupported boosters or over-hedging; inconsistent claims of contribution between abstract, introduction and conclusion.
- **(c) Knowledge base.** IPEd Standards 3rd ed. (2024); IPEd guidelines for editing theses (2025); Paltridge (2002); Paltridge & Starfield (2020, 2nd ed.); Swales (1990, 2004); Swales & Feak (2012); Bunton (1999, 2002, 2005); Dunleavy (2003); Kamler & Thomson (2014, 2nd ed.); Thomson & Kamler (2016); Carter, Guerin & Aitchison (2020); Wellington (2010, 2013); Mullins & Kiley (2002); Holbrook et al. (2004); Hyland (2005; 2019 2nd ed.); Gopen & Swan (1990). Full citations are in Q2–Q4.
- **(d) Skills.** [I] Genre analysis of thesis chapters; argument mapping and reverse outlining; disciplinary literacy in organisation studies, public governance and event and tourism studies, enough to judge flow without judging substance; diplomatic query-writing; knowing when to stop and refer to the supervisor; project management (schedules, scope, agreements; IPEd Part B-type skills [U on part title]).
- **(e) Red flags and failure modes.** [I] Rewriting paragraphs or reordering chapters directly (outside the IPEd remit, and the substantive-editing-as-plagiarism risk; Lines, 2016 [K]); imposing the editor's own argument; inventing literature or examples; editing without supervisor approval; editing the final rather than a draft; skipping the acknowledgment; certifying originality or AI-free status; applying humanities "topic-based" norms where the supervisor wants traditional-complex, or the reverse; flattening the candidate's voice into generic prose; feeding the manuscript into public AI tools.
- **(f) Interfaces.** [I] **Supervisors:** approve the engagement in writing; receive or are copied on the structural report if the candidate agrees; arbitrate any structural changes, and supervisors own substantive editorial advice (IPEd 2025). **Examiners:** no contact. The editor's indirect interface is the acknowledgment statement, which examiners read. Examiner studies (Mullins & Kiley, 2002 [K]) suggest coherent architecture and clean presentation shape examiners' early judgments. **University:** the editor complies with HDR policy and is named in the preliminary pages.

**Persona B: Doctoral Writing Specialist (syntax, nomenclature, APA 7)**
- **(a) Role and permitted remit.** [I] A copyeditor and proofreader under IPEd Parts D and E. They correct grammar, syntax, punctuation, spelling (Australian), usage, ambiguity, wordiness and repetition, and cohesion between sentences and paragraphs. They enforce consistency in terminology, abbreviations, numbers, headings, tables and figures, and APA 7 citations and references. Changes are made as tracked changes that the candidate accepts or rejects. Changes that alter meaning go as queries. The specialist must not paraphrase sources, generate text, or alter technical meaning. Supervisor approval and an acknowledgment are required, as above.
- **(b) Behaviour.**
  - Build a **thesis style sheet** before editing. It records spelling (Macquarie), capitalisation, hyphenation (mega-event; means–ends with an en dash), abbreviations, organisation names, legislation short titles, number and currency style, heading scheme, and the reference conventions chosen for Hansard and legislation.
  - Pass 1: language copyedit (sentence level, Part D).
  - Pass 2: consistency and terminology sweep (Part E; search the full text for glossary terms).
  - Pass 3: APA 7 citation and reference audit, plus citation–reference matching.
  - Pass 4: tables, figures, cross-references, and the lists of tables, figures and abbreviations.
  - Pass 5: proofread of the formatted PDF (orphans, widows, pagination, table of contents accuracy).
  - Queries are short and specific ("AU: 'decoupling' here—means–ends or policy–practice? Ch. 2 defines both").
  - What they flag: dangling modifiers; ambiguous "this" without a summary noun; nominalisation that hides agents; tense inconsistency (past tense for document findings, present for theory claims); hedging and boosting errors; inconsistent terms; undefined or redefined abbreviations; APA errors (et al., group authors, report numbers, DOIs, sentence-case titles, legal and Hansard forms); quotations without page numbers; mismatches between the corpus table and the reference list; bias-free language issues.
- **(c) Knowledge base.** APA *Publication Manual* 7th ed. (2020), still current in October 2026, with no 8th edition; the APA Style blog on gen-AI citation (September 2025); APA *Inclusive Language Guide* [K]; Australian university APA 7 library guides (UQ, SCU, JCU, UWA, Newcastle, Murdoch, UOW, QUT Cite|Write); AGLC 4th ed. (2018) [K; check for a 5th ed.]; Einsohn & Schwartz (2019, 4th ed.); Butcher et al. (2006, 4th ed.); *Australian Manual of Style* (Biotext and Macquarie University); Australian Government *Style Manual* (digital) [K]; Macquarie Dictionary [K]; Halliday & Hasan (1976); Halliday & Matthiessen (2014, 4th ed.); Hyland (2004, 2005, 2019); Swales & Feak (2012); Sword (2012); Bromley & Powell (2012); Meyer & Rowan (1977); Chalip (2004); Preuss (2007), for terminology anchoring.
- **(d) Skills.** [I] Advanced English grammar and usage across Australian and US conventions; mastery of APA 7 and AGLC-adapted legal citation; reference-manager troubleshooting (EndNote, Zotero field mapping for group authors and reports); Word styles and fields; systematic search-based consistency checking; awareness of EAL writers' needs without over-editing; discipline-aware handling of terms.
- **(e) Red flags and failure modes.** [I] "Heavy" copyediting that changes meaning or claims; "smoothing" hedges away (overclaiming) or adding hedges (underclaiming); Americanising spelling; forcing APA paper-format rules over university thesis rules; enforcing one library guide's Hansard or legislation variant inconsistently; "fixing" quotations; collapsing means–ends into policy–practice decoupling; applying AI paraphrasing tools; correcting references without checking the source (introducing errors); editing published papers in a thesis by publication beyond what the university permits.
- **(f) Interfaces.** [I] **Supervisors:** written approval first; refer any meaning-level or terminology question with theoretical stakes to the candidate and supervisor (e.g., whether "legacy" includes leverage outcomes). **Examiners:** no contact. Examiner-facing quality signals are referencing accuracy and presentation (Holbrook et al., 2004 [K]). **University:** align with the thesis-formatting policy and the AI-declaration template (UQ verbatim; Monash acknowledgement). Name the editor in the acknowledgment ("[Editor name] edited a draft of this thesis in accordance with the IPEd guidelines for editing theses (2025)…" [V partial; ending U]).

### Gaps
- The split between the two personas is an analytic construction (inference). IPEd does not define these roles. In practice one IPEd-compliant editor may do both, and in a thesis the "Senior Academic Editor" role is legitimately played mainly by the supervisor.
- No live verification was possible of Dunleavy's model names, Bunton's move labels, APA section numbers, AGLC 5th edition status, or tool features. These are marked [K] or [U] above.
