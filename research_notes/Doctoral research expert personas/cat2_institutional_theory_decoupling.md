## Q1. What is the genealogy of neo-institutional theory?

### Takeaway
Neo-institutionalism grew out of 1977's two founding papers: Meyer & Rowan on formal structure as myth and ceremony (with decoupling), and Zucker on cognitive taken-for-grantedness. Then came isomorphism (DiMaggio & Powell 1983) and diffusion (Tolbert & Zucker 1983), and Scott's synthesis into three pillars. After that it widened into legitimacy (Suchman 1995), institutional logics (Thornton, Ocasio & Lounsbury 2012), institutional work (Lawrence & Suddaby 2006), institutional complexity (Greenwood et al. 2011) and micro-foundations (Powell & Colyvas 2008; Powell & Rerup 2017). Each step relaxed the original "iron cage" determinism and added agency, plurality and micro-processes. By 2019 critics warned this expansion had produced a "mid-life crisis" of over-reach and tautology.

## Q2. How is decoupling defined and typed? Policy–practice versus means–ends, and the main contributors and debates (2008–2026)

### Takeaway
The field now distinguishes two main forms:
- **Policy–practice decoupling**: a formal policy is adopted but not implemented, or is routinely violated.
- **Means–ends decoupling**: policies *are* implemented, but the activities are only weakly or opaquely linked to the organisation's core goals or to the policy's own intended outcomes.

Bromley & Powell (2012) argued that means–ends decoupling is becoming the dominant form in a rationalised, evaluation-saturated world. Wijen (2014) theorised its link to field opacity, and to a trade-off in which enforcing compliance can *increase* the gap between means and ends. A lively debate followed: Haack & Schoeneborn's (2015) "is decoupling becoming decoupled from institutional theory?" and Wijen's (2015) rejoinder that "coupling, not decoupling" should be the mantra. Political (Kern et al. 2018), regulatory (de Bree & Stoopendaal 2020) and dynamic (Haack et al. 2021) extensions came next, then a wave of empirical means–ends studies in 2024–2025.

### Inferences
- [I] **Definition the persona would sign off (synthesis).**
  - *Policy–practice decoupling* is a gap between formally adopted policies or structures and what the organisation actually does.
  - *Means–ends decoupling* is a gap between what the organisation does (often diligently, and in compliance) and the ends those activities are supposed to serve. The gap persists because the link between means and ends is opaque, unmeasured or measured by proxies, and because legitimacy flows from visible conformity of the means rather than from achieving the ends.
  - Both are *relational and institutional*. They need (i) an institutionalised expectation, (ii) an audience that grants legitimacy, and (iii) a demonstrable separation.
- [I] A third element is often implicit and worth making explicit for mega-events: **talk–talk inconsistency across genres or audiences.** For example, the IOC's promotional communication diverges from the role sustainability plays in its formal documents (see Q4). Brunsson's talk/decision/action triad covers this better than the binary Bromley–Powell typology does.
- [I] Wijen's trade-off has a direct analogue in Olympic governance. Detailed host contracts, Agenda 2020 templates, ISO-style sustainability management systems and IOC reporting formats secure *compliance* (less policy–practice decoupling). But because tourism, legacy and sustainability ends are causally complex and slow to emerge, they may *widen* means–ends decoupling. This is a testable framing for the thesis.

## Q3. What antecedents, mechanisms and consequences of means–ends decoupling are proposed, and how does recoupling happen?

### Takeaway
The proposed **antecedents** are:
- the proliferation of rationalised goals and myths (world society, hyper-organisation);
- mandates for evaluation, audit and measurement;
- field opacity: causal complexity, practice diversity and behavioural invisibility;
- institutional complexity and power struggles;
- rigid, consistency-oriented implementation.

The proposed **mechanisms** are:
- measurement used as communication or categorisation rather than as a test of ends;
- proxies substituting for goals (goal displacement);
- buffering in specialist units;
- latent functions of policy enactment.

The proposed **consequences** are diverted resources, persistent failure to reach goals, and sometimes protection of the core work.

**Recoupling** has been traced to:
- inspection and regulation;
- accountability regimes (which can cause turmoil);
- demographic or ideological turnover;
- aspirational talk that creates moral entrapment;
- experimental, learning-oriented implementation.

## Q5. How can means–ends decoupling be operationalised in qualitative document analysis?

### Takeaway
Decoupling cannot be "seen" in a single document. It is a *relation*, established by comparing kinds of evidence:
- stated ends against specified means;
- means against implementation traces;
- implementation against outcome evidence;
- the causal theory linking them.

It is also established across genres (frontstage commitments versus backstage records) and over time (document versions). The persona recommends an abductive design. Sensitising concepts come from the theory: policy adoption, means, ends, opacity dimensions, measurement proxies and recoupling triggers. These are organised into a goal–activity–outcome (GAO) chain matrix and refined inductively, rather than locked into a fixed codebook from the outset. Published exemplars:
- counter-accounts comparing reports with independent news (Boiral 2013);
- talk/decision/action matrices (Cho et al. 2015);
- announced-versus-executed archival comparisons (Westphal & Zajac 2001);
- symbol-versus-substance in CSR reports (Marquis & Qian 2014);
- documentary tracking of the translation of rational myths (Zilber 2006).

  - Bowen, G. A. (2009). Document analysis as a qualitative research method. *Qualitative Research Journal*, 9(2), 27–40.
  - Reay, T., & Jones, C. (2016). Qualitatively capturing institutional logics. *Strategic Organization*, 14(4), 441–454. Pattern deducing, matching and inducing transfer directly to decoupling.

### Inferences
- [I] **Operational scheme the persona would propose (for co-design with the methodologist and the document-analysis expert):**
  1. **Unit of analysis = a commitment chain.** For each stated end E, for example "a lasting visitor-economy legacy for regional Queensland", build a row that records:
     - the institutional source of E: IOC requirement, Host Contract, State plan, or the bid;
     - the stated means M: programmes, structures, budgets, KPIs, owners, timelines;
     - implementation traces I: procurement notices, budget papers, annual reports, minutes, RTI releases, Hansard and estimates answers;
     - outcome or evaluation provisions O: baselines, metrics, independent evaluation, post-Games responsibility;
     - the causal theory C: the explicit or implied logic linking M to E.
  2. **Classification.** Read each chain as follows:
     - **Policy–practice decoupling**: M or E is stated, but I is absent or contradicts it.
     - **Means–ends decoupling**: I is present and compliant, but O measures outputs or proxies rather than E; C is missing or implausible given the opacity; or responsibility for E falls outside any continuing organisation.
     - **Coupled**: M, I and O are aligned with E, with a feedback loop (revision after evaluation).
     - **Selective coupling / compartmentalisation**: some components are coupled while others sit in a separate "legacy" unit with no decision rights.
  3. **Indicators of ceremonial adoption in texts:**
     - boilerplate or isomorphic phrasing, for example lifted from IOC templates (check with text-reuse comparison);
     - aspirational modality ("aim", "strive", "seek to") without owners, budgets or dates;
     - output-only KPIs;
     - no baseline;
     - evaluation deferred to after the Games or delegated to the IOC;
     - legacy bodies with no budget authority;
     - target drift or re-baselining across versions without explanation;
     - commitments that appear only in frontstage genres (bid book, press releases) and never in backstage ones (board minutes, contracts, budget papers).
  4. **Indicators of substantive coupling:**
     - dedicated budget lines and contractual clauses;
     - specific, measurable targets with baselines;
     - named accountable owners whose role extends past 2032;
     - independent assurance with consequences (QAO, parliamentary committees);
     - documented revision in response to evaluation (Athanasopoulou et al.'s "experimental" pattern).
  5. **Opacity coding (Wijen).** For each end, code causal complexity (attribution and lag), practice diversity (many delivery agents) and behavioural invisibility (whether activity can be observed). The expected pattern is that the more opaque the end, the more rigid the means rules and the larger the means–ends gap.
  6. **Temporal and recoupling coding.** Order document versions (2021 candidature → Host Contract → pre-2024 plans → 100 Day Review (2025) → 2032 Delivery Plan → later). Code triggers (review, audit, leadership change, cost crisis) and the direction of change: recoupling to ends, tight coupling to proxies, or re-ceremonialisation.
  7. **Talk / decision / action matrix (Brunsson; Cho et al.).** Treat bid and strategy texts as "talk", Cabinet, board and legislative instruments as "decisions", and procurement, expenditure and delivery records as "actions".
- [I] **Sensitising concepts versus codebook.** Use the constructs above as *sensitising* (Blumer) and *pattern-matching* (Reay & Jones) devices, documented in an evolving codebook with decision rules and an audit trail. Do not use a closed a-priori codebook. Decoupling in mega-events may take forms the typology does not anticipate, such as promissory, inter-organisational or deferred decoupling.
- [I] **Epistemic caution.** Documents are themselves ceremonial artefacts: "formal structure as myth". A document-only design can show *structural and textual* separation and *missing* means–ends links. It cannot directly observe practice. Claims should therefore be phrased as "documentary evidence of decoupling", and triangulated across genres and independent sources (audit reports, the press, NGO counter-accounts in the style of Boiral).
- [I] **Pre-event constraint.** If the corpus ends before 2032, ends cannot yet be observed. The thesis then studies *designed-in* or *prospective* means–ends decoupling: the absence of causal theory, evaluation and accountability for ends. Observing realised outcomes is out of reach. The persona will ask that this be stated as a scope condition.

## Q6. What are the major critiques and boundary conditions, and how does an expert guard against misapplication? (red flags and failure modes)

### Takeaway
The main critiques are:
- over-reach, tautology and myopia (Alvesson & Spicer 2019);
- neglect of power and politics (Munir 2015; Kern et al. 2018);
- the paradox of embedded agency;
- conceptual stretching of "decoupling" away from its institutional core (Haack & Schoeneborn 2015);
- an implicit normative bias that coupling is always good, although decoupling can protect the core work and aspirational talk can drive change.

The expert guards against these by demanding:
- construct clarity;
- explicit boundary conditions (institutional pressure, a legitimating audience, a demonstrable separation);
- falsifiable evidence and rival explanations;
- attention to power and to who sets the "ends";
- reflexivity about documents as performances.

### Cited Findings
- Alvesson & Spicer (2019) diagnose over-reach, myopia, tautology, pseudo-progress and re-inventing the wheel. They recommend narrowing the concept's scope, sharpening focus, avoiding tautological reasoning, and problematising the concept — [V](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/neo-institutional-theory-and-organization-studies-a-mid-life-cris/).
- Kern et al. (2018) set out to restore power and politics to the analysis of decoupling. They note that the logics actors use to justify claims may differ from the logics they enact — [V](https://www.skema.edu/en/faculty-and-research/publications/behind-smoke-and-mirrors-political-approach-decoupling).
- Haack & Schoeneborn (2015) question whether decoupling is becoming "decoupled from institutional theory" — [V](https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/is-decoupling-becoming-decoupled-from-institutional-theory-a-comm/).
- The 2016 *M@n@gement* piece on the micro-foundations of decoupling criticises the over-simple treatment of employees — [V](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-management-2016-4-page-248?lang=en).
- Positive readings of talk (Cho et al. commentary; aspirational talk) caution against treating all gaps as pathological — [V](https://cobsinsights.org/2016/03/10/hypocrisy-and-facades-how-sustainability-reports-can-be-good-for-people-planet-and-profit/).
- [K] Further critiques:
  - Suddaby, R. (2010). Challenges for institutional theory. *Journal of Management Inquiry*, 19(1), 14–20.
  - Suddaby, R. (2010). Editor's comments: Construct clarity in theories of management and organization. *AMR*, 35(3), 346–357.
  - Munir, K. A. (2015). A loss of power in institutional theory. *JMI*, 24(1).
  - Willmott, H. (2015). Why institutional theory cannot be critical. *JMI*, 24(1).
  - Alvesson, M., Hallett, T., & Spicer, A. (2019). Uninhibited institutionalisms. *JMI*, 28(2).
  - Sartori, G. (1970). Concept misformation in comparative politics. *American Political Science Review*, 64(4), 1033–1053. The source of "conceptual stretching".
  - Lawrence, T. B. (2008). Power, institutions and organizations. *SAGE Handbook* 1st ed.

### Inferences
- [I] **Red flags the persona will call out in a draft:**
  1. **Gap equals decoupling.** Labelling every promise–outcome shortfall as decoupling. Rival explanations must be ruled out first:
     - implementation failure or incapacity;
     - exogenous shocks;
     - optimism bias or cost escalation (Flyvbjerg);
     - deliberate policy change after an election or a review;
     - honest re-prioritisation.
  2. **Tautology.** Inferring legitimacy-seeking *from* the adoption and then explaining the adoption *by* legitimacy-seeking. The audience and the legitimacy pay-off need evidence independent of the act of adoption.
  3. **Conflation.** Mixing up policy–practice with means–ends decoupling, or decoupling with greenwashing. Greenwashing implies deliberate misleading communication; decoupling can be emergent (Crilly et al.).
  4. **Researcher-imposed ends.** Judging means against ends the researcher prefers, not those stated by the field or organisation. This turns institutional analysis into programme evaluation, or into critique by assertion.
  5. **Reading talk as practice.** Treating strategy documents as evidence of activity.
  6. **Ignoring power.** Not asking who defines the ends, who benefits from the separation, and who is harmed by recoupling (Hallett).
  7. **Normative bias.** Assuming coupling is always desirable. Under complexity, decoupling can be a rational way to hold plural logics together (Brunsson; Meyer & Rowan).
  8. **Level slippage.** Moving between field (Olympic Movement), organisation (OCOG, GIICA, departments) and programme without saying so.
  9. **Static analysis.** Treating decoupling as a state rather than a process (de-coupling, recoupling, re-ceremonialisation).
  10. **Edition and citation errors.** Citing Scott 3rd ed. as current; citing 2008 Handbook chapters as if they were the 2017 versions; quoting Bromley & Powell or Wijen second-hand.
  11. **Single-source definitional dependence.** Relying only on Bromley & Powell and ignoring Brunsson and translation theory (Czarniawska & Joerges; Sahlin & Wedlin), which explain local variation without positing a "gap".
  12. **Over-reach in contribution claims.** Claiming to "test" decoupling theory with a single, pre-event, document-only case.
- [I] **Safeguards:**
  - a glossary of constructs with source definitions;
  - an explicit "decoupling claim protocol" (pressure → audience → adoption → separation → rival explanations ruled out);
  - a search for negative cases;
  - peer debriefing with the methodologist;
  - an audit trail;
  - reflexive notes on the candidate's own normative stance toward the Olympics.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full critique texts (Munir; Willmott; Suddaby). Their arguments are summarised from established knowledge [K].

## Q7. How do organisational behaviour perspectives (sensemaking, identity, micro-level enactment) complement macro institutional analysis?

### Takeaway
OB perspectives explain *how* decoupling and recoupling are produced and lived:
- **Sensemaking and sensegiving** explain how actors interpret ambiguous ends and frame symbolic responses (Weick; Weber & Glynn 2006; Fiss & Zajac 2006).
- **Identity work** explains commitment to, or distance from, myths (Lok 2010; Creed et al. 2010).
- **Inhabited institutions** show that recoupling is enacted in interaction and can bring turmoil (Hallett & Ventresca 2006; Hallett 2010).
- **Micro-foundations work** links these levels (Powell & Colyvas 2008; Powell & Rerup 2017).

In a document-based thesis, micro-level enactment can be partly reached through minutes, correspondence, testimony and RTI releases, but the limits must be acknowledged.

### Cited Findings
- The 2016 *M@n@gement* article calls for micro-foundations of decoupling (a coping-theory perspective), against treating employees as mindless reproducers or change champions — [V](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-management-2016-4-page-248?lang=en).
- Kern et al. (2018): the gap between how logics are *used to justify* and how they are *enacted* is a micro-political phenomenon — [V](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0170840617693268).
- Athanasopoulou et al. (2025): experimental implementation, where actors generate local knowledge, restrains means–ends decoupling. This is a micro-practice mechanism — [V](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joms.13043).
- [K] Core OB sources:
  - Weick, K. E. (1976). Educational organizations as loosely coupled systems. *ASQ*, 21(1), 1–19.

  - Weick, K. E. (1995). *Sensemaking in Organizations*. Sage.
  - Weick, K. E., Sutcliffe, K. M., & Obstfeld, D. (2005). Organizing and the process of sensemaking. *Organization Science*, 16(4), 409–421.
  - Weber, K., & Glynn, M. A. (2006). Making sense with institutions: Context, thought and action in Karl Weick's theory. *Organization Studies*, 27(11), 1639–1660.

### Inferences
- [I] **Loose coupling is not decoupling.** Weick's loose coupling describes a structural property: elements are responsive yet separate. Ceremonial decoupling is a legitimacy-driven separation. The persona will police this distinction, because mega-event delivery networks are loosely coupled *by design* (multiple agencies, contracts, temporary bodies) whether or not ceremonial decoupling is present.
- [I] For Brisbane 2032, sensegiving analysis of ministerial statements and review reports can show how "legacy" and "tourism leverage" are *framed* to audiences: the IOC, the Commonwealth, ratepayers and regional communities. Identity analysis can show how delivery bodies present themselves ("independent", "world-class", "legacy-led"). Both are document-accessible, unlike tacit practice.
- [I] Practical bridge for a document-based design: treat minutes, committee transcripts (Hansard, estimates), RTI-released correspondence and review submissions as traces of micro-level enactment and sensemaking. Then state plainly that tacit, everyday practice is out of scope.

## (b) and (d) Expert behaviour and skills: what questions they ask, how they police precision, and what they can do

### Takeaway
The persona works by Socratic interrogation of the theory–data link. They ask "decoupling of what from what, for whom, under what pressure, and how would you know?" They require construct clarity and edition-accurate citation. They push for a contribution to institutional theory, and they model rigorous, auditable abduction.

### Cited Findings
- Construct clarity and avoiding tautology are the field's own remedies for its mid-life crisis (Alvesson & Spicer 2019) — [V](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/20777/). Construct-clarity norms come from Suddaby (2010), *AMR* [K].
- [K] Standards for theoretical contribution the persona applies:
  - Whetten, D. A. (1989). What constitutes a theoretical contribution? *AMR*, 14(4), 490–495.
  - Corley, K. G., & Gioia, D. A. (2011). Building theory about theory building. *AMR*, 36(1), 12–32.
- Recent exemplars of means–ends decoupling theorised from qualitative cases (Athanasopoulou et al. 2025; Kaufmann et al. 2024/25) are models for theorising from case data — [V](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joms.13043); [V](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5197024).

### Inferences
- [I] **Questions the persona habitually asks the candidate:**
  1. "State your decoupling claim in one sentence: what is decoupled from what, in which organisation or field, from when to when?"
  2. "Which form is it: policy–practice, means–ends, talk–talk/decision/action (Brunsson), or selective coupling? Why not the others?"
  3. "Whose ends? Point me to the document, clause and date where each end is stated, and to its institutional source: the IOC, the Host Contract, State legislation or the bid."
  4. "Who is the legitimating audience, and what evidence, independent of the adoption itself, shows legitimacy was at stake?"
  5. "What would coupling look like in your documents? Show me a coupled chain so I can see the contrast."
  6. "Which rival explanations have you ruled out (incapacity, shocks, cost escalation, deliberate re-prioritisation), and how?"
  7. "Is the decoupling deliberate or emergent (Crilly et al.)? Can your data actually tell the difference?"
  8. "How opaque is each end, in Wijen's sense? Does opacity predict where the gaps are?"
  9. "Where does measurement substitute for ends (Power; Espeland & Sauder; Kaufmann et al.)?"
  10. "Is there recoupling? What triggered it (the 100 Day Review, QAO, a change of government), and is it recoupling to ends or tighter coupling to budget and time proxies?"
  11. "Where is power? Who gains from the separation, and who bears the cost of recoupling?"
  12. "What do you contribute to *institutional theory*? What does a temporary, multi-level, pre-event setting reveal that firm-level CSR studies cannot?"
  13. "Which edition are you citing, and have you read the original definition rather than a secondary paraphrase?"
  14. "How do you treat documents: as evidence of practice, or as institutional performances? Can your claims carry that weight?"
- [I] **How the persona polices conceptual precision:**
  - requires a construct glossary covering decoupling (both forms), recoupling, loose coupling, organized hypocrisy, façade, symbolic management, greenwashing, goal displacement, selective coupling, compartmentalisation, translation, aspirational talk, legitimacy (pragmatic, moral, cognitive) and institutional logic;
  - rejects synonyms used interchangeably;
  - asks for scope conditions;
  - checks that each empirical claim maps to a defined construct and a coding rule.
- [I] **Skills profile:**
  - command of the sociological (Meyer, Zucker, Brunsson) and management (AMJ/AMR/ASQ/OS) literatures;
  - conceptual analysis (Sartori's ladder of abstraction);
  - typology building;
  - abductive qualitative theorising;
  - historical and processual analysis;
  - critical reading of public-sector accountability instruments;
  - supervising theory chapters;
  - anticipating examiners;
  - journal-targeting advice (*Organization Studies*, *JMS*, *Journal of Management Inquiry*, *Sport Management Review*, *International Journal of Sport Policy and Politics*, *Annals of Tourism Research*);
  - cross-disciplinary translation between institutional theory, sport management and tourism.
