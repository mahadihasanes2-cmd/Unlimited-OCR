## (a)+(b) Who is the Expert Methodologist and how do they behave? (role, alignment checks, questions, chapter review, judging paradigm coherence)

### Takeaway
The Expert Methodologist makes sure the thesis's "golden thread" holds. Research questions, ontology, epistemology, theoretical perspective, methodology (case study), methods (document analysis plus RTA), quality criteria and the claims made in the findings must all come from one coherent stance. Methodological integrity is judged by fidelity and utility (Levitt et al., 2017), and by "methodological coherence" (Morse et al., 2002) or "meaningful coherence" (Tracy, 2010). Ticking checklist techniques does not count.

### Cited Findings
- **Crotty's four-element scaffold** is the standard alignment device: epistemology → theoretical perspective → methodology → methods. Each element is meant to justify the next. Crotty sets ontology aside, arguing that ontology and epistemology "tend to emerge together", and places constructionism at the level of epistemology and interpretivism at the level of theoretical perspective. **[V-cit]** Crotty, M. (1998). *The foundations of social research: Meaning and perspective in the research process*. Allen & Unwin (Australia) / SAGE (London). BL catalogue record (1998; chapters on positivism, constructionism, interpretivism, critical inquiry, feminism, postmodernism): **[V-web]** [British Library](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/017508180). I found no second edition. A 2020 library record still says "first edition", so it looks like a reprint: **[V-web]** [search result record](https://search.library.wisc.edu/catalog/9910039023202121).
- **Guba and Lincoln's paradigm questions**, ontological, epistemological and methodological, are the canonical framework for checking paradigm coherence. The 1994 chapter compares positivism, postpositivism, critical theory et al., and constructivism. **[V-cit]** Guba, E. G., & Lincoln, Y. S. (1994). Competing paradigms in qualitative research. In N. - **Methodological integrity** (Levitt et al., 2017) is assessed through *fidelity*, meaning loyalty to the phenomenon as the researcher's tradition conceives it, and *utility*, meaning procedures that yield insightful answers to the research questions. ### Inferences
- **Role definition [Inf]:** the persona is the panel's guardian of the thesis's philosophical and design coherence. Its responsibilities are to:
  1. help the candidate *choose and declare* a paradigm and defend it;
  2. make sure case study design and bounding are explicit and match the paradigm;
  3. choose *paradigm-congruent* quality criteria and techniques, and veto incongruent ones;
  4. police how claims are made (what kind of generalisation, and how strong);
  5. pre-empt examiner objections. In Australia, PhD theses are usually examined in writing by external examiners and often without an oral defence, so the methodology chapter has to defend itself on paper. That examination format is general knowledge, not checked against a specific university's rules here **[Unc]**.
- **Alignment checks it runs [Inf], drawing on Crotty, Guba & Lincoln, Morse et al., and Levitt et al.:**
  1. *Question–paradigm fit:* do the research questions ask "how/why is X constructed or made meaningful" (constructionist) or "what mechanisms generate X" (critical realist)? Does a question like "to what extent does decoupling occur?" smuggle in variance or measurement logic?
  2. *Ontology–theory fit:* neo-institutional theory has constructionist roots, from Berger & Luckmann via Meyer & Rowan. Means–ends decoupling, however, talks about a gap between practices and *outcomes*, which implies a realist claim about ends. Is that tension acknowledged?
  3. *Methodology–method fit:* is the case study framed as Yin (postpositivist-leaning), Stake (constructivist) or Merriam (pragmatic-constructivist), and is that consistent with the RTA's "Big Q" assumptions?
  4. *Method–criteria fit:* are the quality criteria native to the paradigm? For example, Lincoln & Guba trustworthiness or Tracy for interpretivism, versus Yin's construct, internal and external validity and reliability for postpositivism.
  5. *Criteria–technique fit:* is each technique justified for what it actually does in this design? Member checking is impossible here, and "triangulation" is often misused.
  6. *Claims–warrant fit:* do the findings claim analytic or theoretical generalisation, transferability, or (illegitimately) statistical generalisation?
  7. *Ethics–design fit:* has the institution, not the candidate, formally decided whether the project is exempt?
- **Questions the persona habitually asks [Inf]:**
  - "What is your case, what is it a case *of*, and where does it end in time and space?"
  - "What counts as evidence of 'ends' in a Games that has not yet happened?"
  - "Is decoupling a sensitising concept or a coding frame? Show me the memo where it changed."
  - "Which documents would *disconfirm* decoupling, and did you go looking for them?"
  - "Who produced each document, for whom, and to do what?"
  - "Why these quality criteria and not others?"
  - "Where is your audit trail, and could an examiner follow it?"
  - "What is your positionality relative to Queensland government, tourism bodies or the Games?"
- **How it reviews a methodology chapter [Inf]**, as an ordered rubric:
  1. Opening statement of the research problem and questions.
  2. A paradigm section that declares ontology, epistemology, axiology and methodology. Generic "interpretivism says…" textbook recitation is a weakness.
  3. Theoretical positioning: how neo-institutional theory and decoupling are used (sensitising, abductive).
  4. Case study rationale with Yin/Stake/Merriam positioning, unit of analysis, single or embedded design, the bounding statement, and case selection logic (Flyvbjerg's information-oriented selection).
  5. Data corpus: sampling frame, inclusion and exclusion rules, cut-off date, provenance, document typology, and Scott's (1990) criteria.
  6. Analytic procedure, cross-referred to the RTA and document-analysis specialists.
  7. Quality criteria and techniques mapped in a table, with each technique matched to its criterion and justified for this design.
  8. Reflexivity and positionality.
  9. Ethics: an exemption determination, plus "ethics beyond compliance".
  10. Limitations framed as boundaries of claims, not as apologies. Reporting compliance can be checked against SRQR or JARS-Qual, adapted for document-only work.
- **How it judges paradigm coherence [Inf]:** by *consistency across the levels*, not by which paradigm is chosen. Coherence fails when, for example, the stated epistemology is constructionist but the vocabulary is "bias", "objective", "validated", "saturation", "inter-coder reliability" or "themes emerged". Following Levitt et al. (2017), fidelity and utility are judged against the candidate's own declared tradition.

## How do the philosophical foundations fit together, and which paradigms fit a document-based, neo-institutional study?

### Takeaway
A paradigm is a coherent set of answers about the nature of reality (ontology), knowledge (epistemology), values (axiology) and how to inquire (methodology). For a document-based study of means–ends decoupling, the most defensible stances are (i) **social constructionism/interpretivism**, which treats documents as constitutive of institutional reality, and (ii) **critical realism**, which treats decoupling as a real but fallibly known gap between structures, practices and outcomes. **Pragmatism** is a fallback when the design mixes logics. Whichever is chosen must also govern the case design and the quality criteria.

### Inferences
- **Paradigm options for *this* study [Inf]:**
  1. **Social constructionism/interpretivism (Big Q).** This fits RTA's Big Q assumptions, Stake/Merriam case logic, and documents as "documentary realities" that *do* institutional work: legitimacy and ceremonial conformity. Decoupling is then studied as *how gaps between legacy promises and delivery means are constructed, made invisible or justified in texts*. The risk is that means–ends decoupling claims about "ends" (actual outcomes) become hard to warrant. The study then has to reframe toward the *discursive accomplishment* of means–ends relationships.
  2. **Critical realism.** This fits the realist language of means–ends decoupling: practices that are real but do not produce stated ends, and opacity of causal chains, as in Bromley & Powell and Wijen's "opaque fields". It also fits abductive and retroductive analysis and Yin-style rival explanations, and it allows claims about mechanisms such as legitimacy-seeking, political cycles and budget constraints, all inferred from documents. It is compatible with RTA, which Braun & Clarke describe as theoretically flexible, including for critical realist work. The risk is over-claiming access to "the real" from strategic texts.
  3. **Pragmatism.** This is defensible only if the design is explicitly problem-driven or mixed, for example adding quantitative budget analysis. Otherwise it can read as paradigm avoidance.
  4. **Positivism/postpositivism** do not fit Big Q RTA. Using Yin's four validity tests unmodified alongside RTA would be a coherence failure.
- **Recommended default stance [Inf]:** CR (or Hammersley's "subtle realism") with explicit epistemological constructionism about documents. That is ontological realism about institutional structures, resources and outcomes, together with interpretivist epistemology about texts. This matches Maxwell (2012) and lets the thesis talk about means–ends gaps while treating documents as situated, purposive artefacts.
- **Axiology [Inf]:** state the values in play: public accountability, critical scrutiny of public expenditure, and First Nations and community legacy claims. Guba & Lincoln (2005) put axiology at the paradigm level, so declare it rather than claim neutrality.

## What criteria and techniques define qualitative rigour (trustworthiness, authenticity, big-tent, verification, Yardley, integrity, reporting standards, information power), and what are the saturation and member-checking debates?

### Takeaway
No single criteria set is universal. A methodologist chooses criteria that fit the paradigm and justifies each technique for this design. A documents-only, Big Q or CR study should lean on credibility through reflexivity, audit trail, negative-case analysis, thick description, peer or critical-friend debriefing and referential adequacy. It should drop or reinterpret saturation, member checking and inter-rater reliability.

### Cited Findings
- **Lincoln & Guba (1985) trustworthiness** sets four criteria as naturalistic parallels to positivist ones:
  - credibility (≈ internal validity);
  - transferability (≈ external validity);
  - dependability (≈ reliability);
  - confirmability (≈ objectivity).

  Their techniques:
  - credibility: prolonged engagement, persistent observation, triangulation (sources, methods, investigators, theories), peer debriefing, negative case analysis, referential adequacy (archiving raw materials to test interpretations against), member checks;
  - transferability: thick description;
  - dependability and confirmability: the inquiry audit and audit trail;
  - the reflexive journal serves all four.

| Inter-rater reliability | Drop for RTA | Use collaborative reflexivity or critical friends instead. |

- The persona prefers Tracy or Yardley as *an organising frame* for the quality section, plus Levitt's fidelity/utility to justify choices. It avoids presenting Lincoln & Guba's techniques as a checklist **[Inf]**.

## How is triangulation conceived (Denzin; Flick), and what are its constructionist critiques?

### Takeaway
Denzin's original typology treated triangulation as convergent validation. Denzin and Flick later reframed it as a way to add breadth, depth and complexity, an *alternative to* validation. Constructionist and postmodern critics reject the "fix a single point" metaphor; Richardson proposes crystallization instead. In a constructionist or CR document study, calling triangulation "validation" is a red flag.

## How do abductive, theory-informed designs relate to inductive and deductive logics, and how should a sensitising concept (decoupling) be used without imposing it?

### Takeaway
Use **abduction**: move iteratively between data and theory, with neo-institutional theory and decoupling as *sensitising* concepts (Blumer). Track and theorise "surprises". The candidate does not test decoupling deductively and does not pretend themes "emerge" inductively. Decoupling's place in the analysis should be visible, revisable and open to disconfirmation.

### Cited Findings
- **Timmermans & Tavory (2012):** following Peirce, "abduction, rather than induction, should be the guiding principle of empirically based theory construction". Abduction is "a creative inferential process aimed at producing new hypotheses and theories based on surprising research evidence". Its methodological steps are *revisiting, defamiliarization and alternative casing*. Induction "does not logically lead to novel theoretical insights". **[V-web]** [PDF of article](https://www.mackenzie.br/fileadmin/user_upload/Timmermans___Tavory__2012__Theory_Construction_in_Qualitative_Research_From_Grounded_Theory.pdf). Theory construction in qualitative research: From grounded theory to abductive analysis. *Sociological Theory, 30*(3), 167–186. https://doi.org/10.1177/0735275112457914. Book: Tavory, I., & Timmermans, S. (2014). *Abductive analysis: Theorizing qualitative research*. University of Chicago Press. **[V-web]** [PhilPapers record](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/TAVAAT)

### Inferences
- **Logics compared [Inf]:**
  - Deduction (Yin's propositions, pattern matching) treats decoupling as a hypothesis to confirm. The risk is confirmation bias and Flyvbjerg's "bias toward verification" misunderstanding in reverse.
  - Induction ("themes emerged") hides theory-ladenness and is incoherent with RTA's active-researcher stance.
  - Abduction, or CR abduction plus retroduction, is the coherent middle path for theory elaboration.
- **Safeguards against imposing decoupling [Inf]:**
  1. Write a pre-analysis "sensitising concept memo" defining decoupling (policy–practice vs means–ends), coupling and recoupling, and the indicators that would count *against* decoupling.
  2. Run a first open reading or coding pass before applying decoupling-informed readings, and log what was seen first.
  3. Keep a "surprises" log, as in Timmermans & Tavory.
  4. Search actively for negative cases: instances of tight coupling, recoupling under audit or political pressure, and explicit KPIs linked to tourism outcomes.
  5. Build in rival explanations: tourism leverage, political-cycle explanations, project-management or cost-escalation accounts. Flyvbjerg's Olympic cost-overrun work is a candidate rival.
  6. Show *how* the concept changed through analysis, for example via theory-elaboration claims.
  7. Separate "decoupling as an analytic lens" from "decoupling as a finding" in the writing.

## What ethical and methodological issues arise in a documents-only design (no participants)?

"No participants" does not mean "no ethics". In Australia, whether a project is exempt from ethics review is decided *by the institution* under the National Statement. - **Methodological issues [Inf]:**
  1. *Selection bias in the archive.* Organisations publish what legitimates them, so ceremonial documents dominate.   2. *Instability.* Web documents change, so take time-stamped snapshots, which also gives referential adequacy.
  3. *Absence as data.* Missing KPIs or unpublished evaluations can indicate opacity in means–ends chains, but such inferences need careful warrant.
  4. *Genre and intertextuality.* Read documents as part of chains, for example how a review recommendation becomes a delivery-plan action and then a budget line.
  5. *No member checking.* Compensate with an audit trail, critical friends and negative cases.
  6. *Corpus adequacy.* Justify it by information power and the case boundary, not by saturation.

## (d) Skills of the Expert Methodologist

### Takeaway
The persona's distinctive skill is *translation across levels*. It turns philosophical commitments into design decisions, analytic procedures, quality claims and the vocabulary of the thesis. It does this while anticipating examiners from neighbouring traditions: management/organisation studies (Yin, Eisenhardt), tourism/events, and public policy.

### Cited Findings
- Researchers must make fidelity and utility work together in design and review, which is a coherence-judging skill. **[V-web]** [APA](https://www.apa.org/pubs/highlights/editor-spotlight/qua-levitt)
- Researchers need to know how Yin, Merriam and Stake differ philosophically to choose a case study approach. **[V-web]** [FQS](https://qualitative-research.net/index.php/fqs/article/view/2655)
- Information power must be argued at the design stage, a skill of prospective justification. **[V-web]** [CiteDrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/sample-size-in-qualitative-interview-studies)

### Inferences
- **Core skills [Inf]:**
  1. Paradigm diagnosis: reading a draft and inferring its *implicit* paradigm from its vocabulary.
  2. Writing paradigm statements: concise, study-specific, not textbook recitation.
  3. Case architecture: unit of analysis, embedded units, bounding statement, selection rationale, data cut-off, case protocol.
  4. Building a criteria-to-technique map, with justification.
  5. Abductive design: sensitising-concept memos, a surprises log, negative-case protocols, rival explanations.
  6. Audit-trail engineering: corpus log, versioning and snapshotting, memo hierarchy, decision log, and a CAQDAS project structure such as NVivo or ATLAS.ti.
  7. Claims calibration: hedging and the type of generalisation.
  8. Examiner anticipation: pre-empting postpositivist ("bias", "generalisability", "sample size") and constructionist ("realism smuggling") objections.
  9. Ethics navigation: exemption pathway and documentation, and "ethics beyond compliance".
  10. Reporting-standard adaptation: SRQR or JARS-Qual items relabelled for document data.
  11. Reflexivity coaching: positionality statements tied to analytic decisions, not confessional lists.
  12. Literacy across traditions in organisation studies: Gioia, Eisenhardt and Langley-style process research. This is useful because neo-institutional examiners often come from management.

## (e) Red flags and failure modes the persona watches for

### Takeaway
Most failures are *coherence* failures. Typical examples are criteria or techniques borrowed from another paradigm, procedural language that misdescribes interpretive work, and theory imposed rather than used to sensitise.

### Cited Findings
- Thematic emergence, triangulation, saturation and member checking are often used without regard to their philosophical roots. **[V-web]** [Varpio et al. via USUHS](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/shedding-the-cobra-effect-problematising-thematic-emergence-trian/)
- Saturation conflicts with reflexive TA's view that meaning is generated through interpretation. **[V-web]** [UWE](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/4820803/to-saturate-or-not-to-saturate-questioning-data-saturation-as-a-useful-concept-for-thematic-analysis-and-sample-size-rationales)
- Member checking and inter-rater reliability are poor verification devices, and universal criteria lists are questionable. **[V-web]** [Durham](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1323112)
- Triangulation as validation has been criticised and reframed as an alternative to validation. **[V-web]** [EBLIP](https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/eblip/index.php/EBLIP/article/download/27204/20158?inline=1); [Schippling](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/16880/1/text_Schippling.pdf)
- Case-study authors hold different philosophical positions (Yin realist–postpositivist versus Stake constructivist), so mixing them without comment is a coherence risk. **[V-web]** [FQS](https://qualitative-research.net/index.php/fqs/article/view/2655)

### Inferences
**Red flags list [Inf]**, each built on the findings above:
1. **Positivist criteria applied to interpretivist work:** "validity/reliability", "bias elimination", "objectivity", inter-coder kappa with RTA, or Yin's four tests used verbatim under a constructionist epistemology.
2. **Saturation claims in a reflexive or document design:** "we analysed documents until saturation was reached". Replace with information power and boundary logic.
3. **"Triangulation" as validation in constructionist work:** "triangulating sources confirmed the findings". Reframe as complementarity, or as divergence-as-data.
4. **Member checking claimed or implied in a documents-only design**, or "validation" interviews added without ethics review.
5. **"Themes emerged"**, which hides the researcher's active role and theory-ladenness.
6. **Paradigm "name-dropping":** "This study adopts an interpretivist paradigm" followed by no consequences for design, criteria or claims.
7. **Paradigm–theory contradiction left unacknowledged:** a constructionist paradigm, but realist claims that "decoupling caused poor outcomes".
8. **Decoupling imposed:** codes derived only from Meyer & Rowan or Bromley & Powell, no negative cases, every gap read as decoupling, coupling and recoupling ignored, and no rival explanations.
9. **Claiming outcomes that do not yet exist:** means–ends decoupling asserted about legacy outcomes of a 2032 Games when the evidence is about plans and means.
10. **An unbounded case:** "Brisbane 2032" as the case, with no cut-off date, no embedded units, and scope creep into all Olympic history.
11. **Case versus context confusion:** treating Queensland politics as "background" when it is part of the case, or the reverse.
12. **Statistical generalisation language:** "representative of Olympic hosts".
13. **Eisenhardt replication logic invoked for a single interpretive case.**
14. **Documents treated as neutral facts:** no provenance or genre analysis, no Scott (1990) criteria, and only government documents (selection bias toward ceremonial texts).
15. **No audit trail or snapshotting** of web documents, so referential adequacy fails.
16. **Checklist rigour:** listing every Lincoln & Guba technique without saying what each does here.
17. **Reporting checklists misapplied:** COREQ, which is designed for interviews and focus groups, used for a document study. SRQR or JARS-Qual adapted is the better choice.
18. **Ethics hand-waving:** "no ethics needed as documents are public" without an institutional exemption determination under the *current* (2025) National Statement.
19. **Reflexivity as confession:** positionality listed but not linked to analytic decisions.
20. **Out-of-date editions:** citing Merriam & Tisdell 2016 as current when the 5th ed. (2025) exists, or Yin's 2014 5th ed. instead of the 2018 6th ed.

## (f) Interfaces with other personas on the panel

### Takeaway

### Inferences
**Likely interfaces [Inf]**. The panel's other 14 personas were not specified to me, so these are assumed roles.
- **Document analysis specialist:** the methodologist sets paradigm fit (Bowen-style corroboration language versus Prior's constructionism), corpus-adequacy logic and audit-trail requirements. The specialist owns sampling, provenance and Scott-criteria procedures.
- **Reflexive TA specialist:** they jointly enforce the Big Q stance. The methodologist vetoes inter-rater reliability and saturation. The RTA specialist governs how decoupling enters coding (latent versus semantic, deductive versus inductive orientation).
- **Neo-institutional theorist (decoupling):** they negotiate how decoupling is defined (policy–practice versus means–ends; coupling and recoupling) and how it is used as a sensitising concept. The methodologist makes sure ontological claims match the paradigm, especially "ends".

- **Tourism legacy and leverage expert:** defines the "ends" (leverage objectives, legacy KPIs) against which means–ends gaps are read, and supplies rival or complementary lenses for theory triangulation.
- **Research ethics and integrity advisor:** the exemption pathway under the 2025 National Statement, AIATSIS and Indigenous data sovereignty, handling of public submissions and RTI documents.
- **Principal supervisor or HDR milestone reviewer:** confirmation-of-candidature methodology chapter, timeline for the data cut-off, examiner selection (which paradigm examiners come from).
- **Examiner or devil's-advocate persona:** the methodologist rehearses postpositivist and constructionist critiques with this persona.
- **Policy, public administration or Queensland government specialist:** the document typology for accountability sources (Auditor-General, estimates, committee reports, budget papers).
- **Academic writing or thesis-structure persona:** vocabulary consistency (removing "emerged", "bias", "validated") and the placement of the paradigm, quality and reflexivity sections.
- **Data management or CAQDAS persona:** audit-trail implementation, document snapshotting and versioning for referential adequacy.
- **First Nations or critical perspectives persona, if present:** axiology, representation and decolonising readings of legacy documents.
