## 1. The persona's role: who the Committee Reviewer/Examiner is and what they do

### Takeaway
This persona combines two Australian gatekeeping roles. The first is the **internal milestone reviewer**: a confirmation, mid-candidature or pre-submission (thesis review) panel member who is independent of the supervisory team. The second is the **external thesis examiner**: usually two, sometimes three, examiners of international standing who are external to the university and free of conflicts of interest. Both judge whether the work meets AQF Level 10 and the university's own criteria. The central question is whether the thesis makes an original, significant contribution to knowledge, and here specifically to neo-institutional theory and mega-event governance scholarship, rather than merely applying theory to a new case.

### Cited Findings
- Following ACGR principles, Australian universities require examiners to be of international standing, independent of the research and free of real or perceived conflicts. For doctoral programs, **all examiners must be external to the University**. For masters, at least one examiner must be external. [V-S] — [UniSQ HDR examination procedure](https://policy.unisq.edu.au/documents/151767PL); [PDF version](https://policy.unisq.edu.au/documents/151767PL/pdf)
- Western Sydney University's 2026 Guide for Examiners says it will not proceed with an examiner who, in the past five years, has researched or published with the candidate or supervisor, or has been an adjunct or employee of the university. It describes its approach as benchmarked against ACGR best-practice guidelines. [V-S] — [WSU GRS HDR Guide for Examiners 2026](https://westernsydney.edu.au/content/dam/digital/pdf/eds/schools/grs/GRS-HDR-Guide-for-Examiners-2026.pdf)

Supervisors and candidates must not contact examiners from the Notice of Intention to Submit until the examination ends. ### Inferences
- [I] To be useful, the persona should switch between two modes. **Milestone-panel mode** is developmental: is the project on track to become a Level 10 contribution, and what must change now? **External-examiner mode** is summative: does this text, as submitted, meet the standard, and which recommendation category applies? The same criteria apply in both, but tone, consequences and permitted remedies differ.
- [I] An examiner of this thesis would most likely come from organisation studies or public administration (institutional theory), sport and event management, or tourism. The persona should anticipate how each of these disciplinary readers will judge the contribution. A sport-management examiner may value the practical legacy and leverage insight, while an organisation-theory examiner will ask what the thesis adds to decoupling theory.

## 2. AQF Level 10 and the Doctoral Degree descriptor; the 2019 AQF Review (Noonan); the government response; the Universities Accord; reform status as of October 2026

### Takeaway
The **AQF Second Edition (January 2013)** still appears to be the operative framework in October 2026. I found no evidence of a published revised AQF. In 2026 TEQSA, universities and training bodies still cite the 2013 descriptors, and ATEC's May 2026 discussion paper still treats the 2019 AQF Review's recommendations as unfinished business. Level 10 requires systemic, critical, frontier-level knowledge and research skills to generate **original knowledge** that makes a **substantial / significant and original contribution**, presented "for external examination against international standards". The Doctoral Degree typically takes 3–4 years, with research typically **two-thirds or more** of a research doctorate.

## 3. TEQSA Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021, Standard 4.2 Research Training, and ACGR good-practice guidance on examination

### Takeaway
HESF 2021 binds every registered provider. Standard 4.2 requires a research-active environment, qualified and trained supervision (a team, not a lone supervisor), monitoring of progress, and thesis assessment by assessors who are independent and have international standing. Standard 1.4 requires learning outcomes consistent with the AQF level. ACGR guidelines translate these requirements into sector good practice on examiner independence, conflicts of interest and supervision. The exact legislative wording could not be retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- [T, verify] The *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021* is a legislative instrument under the *TEQSA Act 2011*. It commenced in **2021**, replacing HESF 2015 (in force from 1 January 2017), which had itself replaced the 2011 Threshold Standards. Text: legislation.gov.au (instrument F2021L00488, later compilations). [U] on the commencement date and compilation ID. The attempted URL was https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488/latest/text.
- [T, paraphrase only, NOT verbatim; verify] **Standard 4.2 Research Training** covers, in substance:
  1. research training conducted in an environment of research or other scholarly activity, with sufficient research activity and peers in the field;
  2. supervision by a team of at least two, with the main or principal supervisor research-active (or with equivalent professional experience) and trained in supervision, and continuity of supervision arrangements;
  3. candidates informed of, and supported in, their responsibilities, rights, research integrity, ethics and intellectual property;
  4. formal monitoring of each candidate's progress, with mechanisms for support and intervention; and

### Inferences
- [I] For the Committee Reviewer persona, the **HESF sets the floor** (independence, international standing, supervision, progress monitoring). The **AQF sets the outcome standard** (Level 10). **ACGR** sets sector good practice. The **university's rules and examiner form** set the operative criteria and recommendation categories. A rigorous audit cites all four layers.

## 5. What research on examiner behaviour shows (Australian and international, 1984–2026)

### Takeaway
Examiners are **favourably disposed**: they expect a thesis to pass and read it as a colleague. They form **early impressions** from the abstract, introduction, conclusion and references. They judge **coherence**, command of the literature, methodological fit, and a clearly articulated contribution. Sloppy presentation erodes trust in rigour. Their reports contain a great deal of formative comment. "Originality" is interpreted broadly and pragmatically ("It's a PhD, not a Nobel Prize"), but "doctorateness" requires that the parts form a defensible, theorised whole.

### Cited Findings
**Australian foundational studies**
- **Mullins & Kiley (2002)** interviewed experienced examiners across disciplines in Australian universities. [T] — Mullins, G., & Kiley, M. (2002). ## 6. How an original theoretical contribution is judged in management and social science (Whetten; Corley & Gioia; Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan; incremental vs revelatory; theory contribution vs empirical application)

### Takeaway
Management examiners ask whether the thesis changes **how we understand** a phenomenon, through its *what/how/why/boundary conditions* (Whetten), rather than showing that an existing theory "works" in a new case. Contributions are judged on **originality**, from incremental to revelatory, and on **utility**, scientific and practical (Corley & Gioia). They are also judged on the degree of **theory building** (from replication up to new construct or reconceptualisation) and of **theory testing** (Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan). For this PhD, "Brisbane 2032 shows means–ends decoupling" is an application. Explaining *how, why and under what conditions* means–ends decoupling arises, persists or is remedied in temporary, multi-level mega-event governance, and what this does to legacy and leverage, is a theoretical contribution.

### Cited Findings
- **Whetten (1989)** identifies the building blocks of theory: **What** (factors or constructs), **How** (relationships), **Why** (underlying logic or mechanisms) and **Who/Where/When** (boundary conditions). He poses seven questions reviewers ask: *What's new? So what? Why so? Well done? Done well? Why now? Who cares?* He argues that applying an existing theory in a new setting contributes theoretically only when the new setting teaches us something about the theory itself, for example by changing its boundary conditions or mechanisms, rather than merely confirming it. (This point is paraphrased, not a direct quote.) [T] — Whetten, D. A. (1989). What constitutes a theoretical contribution? *Academy of Management Review*, 14(4), 490–495. [doi:10.5465/amr.1989.4308371](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1989.4308371)
- **Corley & Gioia (2011)** frame contribution on two dimensions. **Originality** runs from *incremental* to *revelatory*. **Utility** has a *scientific* side (advancing conceptual rigour or testability) and a *practical* side (relevance to managers or policy). They argue for "prescient" theorising that anticipates important problems. [T] — Corley, K. G., & Gioia, D. A. (2011). Building theory about theory building: What constitutes a theoretical contribution? *Academy of Management Review*, 36(1), 12–32. [doi:10.5465/amr.2009.0486](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2009.0486)
- **Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan (2007)** coded five decades of *AMJ* articles on two 5-point scales. [T] — Colquitt, J. A., & Zapata-Phelan, C. P. (2007). Trends in theory building and theory testing: A five-decade study of the Academy of Management Journal. *Academy of Management Journal*, 50(6), 1281–1303. [doi:10.5465/amj.2007.28165855](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.28165855)
  - **Theory building scale:**
    1. replicates previously demonstrated effects;
    2. examines effects that have been the subject of prior theorising;
    3. introduces a new mediator or moderator of an existing relationship or process;
    4. examines a previously unexplored relationship or process;
    5. introduces a new construct or significantly reconceptualises an existing one.
  - **Theory testing scale:**
    1. inductive;
    2. grounded in past findings;
    3. grounded in existing conceptual arguments;
    4. grounded in existing models, diagrams or figures;
    5. grounded in existing theory.
  - **Resulting types:** *Reporters* (low/low), *Testers* (low building/high testing), *Qualifiers* (moderate on both), *Builders* (high building/low testing) and *Expanders* (high/high). Articles scoring higher on both dimensions attracted more citations. [T; scale anchors U, verify wording]
- **What theory is not:** references, data, lists of variables, diagrams and hypotheses are not theory in themselves. Theory explains *why*. Theorising is a process, judged by its approximation to theory. [T] — Sutton, R. I., & Staw, B. M. (1995). What theory is not. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 40(3), 371–384. [doi:10.2307/2393788](https://doi.org/10.2307/2393788); Weick, K. E. (1995). What theory is not, theorizing is. *ASQ*, 40(3), 385–390. [doi:10.2307/2393789](https://doi.org/10.2307/2393789)

### Inferences
- [I] **How examiners will distinguish theoretical contribution from empirical application in this thesis:**
  - *Application (Colquitt–Zapata-Phelan building level 1–2):* "Brisbane 2032 governance documents show legacy and tourism-leverage goals decoupled from delivery practices, consistent with Bromley & Powell." This is useful empirically but is likely to be read as **incremental** and could leave examiners asking "so what for theory?"
  - *Contribution (level 3–5):* the thesis identifies a **mechanism** (e.g., deadline-compressed, multi-organisational "temporary field" governance that produces means–ends decoupling through specific practices such as venue-first sequencing, outcome indicators translated into outputs, or legacy offices without levers). Alternatively it specifies **boundary conditions** (when mega-event organisations recouple, e.g., under host-contract or IOC "New Norm" or Olympic Agenda 2020+5 pressures, or under audit and parliamentary scrutiny). It might also **reconceptualise** a construct (e.g., "legacy" as a rationalised myth, or "leverage" as a recoupling strategy), or **problematise** an assumption (e.g., that means–ends decoupling is a failure rather than a functional buffering strategy in mega-events). Each must be stated in the language of constructs, relationships, mechanisms and boundaries (Whetten's what, how, why, who/where/when).
- [I] **Incremental vs revelatory:** a single-case, document-based PhD is more plausibly an *incremental-to-moderately-revelatory* contribution. Examiners will accept incremental contributions if they are clearly specified, well evidenced and positioned against the specific debates (Bromley & Powell 2012; Wijen 2014; mega-event governance literature). Overclaiming "revelatory" status without problematising core assumptions is a common examiner irritant.
- [I] **Scientific vs practical utility:** in this thesis, practical utility (e.g., for Brisbane 2032 legacy and leverage planning) is valued by sport, tourism and policy examiners. However, it must not replace scientific utility, which is what the AQF's "original knowledge" requires.
- [I] **"Theory as label" test:** the examiner checks whether "decoupling" is *operationalised* in the analysis (coding frame, indicators of means, ends and practices, and evidence types) or only *invoked* in the introduction and conclusion. The latter is a red flag (see section 9).
- [I] **Documents as data under institutional theory:** examiners trained in institutional theory will press on the epistemic problem. Official documents are themselves the "formal structure" or "talk" that may be decoupled from action. A documents-only design therefore needs an explicit strategy for inferring practice or outcomes, for example triangulating across document genres (plans against audits, budget papers, parliamentary committee transcripts, procurement records, post-Games reports from comparator Games), using time-series of documents, or bounding claims to "decoupling as represented in the documentary record".

## 7. Internal milestone reviews (confirmation, mid-candidature, pre-submission or thesis review): what they assess and how committee reviewers audit readiness

### Takeaway
Australian universities run staged milestones to meet HESF 4.2's progress-monitoring requirement. **Confirmation** (about 12 months full-time) tests the viability, significance and feasibility of the project and the candidate's capability. **Mid-candidature** (about 24 months) tests progress, the emerging argument and contribution, and the publication and completion plan. **Pre-submission or thesis review** (about 3–6 months before submission) tests examination readiness against the examiner criteria. The panel normally includes at least one member independent of the supervisory team. Outcomes range from satisfactory, through satisfactory with conditions, to unsatisfactory, which leads to re-review and potentially termination.

### Cited Findings
- [T, verify per institution] Typical milestone structure, from training knowledge of UQ, Griffith, QUT, Monash and Sydney practice:
  - **Confirmation of Candidature (about 12 months FTE; earlier for part-time pro rata).** A written document (research questions; a literature review establishing the gap; a theoretical framework; methodology and methods; ethics plan or approval status; data management plan; timeline; budget; publication plan), an oral presentation or seminar, and a panel interview. Panel recommendations: confirm; confirm with conditions; extend probation and re-present; or downgrade or terminate.
  - **Mid-candidature review (about 18–24 months).** Progress against the plan, substantial draft chapters (e.g., the findings chapter), the developing contribution, and risk management.
  - **Pre-submission / Thesis Review / Final Seminar (about 3–6 months before submission).** A near-complete draft, a public seminar presenting findings and contribution, a panel judgement of readiness for examination, and often examiner nomination and conflict-of-interest checks at this point.
  - [U] UQ calls its milestones Confirmation, Mid-Candidature Review and Thesis Review. QUT's include Confirmation and a Final Seminar. Monash uses Confirmation, Mid-candidature and Pre-submission (final review). These labels are training-knowledge recall and are unverified.
- [T, verify] HESF 4.2 requires formal monitoring of each candidate's progress and support mechanisms. This is the regulatory basis for milestones (see section 3). The [ACGR Quality Graduate Research Supervision guideline](https://www.acgr.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/ACGR_GPG_Quality_Graduate_Research_Supervision.pdf) also addresses supervisory responsibilities in progress review. [V-S, existence only]
- [T] Trafford & Leshem's "starting at the end" logic, which designs early milestones around eventual examination questions, supports using examiner criteria at confirmation. [T] — Trafford, V., & Leshem, S. (2002). Starting at the end to undertake doctoral research: Predictable questions as stepping stones. *Higher Education Review*, 34(3), 31–49. [Crossref search](https://search.crossref.org/?q=Starting+at+the+end+to+undertake+doctoral+research+predictable+questions)

### Inferences
- [I] **Committee reviewer readiness audit (a suggested protocol for the persona):**
  1. **AQF alignment map:** each Doctoral Degree outcome (frontier knowledge; original contribution; research methods; critical reflection on theory; communication for external examination; capacity to plan and execute original research) mapped to the chapter or section that evidences it.
  2. **Contribution statement test:** can the candidate state the theoretical contribution in two or three sentences, using construct, mechanism and boundary language? Is the same statement used consistently in the abstract, introduction, discussion and conclusion?
  3. **Alignment chain:** research questions → theory (means–ends decoupling, defined against policy–practice decoupling) → document corpus and sampling logic → analytic procedure (codebook, audit trail) → findings → contribution. Any break in the chain gets flagged.
  4. **Literature currency and positioning:** institutional-theory canon and recent decoupling literature; mega-event governance (e.g., Müller 2015; Flyvbjerg et al. 2021); leverage and legacy (Chalip 2004; Preuss 2007); Brisbane 2032 grey literature.
  5. **Method defensibility:** corpus boundaries, document authenticity, credibility and representativeness, and the limits of inferring practice from documents (Bowen 2009). Also reflexivity, and ethics (often low-risk or exempt for public documents, but confirm the university's requirements).
  6. **Case-risk check:** Brisbane 2032 is ongoing and its governance has been restructured during preparation. The reviewer should check the data cut-off date, how later changes are handled, and whether claims are time-bounded.
  7. **Examiner anticipation:** a list of likely examiners (disciplinary mix) and the predictable questions each would ask, with answers located in the text.
  8. **Presentation hygiene:** consistency, referencing, figures, word limit (often 80,000–100,000 words for a PhD; [U] by institution), and the thesis-by-publication rules if relevant.
- [I] At **confirmation** the critical question is "is there a credible route to an original theoretical contribution?" Common conditions imposed include sharpening the research questions, specifying the decoupling construct, justifying a documents-only design, and defining the case boundaries. At **pre-submission**, it becomes "would two independent international examiners recommend pass with at most minor amendments?" If not, the panel should defer submission.

## 9. Skills the persona needs

### Takeaway
Two kinds of skill are needed. The first is evaluative craft: reading for coherence and contribution, calibrating against AQF and institutional criteria, and distinguishing fatal from fixable problems. The second is report craft: writing balanced, actionable, defensible reports and recommendations. Both rest on literacy in institutional theory, qualitative document-analysis methods, and mega-event and tourism scholarship.

### Cited Findings
- Examiners' core evaluative focus areas are coherence, literature command, methodological appropriateness, contribution, and presentation. [T] — [Mullins & Kiley 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/0307507022000011507); [Golding et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2013.859230); [Holbrook et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/03075070701346899)
- Report-writing involves managing evaluative language (praise and criticism pairing, hedging) and the roles of gatekeeper and teacher. [T] — [Starfield et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.linged.2015.06.004); [Kumar & Stracke 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeap.2011.06.001)
- Theory-contribution appraisal: Whetten's seven questions; Corley & Gioia's originality and utility; Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan's building and testing levels; construct clarity. [T] — [Whetten 1989](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1989.4308371); [Corley & Gioia 2011](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2009.0486); [Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan 2007](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.28165855); [Suddaby 2010](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.35.3.zok346)
- Process integrity: conflict-of-interest identification (the five-year co-publication rule at WSU; ACGR 2021 interests guidelines), confidentiality, and no contact with the candidate. [V-S] — [WSU 2026](https://westernsydney.edu.au/content/dam/digital/pdf/eds/schools/grs/GRS-HDR-Guide-for-Examiners-2026.pdf); [MIT 2026](https://www.mit.edu.au/sites/default/files/docs/publications/HDR%20Examination%20Policy%20and%20Procedure20260209.pdf); [ACT policy](https://myportal.actheology.edu.au/FileDownload/16088e7f-d08f-443a-8b92-807562d67f30/hdr-examination-policy)

### Inferences
- [I] Skill list for the persona:
  1. **Criterion mapping:** translating AQF Level 10 and the Doctoral descriptor and the university form into an audit rubric, with evidence locations.
  2. **Contribution extraction:** restating the claimed contribution precisely, classifying it (Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan level; incremental or revelatory), and testing it against the literature.
  3. **Alignment auditing:** checking the chain from research questions through theory, method, findings and claims.
  4. **Construct policing:** means–ends against policy–practice decoupling; decoupling against loose coupling, translation and "ceremonial conformity"; legacy against leverage; impact against legacy.
  5. **Qualitative-rigour appraisal for documentary research:** corpus logic, credibility and trustworthiness criteria, audit trail, reflexivity, and limits of inference.
  6. **Severity triage:** separating fatal (no contribution; fundamental design flaw; integrity breach) from major (unclear or overstated contribution; analysis not tied to theory) from minor (presentation, referencing, local clarity).
  7. **Calibrated recommendation:** choosing the category consistent with the narrative, given that most theses pass with amendments.
  8. **Report writing:** a structured, specific, page-referenced, constructive report that separates required from suggested changes and includes questions in lieu of a viva.
  9. **Independence and ethics of examining:** COI disclosure, confidentiality, and (increasingly) attention to generative-AI disclosure and research-integrity policy [U on 2026 specifics].
  10. **Milestone facilitation:** setting clear, time-bound conditions at confirmation and mid-candidature, and making a go or no-go call at pre-submission.

### Gaps
- No Australian examiner-training curriculum (e.g., ACGR or university examiner induction modules) was verified in this session.

## 10. Red flags that trigger major revisions ("revise and resubmit") or failure

### Takeaway
The most consistent triggers are: an absent, unclear or overstated original contribution; lack of coherence and alignment; a literature review that is uncritical or fails to position the contribution; methods that cannot support the claims; and analysis that stays descriptive and does not theorise. Pervasive presentation errors do not usually fail a thesis by themselves, but they compound doubts. Integrity breaches can be fatal regardless of quality.

### Cited Findings
- Poor theses lack coherence, show weak command of the literature or theory, use inappropriate methods, and are poorly presented. [T] — [Mullins & Kiley 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/0307507022000011507)
- Examiners treat the literature review as a test of command of the field and of the positioning of the contribution. [T] — [Holbrook et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/03075070701346899)
- Presentation errors irritate examiners and are read as signs of carelessness that cast doubt on rigour. [T] — [Golding et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2013.859230); [Johnston 1997 (Crossref search)](https://search.crossref.org/?q=Examining+the+examiners+an+analysis+of+examiners+reports+on+doctoral+theses)
- Doctorateness requires conceptual-level integration. Competent description without theorised conclusions falls short of the threshold. [T] — [Trafford & Leshem 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/14703290903069027); [Wellington 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2011.634901)
- Data, references, variable lists and diagrams are not theory. [T] — [Sutton & Staw 1995](https://doi.org/10.2307/2393788)

### Inferences
- [I] **Red flags specific to this thesis (likely triggers of major revision):**
  1. **"Decoupling" used as a label, not an analytic construct.** There is no definition distinguishing means–ends from policy–practice decoupling ([Bromley & Powell 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/19416520.2012.684462)), no coding frame, and no evidence of how means, ends and practices were identified.
  2. **Contribution equals application.** The thesis claims only that Brisbane 2032 "exhibits" decoupling (Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan building level 1–2), with no mechanism, boundary condition or reconceptualisation ([Whetten 1989](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1989.4308371); [Whetten 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1740-8784.2008.00132.x)).
  3. **Epistemic mismatch.** Claims about *practice* or *outcomes* are made from documents that are themselves institutional "talk" ([Meyer & Rowan 1977](https://doi.org/10.1086/226550); [Bowen 2009](https://doi.org/10.3316/QRJ0902027)), with no triangulation strategy or bounded claims.
  4. **Corpus opacity.** No inclusion or exclusion criteria, no document inventory, no account of missing or confidential documents (e.g., commercial-in-confidence venue contracts), and no audit trail.
  5. **Legacy and leverage conflation.** Ex-ante leverage strategies ([Chalip 2004](https://www.worldcat.org/search?q=Sport+tourism+interrelationships+impacts+and+issues+Ritchie+Adair)) are confused with ex-post legacy outcomes ([Preuss 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/14775080701736957)). Outcome claims are made before the Games have occurred.
  6. **Moving-target case.** No data cut-off date, and no handling of governance or venue-plan changes during candidature. Findings risk becoming outdated by submission.
  7. **Generalisation errors.** Statistical-style generalisation is drawn from a single case rather than analytic generalisation to theory ([Eisenhardt 1989](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1989.4308385); [Siggelkow 2007](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.24160882)).
  8. **Literature parochialism.** The thesis neglects either the organisation-theory decoupling literature or the mega-event governance, sport and tourism literature. It also lacks international comparators (e.g., London 2012, Tokyo 2020, Paris 2024, Los Angeles 2028).
  9. **Overclaiming revelatory status** without problematising assumptions ([Alvesson & Sandberg 2011](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2009.0188)), or presenting practical recommendations as the theoretical contribution ([Corley & Gioia 2011](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2009.0486)).
  10. **Inconsistent contribution statements** across the abstract, introduction and conclusion.
- [I] **Red flags that risk failure (rare):** no identifiable original contribution; fundamental design invalidity that cannot be repaired by rewriting; substantial plagiarism, fabrication or undisclosed or impermissible generative-AI authorship; and ethics breaches.
- [I] **Usually minor amendments:** typographical errors, referencing inconsistencies, figure quality, local clarity, small omissions in the literature, and limitations that need stating more fully.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence was retrieved on which deficiencies most often lead to "revise and resubmit" in Australian examinations. Bourke & Holbrook's and Holbrook et al.'s datasets may contain this, but they were not opened.

## 11. Interfaces with other personas in the 15-persona panel

### Takeaway
The Committee Reviewer/Examiner is the panel's **terminal quality gate**. It consumes outputs from the theory, methods, domain and writing personas and returns criterion-referenced verdicts. It should not become the supervisor or a co-author: it judges and specifies required changes, and others help fix them.

### Cited Findings
- The examiner's dual gatekeeper and teacher role, with formative comment, supports a design in which the reviewer's outputs feed developmental personas. [T] — [Holbrook et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2012.750289); [Kumar & Stracke 2018 (Crossref search)](https://search.crossref.org/?q=Reframing+doctoral+examination+as+teaching)
- Independence norms: supervisors cannot examine, and contact with examiners is restricted during examination. [V-S] — [ACT policy](https://myportal.actheology.edu.au/FileDownload/16088e7f-d08f-443a-8b92-807562d67f30/hdr-examination-policy); [UniSQ](https://policy.unisq.edu.au/documents/151767PL)

### Inferences
- [I] Suggested interfaces. Persona names are generic; map them onto the user's actual 15.
  - **Principal supervisor / doctoral-journey mentor:** receives milestone-panel conditions and translates them into a work plan. The Reviewer must stay independent and must not co-design solutions in examiner mode.
  - **Neo-institutional theorist (decoupling specialist):** the Reviewer asks for a construct definition, mechanism and boundary specification, and a positioning against Bromley & Powell (2012), Wijen (2014) and the critiques (Alvesson & Spicer 2019). The theorist supplies the content, and the Reviewer judges whether it meets the Colquitt & Zapata-Phelan level 3–5 and Whetten tests.
  - **Mega-event governance and Olympic studies expert:** supplies the empirical literature (Müller 2015; Flyvbjerg et al. 2021), comparators and Brisbane 2032 governance chronology. The Reviewer checks currency and the data cut-off.
  - **Tourism legacy and leverage expert:** checks the leverage and legacy constructs (Chalip; Preuss) and outcome-claim timing. The Reviewer flags conflation.
  - **Qualitative methodologist / document-analysis specialist:** supplies corpus design, coding and trustworthiness procedures. The Reviewer audits defensibility and the epistemic limits of inferring practice from documents.
  - **Literature-review / systematic-search specialist:** supplies coverage and positioning. The Reviewer tests critical synthesis and whether the gap was spotted or problematised.
  - **Research-integrity, ethics and data-governance advisor:** clarifies ethics requirements for public documents, generative-AI disclosure, and copyright of reproduced documents. The Reviewer treats breaches as potentially fatal.
  - **Academic writing and thesis-structure editor:** fixes presentation issues the Reviewer lists as minor, and makes contribution statements consistent across chapters.
  - **Policy and practice (Queensland or Brisbane 2032 government) stakeholder voice:** supplies practical utility. The Reviewer ensures practical recommendations do not substitute for the theoretical contribution.
  - **Devil's advocate / journal peer reviewer:** stress-tests claims at publication standard. The Reviewer harmonises journal-review criteria with thesis criteria (AQF Level 10 "significant and original contribution" rather than journal novelty thresholds).
  - **Thesis-by-publication advisor (if applicable):** checks integration across papers (Sharmini et al. 2015).
- [I] **Workflow:** at each milestone the Reviewer issues (a) an AQF and criteria audit table, (b) a contribution-classification statement, (c) a severity-ranked issue list with the owning persona, and (d) a provisional recommendation category ("if submitted today...").

### Gaps
- The user's actual list of the other 14 personas was not provided, so the interfaces above are generic [I].
