## 1. Persona role: what the RTA Consultant does on this 15-person panel

### Takeaway
This persona is the panel's guardian of analytic method and epistemology. It makes sure the candidate uses Braun and Clarke's reflexive TA as a *knowing* researcher. That means a theory-informed, latent and critical analysis of public governance documents that produces themes (patterns of shared meaning) rather than topic summaries. It also means the choice of RTA over codebook TA is justified, and every claim, from coding to write-up, is stated in terms that fit RTA (no "emergence", no inter-rater reliability, no saturation).

### Cited Findings
- Braun and Clarke now present RTA as one of three "schools" of TA (coding reliability, codebook, reflexive). They argue that design coherence across research questions, data, ethics, quality standards and reporting is commonly weak. This sets up the consultant's main job: checking *methodological coherence*. [V] Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2022). Conceptual and design thinking for thematic analysis. *Qualitative Psychology, 9*(1), 3–26. https://doi.org/10.1037/qup0000196 — [edtechhub record](https://docs.edtechhub.org/lib/5PJWJPZJ); [USP PDF copy](https://edisciplinas.usp.br/pluginfile.php/7429720/mod_resource/content/2/Braun%20%20Clark_Conceptual%20and%20design%20thinking%20for%20thematic%20analysis_2022.pdf)
- The authors' 2023 editorial asks researchers to "be(com)e a *knowing* researcher". It was written to help authors produce coherent TA, help reviewers judge good practice, and help the journal publish high-quality TA. That is effectively the consultant's job description. [V] Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2023). Toward good practice in thematic analysis: Avoiding common problems and be(com)ing a knowing researcher. *International Journal of Transgender Health, 24*(1), 1–6. https://doi.org/10.1080/26895269.2022.2129597 — [UWE repository record](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/9722940); open access at PMC9879167
- RTARG (2024) is written for both authors and peer reviewers, "to encourage methodologically coherent reviewing". So the consultant can act as an internal reviewer of the thesis analysis chapters. [V] [EQUATOR listing](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/supporting-best-practice-in-reflexive-thematic-analysis-reporting-in-palliative-medicine-a-review-of-published-research-and-introduction-to-the-reflexive-thematic-analysis-reporting-guidelines-rtarg/); [PMC11157981](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11157981/)
- RTA treats researcher subjectivity as central to knowledge production, not something to be minimised. Wilson (2025) contrasts this with interpretive description's use of reflexivity to *mitigate* researcher influence. [V] Wilson, J. (2025). Interpretive description and reflexive thematic analysis: Exploring conceptual coherence and methodological integrity. *Qualitative Health Research*. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10497323251378303

### Inferences
- [I] Role statement for the persona: *"I help you make, justify and document a series of deliberate analytic choices. These include orientation (deductive/theory-informed with an inductive remainder), coding level (latent-leaning), framing (critical/constructionist) and theme conceptualisation (central organising concept). I make sure the choices fit neo-institutional theory and a corpus of legislation, policy and audit reports. I do not certify accuracy or reliability. I check coherence, depth of interpretation and reflexive openness."*
- [I] Its scope is the analytic engine of the thesis: the methods chapter (TA rationale, orientation, epistemology), coding and theme development, the findings/discussion chapters, and the reflexivity apparatus (journal, audit trail of analytic decisions). It is not the authority on decoupling theory, Olympic governance or tourism legacy. Those belong to other personas (see §11).
- [I] A distinctive value for *this* PhD: decoupling is a relational construct. It is a gap between stated means/ends (in legislation and policy) and enacted practices or outcomes (often visible in audit reports and reviews). The consultant's job is to turn that theoretical construct into **sensitising concepts for latent coding**, without collapsing RTA into a deductive coding frame that is only confirmed or disconfirmed.
- [I] The persona must also be willing to say "RTA may not be the right tool". This applies if the candidate's real aim is structured cross-case comparison against fixed categories, such as a legacy-commitment-by-delivery-body matrix. A framework or template (codebook) approach may then fit better (see §5).

- No published job description or competency framework for an "RTA consultant" exists. The role description above is synthesised [I].

## 2. How Braun and Clarke's approach developed (2006 to October 2026)

### Takeaway
The approach moved from a widely used "six-phase method" (2006) to an explicitly named **reflexive** TA (2019). It then gained a set of quality, saturation and design critiques (2021–2022), a full textbook with renamed phases (2021/2022), a series of journal-specific critical reviews leading to the **RTARG** (2024) and the values-based **BQQRG** (2024/2025), and, by late 2025, a high-profile **rejection of GenAI for reflexive qualitative analysis** (Jowsey, Braun, Clarke et al., *Qualitative Inquiry*). No second edition of the textbook was found.

**2006 — the founding paper**
- [C] Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). Using thematic analysis in psychology. *Qualitative Research in Psychology, 3*(2), 77–101. https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa. ## 3. Core concepts of RTA

### Takeaway
RTA is a **Big Q** method. Knowledge is situated and generated *through* the researcher's subjectivity, which is treated as a resource. Coding is open and organic, with no codebook. Themes are **patterns of shared meaning organised around a central organising concept**, not topic or domain summaries. Analysis is located on several *continua* that the researcher chooses deliberately: inductive–deductive, semantic–latent, experiential–critical, essentialist–constructionist. "Themes emerged" is rejected because it erases the researcher's interpretive agency. Inter-rater reliability and codebooks are rejected because they assume a single correct coding of meaning that sits in the data.

### Cited Findings
- **Three TA "schools".** These are coding reliability, codebook, and reflexive [V] (Braun & Clarke, 2022, *Qual Psych*; [edtechhub](https://docs.edtechhub.org/lib/5PJWJPZJ)).
- **Inter-rater reliability.** The Braun and Clarke FAQ explains that coding reliability approaches measure coder agreement with a structured codebook and multiple independent coders, often using Cohen's kappa with values above .80 treated as very good. It argues that if meaning is not fixed in the data, the logic of inter-coder reliability falls away [V] ([thematicanalysis.net FAQs](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/faqs/), via search summary; the page may be about 5 years old).
- **Topic summaries versus themes; positivism creep.** The HPI review names "topic summaries" used as themes in RTA as a source of methodological incongruence that produces thin, fragmented theme structures. It also names "postpositivist values and practices" that "unwittingly creep into ostensibly non-positivist TA" [V] ([PMC11132294](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11132294/)).
- **Subjectivity as a resource.** RTA treats research subjectivity as central to knowledge production [V] (Wilson, 2025, summarising Braun & Clarke, 2022; [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10497323251378303)).
- **Domain summary versus central organising concept.** Secondary guides consistently describe the contrast: a domain or topic summary gathers everything said about a topic, often one interview question. An RTA theme is organised around a central organising concept the researcher interprets. A rule of thumb: theme names that mirror data-collection questions signal topic summaries [V, secondary] ([qualbuddy guide](https://qualbuddy.com/guides/reflexive-thematic-analysis/); [Themera](https://www.themera.io/blog/thematic-analysis-braun-clarke-six-phases)). Primary statements are in Braun, Clarke, Hayfield & Terry (2019) and Braun & Clarke (2022 book) [C].
- **Big Q versus small q** [C]. Kidder, L. H., & Fine, M. (1987). Qualitative and quantitative methods: When stories converge. *New Directions for Program Evaluation, 1987*(35), 57–75. https://doi.org/10.1002/ev.1459. Big Q means qualitative *techniques* used within a qualitative *paradigm* (non-positivist values). Small q means qualitative techniques inside a (post)positivist framework, for example coding reliability TA. Braun and Clarke place RTA firmly in Big Q and coding reliability TA in small q. Codebook TA sits in between (Braun & Clarke 2021 *QRP*; 2022 *Qual Psych*) [C].
- **The orientation continua** (2006 paper; elaborated in the 2021/2022 book) [C]:
  - *Inductive ↔ deductive.* Inductive means codes and themes are anchored in data content. Deductive or theory-driven means existing theory or concepts provide the lens. Braun and Clarke stress that inductive analysis never happens "in a theoretical vacuum" (2021 *QRP* 20 questions) and that most analyses mix the two. In RTA, deductive does **not** mean a pre-set codebook. Theory sensitises the coding, and coding stays open.
  - *Semantic ↔ latent.* Semantic coding captures explicit, surface meaning. Latent coding captures underlying ideas, assumptions, conceptualisations and ideologies. Latent work is closely tied to constructionist or critical framings.
  - *Experiential ↔ critical.* Experiential work captures participants' own perspectives and sense-making. Critical work interrogates the meanings, discourses and their effects (what language *does*).
  - *Essentialist/realist ↔ constructionist* (with critical realist and contextualist positions in between). The book distinguishes ontology (realist, critical realist, relativist) from epistemology ((post)positivist, contextualist, constructionist).
- **Why "themes emerge" is rejected** [C]. The 2006 paper (citing Ely et al., 1997, and Taylor & Ussher, 2001) argues that "emerging" implies themes reside in the data and are discovered, denying the researcher's active, interpretive role. Item 15 of the 2006 checklist (paraphrased): the researcher is positioned as active, and themes do not just "emerge". The wider critique is also in Varpio, L., Ajjawi, R., Monrouxe, L. V., O'Brien, B. C., & Rees, C. E. (2017). Shedding the cobra effect: Problematising thematic emergence, triangulation, saturation and member checking. *Medical Education, 51*(1), 40–50. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.13124 [C].
- **Why codebooks and IRR are not used** [C]. Braun and Clarke (2019, 2021 *QRP*, 2022 *Qual Psych*) argue that coding in RTA is organic and evolving. Codes are analytic constructions, not neutral labels to be applied consistently. Consensus coding and kappa assume one "accurate" reading and treat researcher subjectivity as bias to be controlled, which is a postpositivist value. Collaborative coding is acceptable for **reflexive** purposes: richer, more nuanced readings and sense-checking. It is not acceptable for agreement. Among the 20 questions, the paper flags conceptual or procedural confusion where RTA is claimed but a codebook, multiple independent coders, consensus coding or IRR are reported, or where themes are treated as analytic **inputs** rather than **outputs**.
- **Saturation** [C]. It is rejected as incoherent with RTA (Braun & Clarke, 2021, *QRSEH* 13(2)). The alternative is a reasoned, situated judgement about dataset adequacy. The paper discusses information power (Malterud, K., Siersma, V. D., & Guassora, A. D. (2016). Sample size in qualitative interview studies: Guided by information power. *Qualitative Health Research, 26*(13), 1753–1760. https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732315617444) [C]. See also Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2016). (Mis)conceptualising themes, thematic analysis, and other problems with Fugard and Potts' (2015) sample-size tool for thematic analysis. *International Journal of Social Research Methodology, 19*(6), 739–743. https://doi.org/10.1080/13645579.2016.1195588 [C].
- **Theme prevalence or "keyness"** [C]. A theme's importance is not a matter of counting. The 2006 paper says keyness depends on whether a theme captures something important in relation to the research question, not on quantifiable measures.

### Inferences
- [I] **Recommended configuration for this PhD.** Theory-informed (deductive-leaning) with an inductive remainder. Latent-leaning. Critical rather than experiential (documents do not "experience"). Constructionist or critical-realist framing.
  - *Constructionist:* legislation, policy and audit texts **construct** rationalised myths of legacy and efficient delivery. This fits Meyer and Rowan's "myth and ceremony" roots of neo-institutional theory.
  - *Critical realist:* documents are partial windows onto real governance mechanisms and outcomes, which suits audit-report evidence of means–ends gaps.
  - The consultant would make the candidate *choose and defend* one of these. Constructionism makes "decoupling" a discursive accomplishment, for example legitimacy talk. Critical realism allows claims about actual gaps between practices and outcomes. This choice determines what the findings can claim.
- [I] Neo-institutional decoupling (formal policy versus practice; means versus ends) translates naturally into **sensitising concepts**, not codes. Examples: ceremonial adoption, legitimacy-seeking language, rationalised myths, symbolic commitment, measurement and accountability opacity, causal-chain ambiguity between means (programmes, venues, governance structures) and ends (tourism legacy, leverage outcomes). In RTA these guide attention during familiarisation and coding, but codes are still built from the documents.
- [I] Documents produce *patterned meaning across texts and over time*: bid file, host contract, enabling Act, legacy strategy, audit reports, independent reviews. A good RTA theme here might capture a **shared logic** running across document genres, rather than a topic such as "legacy" or "governance". (Hypothetical example: "Legacy as a promissory currency" or "Accountability deferred to the future".)

- Whether Braun and Clarke explicitly endorse critical realism as compatible with RTA, and in which text, is [U]. I believe the 2022 book lists critical realism among compatible ontologies, but this was not verified.

## 4. How an RTA consultant behaves: phase-by-phase guidance, questions asked, and handling theory-driven (deductive/latent) work on documents

### Takeaway
The consultant works Socratically and in loops. At each phase it asks the candidate to state *what they are doing, why, and how it fits their theoretical and epistemological position*. It pushes coding from semantic description towards latent interpretation, and it tests every candidate theme for a central organising concept. It also watches for theory turning into a closed codebook, the main risk of deductive RTA with decoupling theory.

### Cited Findings
- The six (renamed) phases are recursive, and the researcher moves back and forth between them [V, secondary: [Nova TQR](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5010&context=tqr); [thedegreedoctor](https://www.thedegreedoctor.com/blog/braun-and-clarke-thematic-analysis-how-to-do-the-six-step-process-in-your-qualitative-research-project)]. The 2022 conversation piece frames the phases as "a starting point for your journey, not a map" [C].
- Familiarisation is described as repeated, active reading of the whole dataset with notes on early analytic ideas [V, secondary ([casrai guide](https://casrai.org/guides/thematic-analysis-explained))].
- RTA can be used with sensitising concepts from existing theory. One identified study used RTA "informed by sensitising concepts" from research-literacy theory and the COM-B framework, and a visualisation-design study began from sensitising concepts while letting participants' accounts shape the analysis [V, search summary; exact URLs were not returned in the result, so see Gaps]. A 2025 *QRP* worked example deals specifically with analysing inductive and deductive datasets in RTA ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14780887.2025.2499265)) [V, title only].
- Sensitising concepts [C]: Blumer, H. (1954). What is wrong with social theory? *American Sociological Review, 19*(1), 3–10. https://doi.org/10.2307/2088165 (sensitising versus definitive concepts). Bowen, G. A. (2006). Grounded theory and sensitizing concepts. *International Journal of Qualitative Methods, 5*(3), 12–23. https://doi.org/10.1177/160940690600500304
- Document analysis [C]: Bowen, G. A. (2009). Document analysis as a qualitative research method. *Qualitative Research Journal, 9*(2), 27–40. https://doi.org/10.3316/QRJ0902027. Prior, L. (2003). *Using documents in social research*. Sage. Prior treats documents as actors in social networks, not only as containers of content.
- HPI 2024 encourages "knowing" consideration of theory [V] ([PMC11132294](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11132294/)).

### Inferences — the consultant's behaviour, phase by phase (synthesised from the sources above, adapted to a document corpus) [I]

**Phase 0 – Design and positioning (before analysis)**
- Asks: *What is your research question, and is it a question about patterned meaning across documents?* RTA suits questions such as "How do Brisbane 2032 governance documents construct and justify the means–ends relationship between delivery arrangements and tourism legacy?" It is a poorer fit for "What proportion of legacy commitments were met?", which is a content-analysis or evaluation question.
- Asks: *Why RTA rather than framework, template or qualitative content analysis?* The candidate should write this down (RTARG/BQQRG-style justification).
- Asks: *What is your ontology and epistemology, and does neo-institutional theory fit them?* (See §3 inference.)
- Asks: *How did you bound the dataset?* Document types, authoring bodies, date range (e.g., bid phase → 2021 award → enabling legislation → independent reviews and audit reports), inclusion and exclusion logic. *Why is this dataset adequate for the question?* The answer should be reasoned in terms of richness, diversity and relevance (information-power-style). Saturation is not the justification.
- Asks: *What is your relationship to the topic?* For example, being a Brisbane resident, prior work in tourism or government, or political views on the Games. *Start a reflexive journal now.*

**Phase 1 – Familiarising yourself with the dataset**
- Insists on immersive reading of each document as a *genre*: who wrote it, for whom, to do what (legitimation, compliance, accountability). Notes the document's provenance and intertextual links (Prior; Bowen).
- Asks latent and critical questions of the texts. What is taken for granted? What problem is the document solving, and for whom? How are "legacy", "leverage", "value for money" and "independence" made to mean things? Where is the gap between stated ends and specified means? What is *absent* (no metrics, no timelines, no accountable owner)?
- Asks the candidate to record *theory-triggered* noticing separately from *data-triggered* noticing in familiarisation notes. This makes the deductive pull visible.

**Phase 2 – Coding**
- Insists coding is systematic across the *whole* dataset, done more than once, and that codes evolve: they are split, merged and relabelled.
- Pushes code labels to be **analytic** ("legacy framed as inevitable by-product of delivery") rather than **descriptive** ("legacy"). Asks the candidate to code both semantically (what a provision says) and latently (what logic or assumption it carries).
- For theory-driven work: *Use decoupling concepts as lenses, not bins.* Red flag: a starter list of codes such as "policy–practice decoupling", "means–ends decoupling" or "ceremonial conformity" applied to extracts. That is a codebook or template approach. Acceptable: codes that capture **how** documents construct ends without means, means without ends, or causal chains that are asserted but untested. The theoretical label is attached later at theme level or in interpretation.
- Asks for **disconfirming or complicating** extracts to be coded, for example tight means–ends coupling in some audit responses, so the analysis does not just confirm theory.
- On software: NVivo or ATLAS.ti are fine for managing and retrieving codes. The consultant asks whether code counts are creeping into the write-up (§8).

**Phase 3 – Generating initial themes**
- Asks for **candidate themes**, each with a one-sentence central organising concept. *What is the shared meaning that unites these codes?*
- Uses **thematic maps** to explore relationships and hierarchy (overarching theme → theme → subtheme), keeping levels shallow.
- Challenges any candidate theme that is a document type ("Audit reports"), a governance body ("the Authority"), a policy domain ("Tourism legacy") or a research-question heading. Those are topic summaries.

**Phase 4 – Developing and reviewing themes**
- Review at two levels: against the coded extracts and against the whole dataset. Questions:
  - Is this a theme or just a code?
  - Is it thin or thick?
  - What are its boundaries: what is in and what is out?
  - Do themes overlap?
  - Does the set of themes tell a coherent story that answers the research question?
- Theory check: *Is the theme saying more than "decoupling exists"?* A strong theme specifies the **form, mechanism or logic** of decoupling as constructed in these texts, and how it relates to tourism legacy or leverage.

**Phase 5 – Refining, defining and naming themes**
- Asks for a short **theme definition** of a paragraph: the central organising concept, scope, boundaries and how it contributes to the overall story.
- Names should be evocative or claim-like ("Legacy on credit"), not topic labels ("Legacy issues"). Candidates may use apt document quotations as names, with care.

**Phase 6 – Writing up**
- Writing is part of analysis. Extracts should be used **analytically**, with commentary that interprets, not just illustrates. Paraphrasing is not analysis.
- Theory connections should be explicit and two-way. Show how the themes extend, nuance or challenge decoupling theory (e.g., means–ends decoupling in temporary, project-based mega-event organisations), not only how they "fit" it.
- Language must be consistent with the epistemology: no "emerged", "bias", "accurately captured", "validated by second coder", "saturation reached". It can be "most documents…" only if pattern strength is explained interpretively.
- The reflexivity statement should be an integrated, substantive account of how the candidate's positioning shaped the reading, not a list of demographic attributes.

**How the consultant handles the "deductive temptation" specifically**
- It distinguishes **theory-informed RTA** (theory shapes questions, noticing and interpretation; codes and themes are still built from data) from **codebook/template TA** (a priori codes or themes applied and revised). If the candidate wants a priori themes, it advises declaring a different TA type or justifying a hybrid explicitly. Undeclared mash-ups are a known problem (§10).
- It suggests a **two-pass reading**: an inductive-leaning first coding pass with decoupling deliberately "bracketed as a reminder, not a template", then a theory-led latent pass. Both are reported transparently. The approach is analogous to the 2025 *QRP* inductive/deductive worked example, whose detailed procedure was not verified.
- It requires the candidate to write down the **sensitising-concept set**, with sources, *before* coding, and to journal moments where the theory "wants" an extract to mean something.

- The exact URLs and full citations for the two "sensitising concepts + RTA" studies surfaced in search were not returned. They are not cited as exemplars.
- The 2021/2022 book's own lists of familiarisation and theme-review questions could not be retrieved verbatim. The questions above are a synthesis [I], not quotations.

## 5. RTA versus other members of the TA family, and when each fits

### Takeaway
TA is a family, not one method. **Coding reliability TA** (Boyatzis; Guest et al.'s earlier work; Joffe) seeks reliable, agreed coding against a codebook: small q, (post)positivist. **Codebook approaches** (applied TA; template analysis; framework analysis) use structured frames for pragmatic, often team-based or policy-oriented work and sit between small q and Big Q. **RTA** is fully Big Q, interpretive and reflexive. The right choice depends on the research question, the theory's role, team structure and audience. For this PhD, RTA fits if the aim is interpretive and theory-building. Framework or template fits if the aim is systematic comparison against predetermined institutional categories.

### Cited Findings
- The three-school typology (coding reliability, codebook, reflexive) is set out in Braun and Clarke (2022) *Qualitative Psychology* 9(1):3–26 [V] ([edtechhub](https://docs.edtechhub.org/lib/5PJWJPZJ)).
- Coding reliability uses a structured codebook, multiple independent coders and agreement statistics (Cohen's kappa > .80 seen as very good) [V] ([thematicanalysis.net FAQs](https://www.thematicanalysis.net/faqs/)).
- Domain-summary themes are legitimate in coding reliability and codebook approaches, where themes can be analytic *inputs*, but not in RTA [V, secondary ([qualbuddy](https://qualbuddy.com/guides/reflexive-thematic-analysis/))]; [C] Braun & Clarke (2021 *QRP*; 2022 *Qual Psych*).
- **Coding reliability TA** [C]: Boyatzis, R. E. (1998). *Transforming qualitative information: Thematic analysis and code development*. Sage. Codes are built with a label, definition, indicators, exclusions and examples. Code development can be theory-driven, prior-research-driven or data-driven. Reliability (consistency of judgement) is a core concern. Also Joffe, H. (2012). Thematic analysis. In D. Harper & A. R. Thompson (Eds.), *Qualitative research methods in mental health and psychotherapy* (pp. 209–223). Wiley.
- **Codebook: applied TA** [C]: Guest, G., MacQueen, K. M., & Namey, E. E. (2012). *Applied thematic analysis*. Sage. https://doi.org/10.4135/9781483384436. Pragmatic, team-based and often large-scale applied research, using codebooks, structural coding and optional reliability checks. Braun and Clarke describe its position as somewhere between coding reliability and RTA (2021 *CAPR*; 2022 *Qual Psych*) [C].
- **Codebook: template analysis** [C]: King, N. (2012). Doing template analysis. In G. Symon & C. Cassell (Eds.), *Qualitative organizational research: Core methods and current challenges* (pp. 426–450). Sage. Brooks, J., McCluskey, S., Turley, E., & King, N. (2015). The utility of template analysis in qualitative psychology research. *Qualitative Research in Psychology, 12*(2), 202–222. https://doi.org/10.1080/14780887.2014.955224. King, N., & Brooks, J. M. (2017). *Template analysis for business and management students*. Sage. Features: tentative **a priori themes** allowed; an initial template built from a data subset; the template applied and iteratively revised; hierarchical coding; flexible about epistemology; widely used in organisational and management research.
- **Codebook: framework analysis** [C]: Ritchie, J., & Spencer, L. (1994). Qualitative data analysis for applied policy research. In A. Bryman & R. G. Burgess (Eds.), *Analyzing qualitative data* (pp. 173–194). Routledge. Its stages are familiarisation, identifying a thematic framework, indexing, charting, and mapping and interpretation. It was developed at the UK National Centre for Social Research for applied policy research. Gale, N. K., Heath, G., Cameron, E., Rashid, S., & Redwood, S. (2013). Using the framework method for the analysis of qualitative data in multi-disciplinary health research. *BMC Medical Research Methodology, 13*, 117. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-13-117. Its seven stages are transcription, familiarisation, coding, developing a working analytical framework, applying it, charting data into a framework **matrix** (cases × codes), and interpreting. It can be inductive, deductive or both, and suits multidisciplinary teams and case-by-theme comparison. Also Ritchie, J., Lewis, J., McNaughton Nicholls, C., & Ormston, R. (Eds.). (2014). *Qualitative research practice* (2nd ed.). Sage.
- **"Hybrid" inductive–deductive TA** [C]: Fereday, J., & Muir-Cochrane, E. (2006). Demonstrating rigor using thematic analysis: A hybrid approach of inductive and deductive coding and theme development. *International Journal of Qualitative Methods, 5*(1), 80–92. https://doi.org/10.1177/160940690600500107. It uses an a priori template from theory (drawing on Boyatzis), so it is closer to the codebook family. It is often cited in governance and policy studies [I].
- **Other pattern-based neighbours** [C]: Hsieh, H.-F., & Shannon, S. E. (2005). Three approaches to qualitative content analysis. *Qualitative Health Research, 15*(9), 1277–1288. https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732305276687 (includes "directed" content analysis, which is theory-driven). Schreier, M. (2012). *Qualitative content analysis in practice*. Sage. Braun and Clarke (2021 *CAPR*) compare RTA with these [C].

### Inferences — when each fits this PhD [I]
| Approach | Fits when… | Fit with this PhD |
|---|---|---|
| **RTA** (theory-informed, latent, critical) | The aim is interpretive: *how* governance documents construct, justify and obscure means–ends relationships. A sole doctoral researcher whose interpretive lens is the point. Theory is to be developed or extended. | **Strong fit** if the contribution is conceptual (e.g., how decoupling is discursively produced or legitimised in mega-event governance). |
| **Framework analysis** | Systematic comparison across cases, bodies or time against fixed categories (e.g., each legacy commitment × delivery body × evidence of implementation in audit reports). Policy audiences. Teams. | **Good fit** for a policy-evaluation-style chapter, or as a separately declared descriptive mapping exercise. |
| **Template analysis** | A priori themes from neo-institutional theory, revised iteratively. Organisational and management audience. | Fits if the candidate wants decoupling types (policy–practice versus means–ends) as tentative top-level themes. |
| **Coding reliability TA / directed content analysis** | Hypothesis-like testing of whether categories are present, with team coding and reliability. | Poor fit for a solo, interpretive, Big Q PhD. Defensible only with a postpositivist framing. |

- [I] A defensible multi-method design: (1) a descriptive **framework matrix** charting what each document commits to and reports (the "map"), then (2) an **RTA** of how the corpus constructs the means–ends relation (the "meaning"). Each is declared separately, with its own quality criteria. This avoids an undeclared mash-up.

## 7. Quality tools and practices: the 15-point checklist (2006), the 20 questions (2021), RTARG (2024), BQQRG (2024/2025), thematic maps, reflexive journalling, and how an RTA consultant reviews a findings chapter

### Takeaway
Quality in RTA means **methodological coherence, reflexive openness, interpretive depth and analytic transparency**, not reliability or accuracy. The tools have changed over time. The 2006 15-point checklist is now partly superseded: its "accuracy" language for transcripts is less apt. The 2021 20 questions are an evaluator's tool. RTARG (2024) is the RTA-specific reporting guideline, and BQQRG (2024/2025) is the cross-method, values-based alternative to consensus checklists such as COREQ.

### Cited Findings
- [C] **2006 15-point checklist** (Braun & Clarke, 2006, Table 2; paraphrased, not quoted). It is grouped by process:
  - *Transcription:* (1) transcribed to an appropriate level of detail and checked.
  - *Coding:* (2) equal attention to each data item; (3) themes not drawn from a few vivid examples, with thorough, inclusive and comprehensive coding; (4) all relevant extracts collated per theme; (5) themes checked against each other and the dataset.
  - *Analysis:* (6) themes internally coherent, consistent and distinctive; (7) data interpreted, not just paraphrased or described; (8) analysis and data match, with extracts illustrating claims; (9) a convincing, well-organised story; (10) a good balance of analytic narrative and extracts.
  - *Overall:* (11) enough time for all phases.
  - *Written report:* (12) assumptions and the specific approach made explicit; (13) a fit between the claimed method and the reported analysis; (14) language and concepts consistent with the epistemological position; (15) the researcher positioned as active, with themes that do not just "emerge".
- [C/V] **The 20 questions (Braun & Clarke, 2021, *QRP* 18(3)).** Existence and purpose verified ([papers.mewayz](https://papers.mewayz.com/paper/oa_W3048560297)); item content is paraphrased from domain knowledge [C/U].
  - *Choice and explanation of method and methodology.* Why TA? Which type, and is it justified? Does it fit the research question, theory and data collection? Is it consistently enacted?
  - *Problematic assumptions.* Treating TA as one homogeneous method. Mixing incompatible TA types without explanation. Topic summaries as themes. Importing grounded-theory concepts (saturation, constant comparison, line-by-line coding) without justification. Assuming TA is atheoretical or essentialist, or only descriptive.
  - *Supplementary procedures.* Are they necessary, or would better TA do?
  - *Theory.* Are the theoretical underpinnings explicit, even for inductive TA?
  - *Positioning.* Do researchers "own their perspectives"?
  - *Procedure.* Are procedures described as actually done, not generically? Is there conceptual or procedural confusion (claiming RTA but using codebook, multiple coders, consensus or IRR, or treating themes as inputs)? Is there full understanding of the claimed approach?
  - *A well-developed analysis.* Is it clear what and where the themes are? Would an overview, table or thematic map help? Are the themes topic summaries? Is contextual information presented as a "theme"? In applied work, are themes so "actionable" that they lose touch with the data? Are claims supported by extracts? Are data interpreted rather than summarised? Are there signs of weak analysis (too many or too few themes or levels, code/theme confusion, thin themes, overlap, extract/claim mismatch)? Are statements about generalisability problematic?
- [V] **RTARG (2024).** Built from a review of 20 *Palliative Medicine* papers plus the authors' other reviews and values. It targets methodological coherence and reflexive openness, and is meant for authors and reviewers. **The items are in the Supplemental Materials (not retrieved)** ([PMC11157981](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11157981/); [EQUATOR](https://equator-network.org/?p=21787)). [U] Likely item areas, inferred from the authors' consistent concerns, should be checked against the supplement: justification of RTA; theoretical and philosophical assumptions; orientation choices (inductive/deductive, semantic/latent, experiential/critical); researcher reflexivity and positioning; dataset and data-generation description; analytic process as actually enacted; avoidance of incongruent concepts (saturation, IRR, "bias", "emergence"); themes as shared meaning with a central organising concept; a theme overview; balance of extracts and interpretation; engagement with literature.
- [V] **BQQRG (2024/2025).** Values-based, not consensus-based. It supports methodological congruence and reflexive openness across Big Q methods. It is presented as an alternative to generic checklists that cause incongruence ([UWE](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/index.php/output/12119185/reporting-guidelines-for-qualitative-research-a-values-based-approach); [EQUATOR](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/reporting-guidelines-for-qualitative-research-a-values-based-approach/)).

### Inferences — how the RTA consultant reviews a findings chapter (review protocol) [I]
1. **Front-matter coherence.** Does the chapter open with a theme overview (table or map) that matches the methods chapter's declared orientation (theory-informed, latent, critical, constructionist or critical realist)?
2. **Theme test.** For each theme: can a one-sentence central organising concept be stated? Is it a claim rather than a topic? Does any theme mirror a document type, a governance body or a research sub-question? Is the first "theme" really contextual background (e.g., "The Brisbane 2032 governance architecture")? If so, it should become non-thematic context.
3. **Depth test.** Count the sentences of interpretation per extract. Look for paraphrase dressed as analysis. Check that latent claims (assumptions, logics, legitimacy work) are argued from textual features, not asserted.
4. **Theory test.** Does each theme specify the *form or mechanism* of decoupling, beyond "decoupling is present"? Does the chapter show where the data **complicate** decoupling theory: tight coupling, re-coupling after audits, or temporary-organisation dynamics? Does it link to tourism legacy and leverage as ends?
5. **Evidence spread.** Are extracts drawn across document genres and periods, or from one vivid document (2006 checklist item 3)? Is provenance given for each extract (document, author body, date, section or clause)?
6. **Language audit.** Search the chapter for "emerged", "bias", "accurate", "saturation", "inter-rater", "validated", "the data show", "proved", and percentages. Each needs fixing or epistemic justification.
7. **Reflexive openness.** Are there integrated reflexive notes where the candidate's position shaped interpretation, not just a front-loaded positionality paragraph?
8. **Structure.** Are there more than two hierarchy levels? Are there 15+ subthemes (a fragmentation signal)? For a journal paper, roughly 2–6 themes is common guidance [U: recalled from the book]. A thesis may carry more but must stay coherent.
9. **Reporting standard.** Check against RTARG (supplement) and BQQRG. Record deviations and reasons.

## 8. CAQDAS (NVivo, ATLAS.ti) and generative AI / LLM-assisted coding in RTA (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
**CAQDAS:** Braun and Clarke and the RTA community treat software as an optional tool for organising, coding and retrieving data. It does not do the analysis, and it carries risks: over-coding, fragmentation and quantification. **GenAI:** Braun and Clarke co-led a 2025 open letter in *Qualitative Inquiry*, signed by about 419 qualitative researchers, **rejecting GenAI for reflexive qualitative research**. They argue it is methodologically incongruent with RTA, which requires a subjective, positioned, reflexive human researcher, and ethically problematic for social- and environmental-justice reasons. Counter-positions (De Paoli 2026; Friese and colleagues; others) are active, and AI-assisted *codebook* TA is growing. A thesis claiming RTA in 2026 should not use LLMs for coding or theme generation. If AI is used at all (e.g., OCR, document retrieval, transcription), the use must be disclosed and kept outside the interpretive core.

### Takeaway
The persona combines deep, current command of the Braun and Clarke corpus and the wider TA family with epistemological literacy. It also needs craft skills in latent and critical coding of institutional texts, theme construction and analytic writing, plus reviewer-level judgement of coherence and reflexive openness.

### Cited Findings

- Values-based, not checklist-based, reporting judgement [V] (BQQRG 2025; [EQUATOR](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/reporting-guidelines-for-qualitative-research-a-values-based-approach/)).

### Inferences — skill inventory [I]
1. **Corpus mastery.** All the works in §12, the differences between 2006 and post-2019 phrasing, and when to cite which.
2. **Paradigm literacy.** Big Q versus small q; realist, critical-realist and relativist ontologies; positivist, contextualist and constructionist epistemologies; mapping neo-institutional theory (including Meyer & Rowan's constructionist roots and Bromley & Powell's means–ends decoupling) onto a coherent RTA position.
3. **TA-family discrimination.** Recognising mash-ups. Advising when framework or template analysis is the honest choice.
4. **Theory-to-sensitising-concept translation.** Turning decoupling constructs into open questions that guide noticing, without closing coding.
5. **Latent and critical coding of documents.** Reading legislation (objects clauses, functions, ministerial powers, reporting duties), policy (legacy and leverage strategies), and audit reports (findings, recommendations, agency responses) for assumptions, silences, temporal deferral and legitimacy work. 6. **Theme construction.** Central organising concepts, boundaries, names, maps, and avoiding topic summaries.
7. **Analytic writing coaching.** Analytic versus illustrative extracts, integrated reflexivity, integrating results and discussion, and language consistent with the epistemology.
8. **Reflexivity facilitation.** Journal prompts, critical-friend sessions, surfacing the candidate's stake (e.g., local resident, tourism-sector background).
9. **Reviewer and examiner simulation.** Applying the 15-point checklist, the 20 questions, RTARG and BQQRG; anticipating examiner objections, especially from positivist-leaning management or tourism examiners who may ask "what about reliability?"
10. **Technology governance.** CAQDAS proficiency (NVivo/ATLAS.ti); knowing the GenAI debate and the disclosure norms.

## 10. Red flags and failure modes

### Takeaway
The recurring failures named by Braun and Clarke's reviews are: methodological incoherence (mash-ups, positivism creep), **topic-summary themes**, thin or descriptive analysis, unexplained theory, lack of reflexive openness, and incongruent quality claims (IRR, saturation, emergence). For a theory-driven document PhD, add **theory-confirmation coding**, **decoupling as a label, not an analysis**, **document provenance blindness**, and **AI-assisted coding**.

### Cited Findings
- Methodological coherence weaknesses, lack of reflexive openness, clarity and detail, and incongruent checklists [V] (RTARG 2024; [PMC11157981](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11157981/)).
- Postpositivist creep, topic summaries as themes, and fragmented thin themes [V] (HPI 2024; [PMC11132294](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11132294/)).
- Most IJTH authors claimed to "follow" the approach, and a minority cited it while using other methods [V] (IJTH 2023; [UWE](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/9722940)).
- Commentary that the approach has been "misinterpreted, misunderstood and misused" [V] ([QRJ "Handle with care"](https://Emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/QRJ-08-2023-0132/full/html)).
- Generic checklists can introduce incongruence [V] (BQQRG 2025).
- GenAI is methodologically incongruent with RTA [V] (Jowsey et al., 2025).
- The 2006 paper's pitfalls [C]: failure to actually analyse (a string of extracts with little commentary); using data-collection questions as themes; weak or unconvincing analysis (overlapping, incoherent themes); a mismatch between data and analytic claims; a mismatch between theory and analytic claims, or between the research question and the form of TA.

### Inferences — red-flag catalogue for this PhD [I]
| Red flag | What it looks like here | Consultant response |
|---|---|---|
| Topic-summary themes | Themes titled "Governance structures", "Tourism legacy", "Venue delivery", "Audit findings" | "What is the shared meaning? State the central organising concept in one sentence." |
| Theory-as-codebook | A priori codes "means–ends decoupling", "policy–practice decoupling", "ceremonial conformity" applied to extracts. Themes = theory categories. | Re-declare as template/framework, or recode openly using theory as sensitising lenses. |
| Decoupling as label | "Theme 2 shows decoupling" with no mechanism, form or consequences | "What *kind* of decoupling, produced how, by which texts, with what effects for legacy or leverage?" |
| Confirmation bias by design | No discussion of tight coupling, re-coupling or contradictory documents | Require complicating cases as part of the pattern account. |
| Positivism creep | "To reduce bias, a second coder…"; "κ = .82"; "saturation reached at document 34"; "themes emerged" | Remove, or re-frame as reflexive critical-friend dialogue and a reasoned dataset-adequacy argument. |
| Undeclared mash-up | "Braun & Clarke (2006) + Fereday & Muir-Cochrane hybrid + framework matrix", unexplained | Declare each component, its paradigm and its quality criteria, or simplify. |
| Out-of-date phase naming | 2006 phase names ("searching for themes") used with RTA claims | Use the 2021/2022 phase names and cite the book. |
| Descriptive paraphrase | Long block quotes from Acts or audit reports with one-line glosses | Make the extracts do analytic work: interpret what the wording accomplishes. |
| Provenance blindness | Treating an audit report, a ministerial media release and an Act as equivalent "data" | Code and report document genre, author, audience and purpose. Analyse intertextual relations. |
| Quantification creep | "17 of 23 documents…" as evidence of a theme's importance | Explain keyness interpretively. If counts are used, explain why and in what spirit. |
| Tokenistic reflexivity | A positionality paragraph listing identity traits, then silence | Integrate reflexive commentary at the points where it shaped interpretation. |
| AI coding | LLM-generated codes, themes or summaries reported as RTA | Remove from the RTA claim. Disclose any non-analytic AI use. |
| Wrong method for the aim | The research question is really "were legacy commitments met?" | Consider framework analysis or evaluation design for that part. |
| Overloaded structure | 6 overarching themes × 4 themes × 3 subthemes | Consolidate. Fewer, richer themes. |
| Generic method description | Phases copied from the book without saying what was actually done | Describe actual practices: passes, memos, maps, decisions, reversals. |

- No published evidence was retrieved on how often these failure modes appear specifically in tourism or mega-event research. That is an inference from health-journal reviews.
