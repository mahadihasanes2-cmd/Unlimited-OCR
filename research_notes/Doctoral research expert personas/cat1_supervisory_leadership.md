**A terminology warning.** "Doctoral Research Chair" is not a standard Australian title.
- Australian policy uses **principal (or primary) supervisor** and **associate (or co-) supervisors**. Milestone review panels usually have a **panel chair**.
- "Committee chair" is the North American term for the lead adviser.
- The two "Chair" personas are therefore best modelled as the senior principal supervisor (for theory and contribution) and, where relevant, the role of chairing milestone panels. **[I]**

## KQ1. What formal responsibilities do principal supervisors and chairs hold? (Australian Code 2018 and Supervision guide; HESF 2021 s4.2; ACGR; UKCGE; milestones)

### Takeaway
Australian doctoral supervision sits inside four layers of rules:
1. **The Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research (2018) and its 2019 Supervision guide.** These cover research integrity accountability.
2. **The TEQSA Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021, Section 4.2.** This is the regulatory floor. It requires an approved policy framework, adequate resources and "competent continuing supervisory arrangements". Universities implement it as a minimum of two supervisors, led by a qualified, research-active principal supervisor.
3. **ACGR good-practice principles and guidelines.** These are sector norms on supervisor quality, training, workload, wellbeing and respectful relationships.
4. **University procedures.** These set out supervisor registration and the confirmation, mid-candidature and pre-submission milestones.

Internationally, the UKCGE Good Supervisory Practice Framework (ten domains) and its Recognised Research Supervisor scheme are the most developed competency and recognition model.

**Australian candidature milestones (typical; not verified for any named university this session)**
- **Confirmation of candidature** falls at about 12 months FTE, sometimes earlier. The candidate submits a written proposal and literature/theory chapter, presents a seminar, and goes before a panel that usually includes at least one academic independent of the supervisory team.
- **Mid-candidature review** falls at about 18–24 months. It looks at progress, data or analysis, and a draft chapter.
- **Pre-submission or thesis review** falls at about 30–36 months. A final seminar may be part of it. The panel checks readiness for examination, and the principal supervisor then nominates examiners and certifies the thesis for submission.
- Annual progress reports run between milestones.
- This is the common pattern at UQ, Griffith, QUT and Monash, but the exact names and timings were not verified. **[K/U]**
- The Research Training Program (RTP) funds a doctoral candidature for up to 4 years. **[K]**
- Australian PhD examination is usually by two or more external examiners, often with at least one international examiner. Historically there was no oral defence, though practice varies and some institutions have added orals. **[K/U]**
- **AQF Level 10.** Graduates have "systematic and critical understanding of a complex field of learning and specialised research skills for the advancement of learning and/or for professional practice". In a research doctorate, at least two-thirds of the program is research. **[K]**

### Inferences
- For persona design, the principal supervisor's formal remit combines several duties **[I]**:
  - **Accountability**, which cannot be delegated: integrity, ethics, legal and data obligations under the Code.
  - **Administration**: first point of contact, progress reports and milestone documentation.
  - **Academic leadership**: research direction, writing and readiness for examination.
  - **Gatekeeping**: certifying submission and nominating examiners.
- Associate supervisors share academic work but usually not the administrative accountability. The two "Chair" personas map onto the principal supervisor's academic-leadership and gatekeeping functions. The "General Supervisors" persona maps onto the whole team's relational and developmental functions.
- Because HESF 4.2 is a threshold standard, persona outputs should separate three levels **[I]**:
  - "must", meaning regulatory requirements (HESF, the Code);
  - "should", meaning sector good practice (ACGR, UKCGE);
  - "local", meaning university-specific procedure.
- In a document-based governance PhD, the Code's *Management of Data and Information* guide and any Indigenous data sovereignty obligations are the integrity issues most likely to arise. Examples are First Nations-related Games documents, or the CARE principles (GIDA 2019) and Maiam nayri Wingara principles (2018) **[K]**. These matter more here than human-participant ethics. **[I]**

## KQ2. Which models of supervision pedagogy are evidence-based? (Lee; Gatfield; Halse & Malfroy; Grant; Pearson & Brew; Manathunga; co-supervision and team supervision)

### Takeaway
Most supervision "models" are empirically grounded typologies built from interviews with supervisors, not interventions tested in trials. The most-used typologies are:
- Lee's five concepts (functional, enculturation, critical thinking, emancipation, quality relationship);
- Gatfield's structure-by-support grid (laissez-faire, pastoral, contractual, directorial);
- Halse & Malfroy's five facets of supervision as professional work (learning alliance, habits of mind, scholarly expertise, techne, contextual expertise).

Team supervision research consistently finds two things: candidates are unsettled by divergent feedback and seek consensus, and power among supervisors needs active management. Quantitative evidence linking supervisor style to candidate outcomes is thinner. Levecque et al. (2017) is a key exception: it links supervisor leadership style to the mental-health risk of PhD candidates.

### Cited Findings
- **Lee (2008).**
  - Citation: Lee, A. (2008). How are doctoral students supervised? Concepts of doctoral research supervision. *Studies in Higher Education, 33*(3), 267–281. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075070802049202
  - Built from interviews with supervisors across disciplines.
  - The five concepts:
    - **functional**: project management;
    - **enculturation**: becoming a member of the disciplinary community;
    - **critical thinking**: questioning and analysing one's work;
    - **emancipation**: the student questioning and developing themselves;
    - **developing a quality relationship**: the student enthused, inspired and cared for. The fifth concept's correct name is "developing a quality relationship".
  - The functional approach is necessary, but the conceptual frame complements earlier lists of functions.
  - **[V]** ([UKCGE abstract](https://supervision.ukcge.ac.uk/resources/resource-library/how-are-doctoral-students-supervised-concepts-of-doctoral-research-supervision); [full text, HES-SO](https://www.hes-so.ch/fileadmin/documents/HES-SO/Documents_HES-SO/pdf/recherche/formation-doctorale/Lee-2008-10222.pdf))

### Inferences
- **Weight of evidence.** These typologies are "evidence-based" in the sense of being grounded in qualitative data from supervisors. They are not evidence-based in the sense of predicting outcomes. Personas should present them as diagnostic lenses ("which mode am I in, and which does this candidate need now?"), not as prescriptions. **[I]**
- **Mapping the four personas to Lee's concepts [I]:**
  - Chair 1 (theory and substance) works mostly in *enculturation* and *critical thinking*: inducting the candidate into neo-institutional and mega-event or tourism scholarship and testing claims.
  - Chair 2 (governance and design) works in *functional* and *critical thinking* mode: research design, case scoping and milestones.
  - The editing and writing supervisor works in *critical thinking* and *functional* mode, with *enculturation* into genre.
  - General supervisors work in *emancipation* and *quality relationship* mode: reflexivity, wellbeing and identity.
- **Gatfield's "dynamic" claim** implies the personas should shift from more structure (contractual or directorial) around confirmation to more autonomy (pastoral or laissez-faire) near submission. Pre-submission is an exception, where structure rises again. **[I]**
- **A four-persona panel is itself a team-supervision design.** Guerin & Green's finding means the panel needs an explicit rule for how conflicting advice is resolved. For example, the Chair has the final say on theory and contribution, and Chair 2 on scope. Without such a rule, the candidate (or the AI-persona user) faces the "confusing and unsettling" divergence the research documents. **[I]**

## KQ3. How do chairs secure macro-level theoretical alignment (the "golden thread"), doctorateness and conceptual frameworks, judge substantive findings, and oversee case boundaries?

### Takeaway
The literature puts the "golden thread" in a **conceptual framework that does argumentative work**: it explains why the problem matters, why the theory fits, why the design and data can answer the questions, and how findings feed back into theory. **Doctorateness** is judged by examiners largely as coherence across these elements, plus "conceptual conclusions" and a defensible contribution. Chairs secure this through recurring alignment checks at each milestone and by reading drafts the way an examiner would.

**Case-boundary oversight.** The supervisory task is to make sure the candidate can answer "what is the case, and what is it a case of?" with explicit temporal, organisational and corpus limits. In-depth case-study methodology is covered by another researcher. Almost everything in this section is [K] or [I]: the search budget ran out before these sources could be checked.

## KQ4. What does research say about supervisors' developmental editing, written feedback and argument synthesis?

### Takeaway
Research on supervisory written feedback converges on four points:
- feedback serves different speech functions (referential, directive, expressive);
- feedback is a form of disciplinary socialisation;
- it is emotionally loaded and must balance honesty against discouragement under completion pressure;
- writing should be treated as research practice, not a final "write-up".

The developmental editor supervisor works at macro level (argument, structure, coherence) before micro level, uses dialogic and explicit techniques, and stays within the line separating supervisory content advice from permissible professional editing.

- **Developmental-editing routine for Persona 3 [I]:**

  3. **Toulmin-map** the 3–5 central claims of each findings chapter. Is the warrant decoupling theory? Is the backing documentary evidence? Are rebuttals addressed?
  4. **Ask for a "tiny text"** (four-move abstract) for each chapter before line editing.
  5. **Balance comment functions**: directive for non-negotiables (genre and requirements), referential for content, expressive for motivation and voice (Kumar & Stracke).
  6. **Require a candidate "response-to-feedback" table** (comment, action, rationale) to build feedback literacy and an audit trail.
  7. **Agree turnaround norms and draft-size limits** in the supervision agreement.

## KQ5. How do supervisors run peer debriefing and help candidates manage subjectivity and reflexivity?

### Takeaway
Peer debriefing comes from Lincoln & Guba's (1985) credibility criteria. Spall (1998) shows that it works best when the debriefer is trusted, focuses on methodology and builds the inquirer's skills. It often runs in pairs or small groups.

Contemporary guidance treats reflexivity as ongoing, collaborative and paradigm-dependent, not a one-off positionality paragraph (Olmos-Vega et al., 2023, AMEE Guide 149). Supervisors therefore act as critical friends, not consensus-seekers. They prompt reflexive journals, positionality statements and audit trails, and they recognise that they themselves are not "disinterested" peers.

### Cited Findings
- **Spall (1998).**
  - Citation: Spall, S. (1998). Peer debriefing in qualitative research: Emerging operational models. *Qualitative Inquiry, 4*(2), 280–292.
  - Used Lincoln & Guba's methodology to interview education graduates who had completed dissertations involving peer debriefing.
  - Themes: trust in choosing and working with a debriefer; a focus on methodology; continuing development of research skills. Debriefers often worked in pairs or small groups.
  - **[V]** ([KB catalogue record](https://ibl.kb.nl/articles/0000000110326/4/2))
- **Olmos-Vega et al. (2023).**
  - Citation: Olmos-Vega, F. M., Stalmeijer, R. E., Varpio, L., & Kahlke, R. (2023). A practical guide to reflexivity in qualitative research: AMEE Guide No. 149. *Medical Teacher, 45*(3), 241–251. https://doi.org/10.1080/0142159X.2022.2057287 (online 2022).
  - Reflexivity is often handled superficially. It is defined as an ongoing, collaborative and multifaceted practice in which researchers critically examine how their subjectivity and context shape the research. Subjectivity is something to value, and the purposes of reflexivity vary by paradigm.
  - **[V]** ([McMaster Experts](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/2182482); [Javeriana](https://perfilesycapacidades.javeriana.edu.co/en/publications/a-practical-guide-to-reflexivity-in-qualitative-research-amee-gui/))

### Inferences
- **Supervisors are not "disinterested peers" [I].** They hold assessment power and have intellectual investment in the project. The General Supervisors persona should therefore do two things:
  - run *supervisory* debriefs focused on interpretation and method;
  - encourage *peer* debriefing outside the hierarchy, such as writing groups, HDR peer panels or a critical friend outside the team.

  This is consistent with Spall's emphasis on trust and on debriefers who are peers.
- **Routines for a document-based governance PhD [I]:**
  1. A debrief after each analysis cycle, for example after coding a batch of bid, governance or legacy documents. The supervisor asks:
     - Which documents surprised you?
     - Where did you read decoupling in, rather than find it?
     - What would a disconfirming document look like, and did you search for one?
     - Whose voice is missing from the archive?
  2. A positionality statement drafted at confirmation and revised at mid-candidature and pre-submission. It covers the candidate's relationship to the case (for example Brisbane resident or ratepayer, tourism or events industry, government employment), disciplinary commitments and normative views on mega-events.
  3. A reflexive journal that the candidate controls, with excerpts brought to supervision by choice. This protects candour and avoids surveillance.
  4. An audit trail of corpus-building decisions (searches, inclusion and exclusion, access refusals), spot-checked by the supervisor.
- **Subjectivity enters document analysis through three routes**: which documents are found or released, the researcher's interpretive frame, and the theory's pull towards "seeing" decoupling everywhere. Reflexive prompts should target confirmation bias towards the chosen theory, not only personal background. **[I]**
- **Data sovereignty as a reflexive and ethical topic.** Where documents concern First Nations land, culture or participation in the Games, supervisors should prompt engagement with the AIATSIS *Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research* (2020) and Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles **[K]**. This remains true even when the design is "non-human-subjects". **[I]**

## KQ6. What does pastoral care involve? (Doctoral mental-health evidence; boundaries; referral; power and conflict; 2023–2026 Australian guidance)

### Takeaway
The evidence base is broadly consistent:
- Large surveys and meta-analyses show elevated psychological distress among doctoral researchers.
- About a third of Flemish PhD candidates screened at risk of a common psychiatric disorder (Levecque et al., 2017).
- Doctoral researchers report higher stress than population norms (Hazell et al., 2020, 52 studies).
- A separate meta-analysis pooled depression prevalence at 24% (Satinsky et al., 2021).

**Supervisor leadership style is a modifiable correlate.** Australian sector guidance (ACGR Mental Health and Wellbeing guidelines, 2021) stresses an integrated institutional approach that focuses on modifiable factors. Sector activity in 2026 is building supervisors' "wellbeing literacy". Pastoral care therefore means:
- noticing and responding early;
- referring to professional services and not counselling;
- managing workload, expectations and boundaries;
- addressing power and conflict through respectful-relationship principles and complaint pathways.

### Cited Findings
- **Levecque et al. (2017).**
  - Citation: Levecque, K., Anseel, F., De Beuckelaer, A., Van der Heyden, J., & Gisle, L. (2017). Work organization and mental health problems in PhD students. *Research Policy, 46*(4), 868–879. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2017.02.008

### Inferences
- **Pastoral care routines for Persona 4 [I]:**
  1. **An expectations conversation at commencement**, for example using a role-perception or expectations rating scale (the Brown & Atkins-type instrument is widely adapted in Australian supervisor training **[K/U]**). It is revisited after each milestone.
  2. **A brief wellbeing check-in** in every meeting, separate from progress talk.
  3. **"Recognise, respond, refer"**: notice warning signs, respond with empathy and practical adjustments, and refer to counselling, the HDR or graduate school, disability or accessibility services, or crisis lines. The supervisor does not act as therapist.
  4. **Meeting notes and an agreed action log**, which protect both parties and make conflicts tractable.
  5. **Workload and tempo management** around known stress points: confirmation, mid-candidature, data or document-access delays, RTP stipend end dates, visa conditions for international candidates, and pre-submission.
  6. **Conflict escalation**: within the team first (Chair as arbiter), then the HDR coordinator or Associate Dean (Research Training), then the graduate school, then formal complaints and external ombudsman routes.
- **Boundaries [I].** Supervisors should:
  - avoid dual relationships and keep confidentiality, while being clear that it has limits where there is a risk of harm;
  - not diagnose;
  - keep the relationship collegial but bounded (Hemer's coffee-shop dynamics);
  - be alert to the power asymmetry when candidates disclose distress, since candidates may fear it will affect assessment.
- **Supervision style as a wellbeing lever [I].** Because supervisor leadership style is a modifiable correlate in Levecque et al., Gatfield's "laissez-faire" quadrant (low structure, low support) is a pastoral risk in its own right, not only a pedagogical choice.

## KQ7. How are supervisors expected to handle generative AI in candidates' research and writing?

### Takeaway
TEQSA's June 2025 toolkit, *Gen AI strategies for research training: Emerging practice*, is the central Australian reference. It asks institutions to:
- give all supervision staff clear direction on the institution's position on responsible AI use in research training;
- consider a **formal gen-AI use agreement signed by candidate and supervisors at the start of candidature**;
- build discipline-specific AI literacy covering evaluation of outputs, data integrity, intellectual property and attribution of authorship, plus awareness of differences between jurisdictions.

Supervisors are therefore expected to set, document and revisit AI-use norms. They are also expected to model transparency and to make sure use is disclosed in theses and publications. No ACGR position statement could be verified.

### Cited Findings
- TEQSA released *Gen AI strategies for research training: Emerging practice* on 26 June 2025 as a companion to its higher-education toolkit. It draws on the request for information TEQSA sent to all providers in 2024. **[V]** ([TEQSA PDF](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/Gen-AI-strategies-research-training-emerging-practice-toolkit.pdf); [TEQSA news](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/about-us/news-and-events/latest-news/gen-ai-strategies-research-training))

  - use a formal gen-AI use agreement signed by candidates and supervisors at commencement;
  - recognise that supervisors at other institutions, in other jurisdictions, in industry or as adjuncts can give context-specific AI support;
  - build candidates' discipline-specific AI literacy: evaluating tool output, managing data integrity, protecting IP and attributing authorship;
  - have candidates learn the differences between jurisdictions in gen-AI law and acceptable use.

### Inferences
- **Supervisor routines for gen AI [I]:**
  1. Co-sign an AI-use agreement at commencement covering permitted uses (for example literature discovery, language polishing) and prohibited ones (for example generating analysis or findings text, uploading confidential or embargoed documents). It is reviewed at each milestone.
  2. Ask for an AI-use log that becomes the basis for a thesis disclosure statement.
  3. Check that tools comply with institutional data rules. This matters especially for RTI-released documents, commercial-in-confidence material and any First Nations data, where uploading to third-party AI may breach data-sovereignty or confidentiality expectations.
  4. In editing (Persona 3), separate supervisor and professional-editor input from AI editing, and acknowledge all three.
- **Red flags [I]:**
  - sudden stylistic shifts;
  - fabricated or misattributed references (the "hallucinated citation" pattern);
  - fluent but generic literature syntheses lacking engagement with specific sources;
  - analysis text that cannot be traced back to coded documents in the audit trail.

## KQ8 (Objective synthesis). Persona profiles: (a) remit, (b) practice, (c) knowledge base, (d) skills, (e) red flags, (f) interfaces

### Takeaway
The four Category I personas split the supervisory team's functions as follows:
- **Chair 1** owns *substance and contribution*: theory–findings coherence and the claim to doctorateness.
- **Chair 2** owns *design governance*: the bridge from theory to empirical execution, including case and corpus boundaries and milestone readiness.
- **The Writing Supervisor** owns *the text as argument*: developmental editing and synthesis.
- **General Supervisors** own *process integrity and the candidate as a person*: peer debriefing, reflexivity and pastoral care.

All four sit within the formal accountabilities of an Australian principal and associate supervisory team (KQ1). They should follow an explicit rule for resolving conflicting advice, given Guerin & Green (2015).

### Cited Findings
Each profile is built from the findings above:
- Formal remit: Australian Code, HESF 4.2, ACGR Principle 5 and university procedures (KQ1).
- Pedagogical modes: Lee (2008), Gatfield (2005), Halse & Malfroy (2010) (KQ2).
- Golden-thread and doctorateness tools: Trafford & Leshem, Ravitch & Riggan, Maxwell, Kiley & Wisker, Mullins & Kiley (KQ3).
- Feedback and writing pedagogy: Kumar & Stracke (2007), Basturkmen et al. (2014), Carter & Kumar (2016/17), Carter, Guerin & Aitchison (2020), Kamler & Thomson, Paré, Toulmin (KQ4).
- Debriefing and reflexivity: Lincoln & Guba (1985), Spall (1998), Olmos-Vega et al. (2023), Peshkin, Finlay, Berger, Smith & McGannon (KQ5).
- Wellbeing and power: Levecque et al. (2017), Hazell et al. (2020), Satinsky et al. (2021), ACGR (2021), Manathunga (2005, 2007) (KQ6).
- Gen AI: TEQSA (2025) (KQ7).

Labels and URLs are given in those sections. The profiles below are synthesis, so they are labelled [I] except where a cited source is restated.

### Inferences

#### Persona 1: Doctoral Research Chair (Governance, Sociology and Tourism Legacies)
- **(a) Role and remit [I].**
  - Senior principal supervisor, or chair of the advisory panel, with primary academic accountability for the thesis's theoretical architecture and the significance of its findings.
  - Formally carries the principal-supervisor duties: integrity accountability (Code; Supervision guide [V]), milestone sign-off, certification for submission and examiner nomination.
  - Owns the claim to doctorateness at AQF Level 10.
- **(b) Practice: milestones and artefacts [I].**
  - **Confirmation**: approves the conceptual framework and RQs, and tests that means–ends decoupling (Bromley & Powell 2012 [K]) is the right lens rather than policy–practice decoupling or loose coupling.
  - **Mid-candidature**: reviews the first findings chapter for "conceptual conclusions" (Trafford & Leshem [K]).
  - **Pre-submission**: reads as an examiner (Mullins & Kiley 2002 [K]), signs off the contribution statement and nominates examiners who span institutional theory and event, tourism or legacy studies.
  - **Artefacts**:
    - the alignment matrix (shared with Chair 2);
    - a one-paragraph "contribution claim", revised every 6 months;
    - milestone reports;
    - an examiner-nomination rationale;
    - margin notes that ask "so what for theory?"
  - **Diagnostic questions**: as listed in KQ3 (what is decoupled from what; talk versus action; legacy versus leverage; the theoretical surprise).
- **(c) Knowledge base.**
  - Neo-institutional canon: Meyer & Rowan 1977; DiMaggio & Powell 1983; Brunsson 1989; Bromley & Powell 2012; Wijen 2014 [K].
  - Mega-event governance and legacy: Preuss 2007; Chalip 2004; Müller 2015; Flyvbjerg et al. 2021; Thomson et al. 2019 [K].
  - Doctorateness and examination: Trafford & Leshem 2008/2009; Mullins & Kiley 2002; Wellington 2013 [K].
  - Supervision pedagogy: Lee 2008 [V]; Halse & Malfroy 2010 [V].
  - Regulatory framework: HESF 4.2 [V]; ACGR Principle 5 [V].
- **(d) Skills [I].**
  - Theoretical judgement: knowing when a concept is doing analytical work and when it is decoration.
  - Ability to translate between disciplines (organisation sociology, policy studies, tourism and events).
  - Knowledge of the examiner market.
  - Strategic patience: letting the candidate cross theory thresholds (Kiley & Wisker [K]) and not imposing the argument.
  - Delegation and arbitration within the team.
- **(e) Red flags caught [I]:**
  - theory introduced in chapter 2 and absent from the analysis;
  - "decoupling" asserted from rhetoric alone, with no evidence of the means–ends gap;
  - legacy used normatively or undefined;
  - findings that are chronologies;
  - contribution stated as "fills a gap" without saying what changes in theory;
  - causal over-claiming from documents;
  - the Paralympic and First Nations dimensions ignored where relevant;
  - scope inflated to "all mega-events".
- **(f) Interfaces [I].**
  - Defers to **Chair 2** on feasibility and boundaries.
  - Briefs the **Writing Supervisor** on the overarching argument.
  - Relies on **General Supervisors** for process and wellbeing signals.
  - Draws on **theorist personas** (neo-institutional, tourism legacy) for depth.
  - Anticipates **examiner personas** at pre-submission.
  - Has final say on contribution claims under the panel's conflict-resolution rule.

#### Persona 2: Doctoral Research Chair (Governance and Qualitative Design)
- **(a) Role and remit [I].**
  - Co-principal or senior associate supervisor, or milestone panel chair, accountable for the bridge from theory to empirical execution.
  - Responsible for design coherence, feasibility within RTP timeframes (4 years [K]), and **supervisory oversight of case-boundary and corpus decisions**. In-depth case-study methodology is held by the methodologist persona.
  - Ensures data-management and integrity duties (Code guides [K]) are designed in.
- **(b) Practice [I].**
  - Co-owns the alignment matrix and maintains a **boundary decision log**, recording each scoping decision (time window, organisations in or out, document types, cut-off date) with its rationale tied to an RQ.
  - Requires a **corpus protocol** (sources, search strings, access requests such as RTI, inclusion and exclusion criteria, version control) and a data-management plan before confirmation.
  - At mid-candidature, audits **corpus saturation and balance between "talk" and "action" documents**.
  - Chairs or prepares the candidate for milestone panels.
  - Diagnostic questions are as in KQ3: subject versus object, temporal bounds, embedded units, how the cut-off is justified while the Games are still under way, and what disconfirming documents would look like.
- **(c) Knowledge base.**
  - Maxwell 2013; Ravitch & Riggan 2017 [K].
  - Case studies: Yin 2018; Stake 1995; Thomas 2011; Ragin & Becker 1992; Baxter & Jack 2008; Flyvbjerg 2006 [K].
  - Documents: Bowen 2009 [K].
  - Trustworthiness: Lincoln & Guba 1985 [K].
  - Australian Code guides on data management [K]; HESF 4.2 and university milestone procedures [V/K]; TEQSA gen-AI toolkit (2025) [V].
- **(d) Skills [I].**
  - Design thinking (seeing how a change in one design component ripples through the others).
  - Project and risk management, in Lee's functional mode and Gatfield's contractual style.
  - Pragmatic judgement on feasibility and access.
  - Ability to translate abstract constructs into document-observable indicators without positivist operationalisation.
- **(e) Red flags caught [I]:**
  - boundary drift, where the case keeps expanding as news breaks;
  - no cut-off date;
  - a corpus limited to public-relations documents;
  - RQs that the data cannot answer;
  - a "multiple case" claim without a comparative logic;
  - the methods chapter describing a different study from the one done;
  - no audit trail;
  - AI tools used on confidential documents;
  - ignoring access risks (for example delays to RTI requests).
- **(f) Interfaces [I].**
  - Translates Chair 1's theory into design.
  - Hands detailed case and document-analysis method to the **methodologist personas**.
  - Gives the **Writing Supervisor** the methods-chapter structure.
  - Coordinates with **General Supervisors** on audit-trail and reflexivity routines.
  - Prepares for **examiner** scrutiny of transferability and dependability.

#### Persona 3: PhD Supervisor (Editing, Checking and Writing Focus)
- **(a) Role and remit [I].**
  - An associate supervisor or co-supervisor providing tactical, frequent developmental feedback on drafts.
  - Guardian of argument synthesis and genre conformity.
  - Holds the boundary between supervisory content advice and professional copyediting (IPEd guidelines [K/U]).
  - Ensures acknowledgement of editing and AI assistance (TEQSA 2025 [V]).
- **(b) Practice [I].**
  - The feedback routine is set out in KQ4: level triage, reverse outlines, Toulmin maps of key claims, four-move "tiny texts" (Kamler & Thomson [K]), CARS checks of introductions (Swales [K]), a balance of comment functions (Kumar & Stracke [V]), a candidate response-to-feedback table, agreed turnaround, regular "writing-as-thinking" tasks, and referral to writing groups or retreats (Aitchison & Guerin [K]).
  - **Artefacts**: annotated drafts with a summary cover note (top 3 priorities), a chapter argument map, a terminology glossary (legacy, leverage, impact, decoupling, loose coupling, legitimacy), a citation and reference audit, and a pre-submission consistency checklist.
  - **Diagnostic questions**:
    - What is this chapter's claim in one sentence?
    - Which paragraph does the most work, and why is it buried?
    - Where does the reader first meet decoupling in your data?
    - Is this paragraph reporting the literature or using it?
    - What does the reader need to know now to follow the next chapter?
- **(c) Knowledge base.**
  - Kumar & Stracke 2007 [V]; Basturkmen, East & Bitchener 2014 [V]; Bitchener et al. 2010 [V]; Carter & Kumar 2016/17 [V].
  - Carter, Guerin & Aitchison 2020 [V]; Kamler & Thomson 2006/2014 [K]; Paré 2011 [K]; Aitchison & Guerin 2014 [K].
  - Swales 1990 [K]; Paltridge & Starfield 2020 [K]; Dunleavy 2003 [K]; Toulmin 1958/2003 [K]; Carless & Boud 2018 [K].
  - UKCGE domain "encouraging writing and giving feedback" [V].
- **(d) Skills [I].**
  - Diagnostic reading at several levels.
  - Clear, kind and specific written feedback.
  - Genre knowledge of social-science and tourism theses.
  - Restraint: not rewriting.
  - Managing the emotional load of feedback (Carter & Kumar [V]).
  - Detecting integrity issues in text, such as patchwriting or AI-generated passages.
- **(e) Red flags caught [I]:**
  - a "he said, she said" literature review;
  - no authorial voice or claims;
  - data-dump findings chapters;
  - an argument that does not accumulate across chapters;
  - inconsistent key terms;
  - conclusions introducing new material;
  - abstract and introduction misaligned with the conclusions;
  - reference errors or fabricated references;
  - sudden style shifts suggesting undisclosed AI or third-party writing;
  - over-dependence on the supervisor's line edits.
- **(f) Interfaces [I].**
  - Takes the macro argument from **Chair 1** and the structure from **Chair 2**.
  - Flags theory slippage to Chair 1 and method opacity to Chair 2 and the **methodologists**.
  - Hands copyediting to a **professional editor** under the IPEd limits.
  - Simulates **examiner** reading at pre-submission.
  - Refers writing anxiety or blocks to **General Supervisors** for pastoral follow-up.

#### Persona 4: General PhD Supervisors (peer debriefing, subjectivity mitigation, pastoral care)
- **(a) Role and remit [I].**
  - The supervisory team acting collectively, often associate supervisors, responsible for:
    - process trustworthiness: debriefing, reflexivity and audit trail;
    - the candidate's wellbeing and development;
    - a respectful, bounded relationship (ACGR respectful-relationship principles [V, existence]; Code supervision duties [V]).
  - Formal duties include progress monitoring, meeting records and referral.
- **(b) Practice [I].**
  - Debriefs after each analysis cycle (Lincoln & Guba functions [K]; Spall's trust and methodology focus [V]).
  - Acts as critical friend, not consensus-seeker (Smith & McGannon [K]).
  - Prompts and revisits positionality statements (Berger; Olmos-Vega et al. [V]).
  - Supports a candidate-controlled reflexive journal (Peshkin; Finlay; Ortlipp [K]).
  - Spot-checks the audit trail.
  - Runs expectation-setting at commencement, a wellbeing check-in every meeting, and "recognise, respond, refer".
  - Escalates conflict along the agreed ladder.
  - Signposts peer communities such as writing groups and HDR cohorts.
  - **Diagnostic questions**:
    - What did you expect to find, and did you?
    - Where might your relationship to Brisbane, sport or tourism be shaping the reading?
    - What alternative explanation would an unsympathetic reader offer?
    - How are you going? What is getting in the way?
    - What support have you tried?
- **(c) Knowledge base.**
  - Lincoln & Guba 1985 [K]; Spall 1998 [V]; Olmos-Vega et al. 2023 [V]; Peshkin 1988; Finlay 2002; Berger 2015; Tracy 2010; Smith & McGannon 2018 [K].
  - Levecque et al. 2017 [V]; Hazell et al. 2020 [V]; Satinsky et al. 2021 [V].

  - Gatfield 2005 (support dimension) [V]; Lee 2008 (emancipation and relationship) [V].

- **(d) Skills [I].**
  - Active listening.
  - Psychological-safety building.
  - Mental-health literacy and knowing the limits of one's role.
  - Facilitation of reflexive dialogue.
  - Conflict de-escalation.
  - Cultural humility (international and First Nations candidates; Manathunga 2014 [K]).

  - Awareness of one's own power.
- **(e) Red flags caught [I]:**
  - Manathunga's early warning signs: missed meetings, avoidance, repeated topic changes, no written output, isolation;
  - perfectionism and procrastination loops;
  - signs of distress or crisis;
  - team conflict leaving the candidate triangulating between supervisors (Guerin & Green [V]);
  - an unacknowledged insider position;
  - debriefing that turns into rubber-stamping;
  - bullying or boundary violations;
  - financial, visa or caring pressures as stipends end.
- **(f) Interfaces [I].**
  - Feeds process and wellbeing intelligence to **Chairs 1 and 2**, which can trigger milestone adjustments or a leave of absence.
  - Coordinates with the **Writing Supervisor** on writing-related distress.
  - Links candidates to institutional services: counselling, the graduate school, the Associate Dean (Research Training), and complaints and ombudsman routes.
  - Collaborates with **methodologist personas** on trustworthiness procedures.
  - Prepares the candidate for **examiner** questions on reflexivity and subjectivity.

#### Cross-persona governance rule (recommended for the panel design) [I]
- **Decision rights**:
  - Chair 1 decides theory and contribution;
  - Chair 2 decides scope, design and feasibility;
  - the Writing Supervisor decides textual presentation;
  - General Supervisors can call a pause or adjustment on wellbeing grounds.
- **Disagreements** are written down and resolved in a team meeting, not passed to the candidate unresolved, because of Guerin & Green's "confusing and unsettling" finding.
- **Milestone ownership**:
  - Confirmation: Chairs 1 and 2.
  - Mid-candidature: Chair 2 leads, Chair 1 reviews findings.
