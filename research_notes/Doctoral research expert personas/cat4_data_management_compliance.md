## 1. Persona role: who is the Research Consultant (Data Management and Compliance) and what is in scope?

### Takeaway
This persona combines a research data steward, a CAQDAS project architect and a compliance reviewer. They make sure the document corpus and the NVivo/ATLAS.ti project are traceable, reproducible, secure, lawfully obtained and used, and governed in line with Indigenous data sovereignty. Their benchmarks are the Australian Code (2018), the NHMRC data guide (2019), the National Statement (2025 edition, in force from 23 June 2026), Queensland privacy, RTI and records law, and the CARE, Maiam nayri Wingara and AIATSIS frameworks.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The Australian Code's guide *Management of Data and Information in Research* supports the Code's principles, especially rigour and transparency. It splits duties into institutional responsibilities (training, ownership and stewardship, storage and retention, safety and confidentiality, access, facilities) and researcher responsibilities. — [NHMRC guide PDF](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/Management-of-Data-and-Information-in-Research.pdf)
- **[V-sec]** The Code sets out 29 responsibilities: R1–R13 for institutions and R14–R29 for researchers. Check the numbering against the Code itself. — [CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/australian-code-responsible-conduct-research); [Code PDF](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/grant%20documents/The-australian-code-for-the-responsible-conduct-of-research-2018.pdf)
- **[V-sec]** Researchers must "retain clear, accurate, secure and complete records" of research, including data and primary materials, and allow access where possible and appropriate (R22). A data management plan is "strongly encouraged" from the outset. — [UNSW climate-cms wiki summary of the Code](http://climate-cms.wikis.unsw.edu.au/Australian_Code_for_the_Responsible_Conduct_of_Research)
- **[V]** UQ researchers act under the Code and the University's Responsible Research Management Framework Policy. UQ points them to a library guide and a data management system. — [UQ Research Support](https://research-support.uq.edu.au/resources-and-support/ethics-integrity-and-compliance/research-integrity/management-data-and-information-research)
- **[V]** The ARDC describes "doing data right" as a joint responsibility of researchers and institutions, framed around FAIR. — [ARDC Good Data Practices](https://ardc.edu.au/resource/good-data-practices/)

### Inferences
- **[I]** For this PhD the persona's scope is: (1) designing and maintaining the DMP; (2) the document register and provenance chain (URL, retrieval date, archived copy, checksum, licence, access route such as public, RTI or FOI); (3) CAQDAS project architecture (cases, classifications and attributes, code hierarchy, memo system, sets, backups, exports); (4) governing generative-AI use inside CAQDAS; (5) compliance reviews against ethics, privacy, RTI/FOI, copyright, records retention and Indigenous data sovereignty; (6) planning for thesis deposit, embargo and data archiving.
- **[I]** The persona does not decide methodology, which belongs to the supervisors and the RTA and document-analysis personas, and does not give legal advice, which belongs to university Legal or the Privacy Office. They act as a gatekeeper and auditor, and they escalate.
- **[I]** In practice the role sits where the university Library (RDM and eResearch), Research Ethics and Integrity, the Graduate School, IT Security and the Indigenous research or engagement unit meet.

### Gaps
- I could not verify a published position description for a "research data consultant" at a Queensland university. The persona is a composite built from the frameworks above.

### Inferences
- **[I]** For a solo, document-based PhD, desktop NVivo 15.6 or ATLAS.ti 26 with local storage on university-managed infrastructure is the lowest-risk set-up. ## 3. How do generative-AI features in NVivo, ATLAS.ti (and MAXQDA) process data, and what does that mean for data sovereignty and university policy?

### Takeaway
All three vendors send prompts and selected data to external LLM infrastructure. NVivo desktop and ATLAS.ti use OpenAI under enterprise or zero-retention terms, and ATLAS.ti has an EU DPA with SCCs. MAXQDA uses Amazon Bedrock in the EU. NVivo AI Cloud runs on Azure and lists an Australian region. All three say customer data is not used for training. None of the desktop AI tools I found documents Australian-only processing. For any sensitive, personal or Indigenous material, that means offshore processing that needs institutional approval, ethics disclosure and documentation in the DMP.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Lumivero's privacy policy says some products use third-party AI providers. Prompts, files and submitted content may go to the provider "only to generate the requested output", and Lumivero does not use inputs for training. If a user independently configures a third-party provider, that provider's handling falls under the user's own agreement with it. — [Lumivero Global Privacy Policy](https://lumivero.com/legal-information/global-privacy-policy/)
- **[V]** Lumivero says neither it nor its AI providers train on customer data, and cloud data is encrypted in transit and at rest. — [Lumivero Security and compliance](https://lumivero.com/legal-information/security-and-compliance/); [Data Protection Addendum](https://lumivero.com/legal-information/data-protection-addendum/)

### Inferences
- **[I]** Data-sovereignty reading: NVivo desktop AI Assistant → OpenAI, region undocumented, probably US. ATLAS.ti → OpenAI under an EU-to-third-country SCC arrangement, so probably outside the EU. MAXQDA → EU. NVivo AI Cloud → Azure with an Australian region option. Only the last offers a plausible Australian-residency story, and it is new and not yet sold to institutions.
- **[I]** "Zero retention" and "no training" are contractual promises, not technical guarantees. They do not change *where* data is processed, and offshore transfer is what matters under Queensland's QPPs and under Indigenous data sovereignty principles. Have the university Privacy Office and IT Security assess this before enabling any AI feature on non-public material.
- **[I]** Material by risk tier: (a) published legislation, policy, audit reports and other CC BY government documents carry low privacy risk for AI processing, though licence attribution still applies; (b) RTI/FOI releases with names, emails or third-party personal information carry moderate risk, and the default should be no AI processing unless approved; (c) Indigenous-specific material, such as Traditional Owner consultation records, cultural heritage information or community statements, carries high governance sensitivity, and AI processing should be opt-out by default unless Indigenous governance consent supports it.
- **[I]** Methodologically, AI coding outputs need to be logged and kept apart from interpretive coding (prompt, model and build, date, documents sent, outputs accepted or rejected). That way the thesis can disclose AI use, and examiners can check that interpretive claims rest on researcher reasoning. This matters especially if the RTA persona advises reflexive thematic analysis.
- **[I]** Most Australian universities (expected; [U]) prohibit entering confidential, personal or sensitive research data into non-approved generative-AI tools and require AI use in theses to be disclosed. The persona should obtain the current university AI policy and HDR AI-disclosure rules and file them in the DMP.

## 4. What do research data management frameworks require (Australian Code, 2019 data guide, National Statement, DMPs, retention, FAIR, ARDC)?

### Takeaway
The Code (2018) and its 2019 data guide put retention, security, access and DMP expectations on both the institution and the researcher. The **National Statement 2025 edition superseded the 2023 edition from 23 June 2026**, so the thesis must cite the 2025 version. NHMRC has flagged a separate future consultation on the definition of human research and exemption from review. A documents-only study may qualify for exemption, or fall outside "human research", but only on an institutional determination. In Queensland the University Sector Retention and Disposal Schedule (QDAN 601) sets minimums; "Research data – other" is reported as 5 years after last action, such as publication.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The 2019 guide's structure covers institutional responsibilities (training, ownership and stewardship, storage and retention, safety and confidentiality, access, facilities) and researcher responsibilities. — [NHMRC guide](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/Management-of-Data-and-Information-in-Research.pdf)
- **[V-sec]** Institutions must provide secure storage facilities and have a retention policy for research data and materials. — [ANU LibGuide](https://libguides.anu.edu.au/c.php?g=881167&p=6358495); [Victoria University guide](https://libraryguides.vu.edu.au/research-data-management/AustralianCode)
- **[V]** **National Statement 2025** was released on 6 March 2025 with an original effective date of 1 October 2025. It was postponed in September 2025, and the final effective date is **23 June 2026**. The HREA form was updated to v1.6. The main change is a revised Section 4 (participants), which now uses "increased risk of harm" as the organising principle, plus a new chapter on research in disasters and crises and minor changes elsewhere. ## 5. What do Indigenous data sovereignty frameworks require (CARE, Maiam nayri Wingara, AIATSIS Code, Framework for Governance of Indigenous Data), and how do they apply to secondary or public documents?

### Takeaway
These frameworks treat Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples as rights-holders over data *about* them, including data in government records. Public availability does not end those interests. For a Brisbane 2032 study that analyses First Nations commitments, consultation records or cultural material, the persona should flag "Indigenous data" at intake, apply CARE alongside FAIR, consider the AIATSIS Code's responsibilities, and route the question to the university's Indigenous research governance.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** CARE Principles: Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics. They are "people and purpose-oriented" and complement the data-centric FAIR principles ("Be FAIR and CARE"). Developed by the RDA International Indigenous Data Sovereignty Interest Group with GIDA. Published as Carroll, S. R., et al. (2020), *Data Science Journal*, 19(1), 43; the DOI is reported as 10.5334/dsj-2020-043, though one record gives -042. A secondary source dates the principles' release to 2019. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/b3fcded6f18a4d0488c20ce01be01994); [ANU Research Portal](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/the-care-principles-for-indigenous-data-governance/); [Native Nations Institute](https://nni.arizona.edu/publications/care-principles-indigenous-data-governance); [CASRAI CARE explainer](https://casrai.org/learn/what-are-the-care-principles)
- **[V]** Maiam nayri Wingara: the Indigenous Data Sovereignty Summit was held in Canberra on **20 June 2018**, convened by the Maiam nayri Wingara Collective and the Australian Indigenous Governance Institute. Several key dates are now confirmed. ## 7. What are best practices for document version control and provenance (naming, URLs and retrieval dates, archived copies, checksums, reference managers, Git/OneDrive/SharePoint, secure storage)?

### Takeaway
Treat every document as an evidentiary object with a fixed identity: a unique ID, an ISO 8601 date-stamped filename, the source URL, the retrieval date, an archived copy URL, a SHA-256 checksum, licence and access route, and a version relationship to other documents. Keep all of this in a document register that is the single source of truth, linked to CAQDAS attributes. I could not re-verify most of the tool-specific guidance this session, so the items below are labelled as standard practice.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** The Queensland and Commonwealth legislation sites expose point-in-time "as made", "in force" and dated compilation URLs, which can be used to pin exact legal versions. — [Qld PRA 2023 as made](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/act-2023-033); [Federal Register compilation example](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/2024-03-20)
- **[V]** RTI disclosure-log retention is not set by statute (agency practice, e.g., at least 6 months), so log documents can disappear. — [OIC disclosure logs](https://www.oic.qld.gov.au/guidelines/for-government/access-and-amendment/disclosure-logs); [Dept of Education](https://alt-qed.qed.qld.gov.au/about-us/rti/disclosure-log)
- **[V]** Bond University's RDM toolkit addresses storage and backup within its research data guidance. — [Bond RDM toolkit: storage & backup](https://bond.libguides.com/research-data-management/research-data-management-toolkit/storage-backup)
- **[V]** UQ HDR thesis submission runs through UQ's Research Data Manager (UQRDM). — [UQ eSpace thesis submission guide](https://my.uq.edu.au/files/19615/my_UQ%20-%202_%20Thesis%20Submission%20-%20UQ%20eSpace.pdf)
- **[U]** Standard tools and identifiers, from prior knowledge, URLs not fetched this session: ISO 8601 dates (YYYY-MM-DD; [ISO 8601 page](https://www.iso.org/iso-8601-date-and-time-format.html)); Internet Archive Wayback Machine "Save Page Now" ([web.archive.org/save](https://web.archive.org/save)); the National Library of Australia's Australian Web Archive in Trove, which incorporates the PANDORA selective archive (since 1996) and whole-domain .au harvests ([webarchive.nla.gov.au](https://webarchive.nla.gov.au/)); Zotero ([zotero.org](https://www.zotero.org/)), which stores URL, "Accessed" date and page snapshots; EndNote; SHA-256 checksums (`sha256sum` on Linux, `shasum -a 256` on macOS, `Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256` in PowerShell).

### Inferences
- **[I]** File naming convention (recommended): `{DocID}_{YYYY-MM-DD}_{IssuerCode}_{DocType}_{ShortTitle}_v{NN}.pdf`, for example `D0147_2025-03-25_QLDGOV_POLICY_Games-Delivery-Plan_v01.pdf`. Rules: no spaces; ISO 8601 date = the document's *publication or effective* date, with retrieval date held in the register; the DocID never changes; a new version (amended plan, re-issued report, updated web page) gets a new DocID and version number, linked to its predecessor in a `supersedes` field. Never rename a file after CAQDAS import; the register maps names.
- **[I]** Document register fields (CSV or XLSX, version-controlled): DocID; title; issuing body; author; document type (legislation, regulation, policy, strategy, plan, audit report, parliamentary record, RTI/FOI release, media release, IOC/Organising Committee document); jurisdiction (Cth, Qld, local, IOC); publication date; effective date; version or edition; source URL; retrieval date and time (with time zone, AEST); archived URL (Wayback and/or Trove AWA); archive capture date; SHA-256; file format and pages; licence (CC BY 4.0, ©Crown, ©third party, unknown); access route (public web, disclosure log [log ID], own RTI/FOI [application ref, decision date]); sensitivity tier (public / contains personal info / restricted); Indigenous-data flag; AI-permitted flag (Y/N plus approval ref); CAQDAS import date and project build; supersedes / superseded-by; notes on authenticity, credibility, representativeness and meaning (Scott's criteria, from the document-analysis persona).
- **[I]** Capture routine for web documents: download the original file; trigger Save Page Now for the URL and the landing page; check for an existing Trove AWA capture; compute SHA-256; save to the Zotero item with an "Accessed" date and snapshot; enter in the register. For dynamic pages (dashboards, the Games website), also save a PDF print with a visible timestamp.
- **[I]** Version control by artefact type: binary CAQDAS projects should not be put in Git. Keep dated copies (`Project_YYYY-MM-DD_vNN.nvp`) and close the project before copying. Text artefacts (document register CSV, codebook exports, memos exported to Markdown or DOCX, analysis scripts, DMP) can go in a private Git repository on university-approved hosting, or rely on OneDrive/SharePoint version history.
- **[I]** Storage: put the master project on university-managed storage (for UQ, UQRDM-provisioned storage [U]). Avoid opening live CAQDAS projects from inside sync clients (OneDrive/Dropbox), because sync conflicts can corrupt the database file (vendor guidance commonly warns against this [U]). Follow 3-2-1 backups: three copies, two media, one off-site, all on approved infrastructure. Encrypt laptops; no personal cloud accounts; restricted folders for RTI/FOI tier-2 material and the Indigenous-data subset.
- **[I]** Reference managers: use Zotero or EndNote for scholarly literature *and* grey literature, with collections mirroring the register's document types. Store the DocID in Zotero's "Extra" field so thesis citations, the register and CAQDAS items reconcile.
- **[I]** Fixity checks: re-run the checksums at each milestone and before deposit. A mismatch means the file has been altered or replaced, so investigate before analysing further.

## 8. What are thesis deposit, open access and embargo requirements at Australian (Queensland) universities, and how are data and analysis archived?

### Takeaway
Australian universities deposit HDR theses in the institutional repository, open access by default, with a time-limited embargo available on stated grounds. At UQ the thesis is submitted through UQRDM and published in UQ eSpace; embargoes are typically about 3 years and can be extended by form. Archiving the data and analysis is a separate decision: a mediated deposit of metadata, codebook and analysis outputs, subject to licence, privacy and Indigenous governance limits.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** UQ eSpace is "the archival home of UQ Research Higher Degree theses". HDR students submit through the Graduate School process using Research Data Manager, and the thesis is published according to the access preference chosen at finalisation. Open access is the default. An embargo (typically about **3 years**) can be justified on academic, cultural, ethical, legal or commercial grounds. If IP is assigned to UQ, the principal advisor chooses. Forms exist to lift an embargo, or to request or extend one after conferral. — [UQ eSpace scope and policy](https://web.library.uq.edu.au/find-and-borrow/collections-overview/collection-management/uq-espace-scope-and-policy); [my.UQ Award of degree](https://my.uq.edu.au/node/591); [UQ thesis submission guide PDF](https://my.uq.edu.au/files/19615/my_UQ%20-%202_%20Thesis%20Submission%20-%20UQ%20eSpace.pdf)
- **[V]** Other Australian universities run comparable deposit and embargo procedures, for example JCU's HDR Thesis Library Deposit Guide, UTS's embargo extension request form (April 2026) and UNE's "Right of Access" form. — [JCU deposit guide](https://www.jcu.edu.au/graduate-research-school/forms-and-policies/hdr-thesis-library-deposit-guide); [UTS embargo extension form](https://www.uts.edu.au/globalassets/shared-media/documents/grs/grs-embargo-extension-request-form-14042026.docx); [UNE Right of Access](https://www.une.edu.au/research/graduate-research-school/hdr-forms-and-policies/hdr-supervision-and-examination/Right-of-Access.docx)

### Inferences
- **[I]** Embargo triggers for this PhD: material from the candidate's own RTI/FOI releases that is not on a disclosure log; information obtained in confidence; Indigenous cultural material where community review is pending; publication strategy, if supervisors want articles out before the thesis. Otherwise the thesis should be open access.
- **[I]** Deposit package (an analysis archive, separate from the thesis): README; DMP (final); document register (CSV) with archived URLs and checksums; licence-cleared documents only (for example, CC BY Queensland documents); codebook (.qdc and DOCX) with version history; a QDPX export with non-redistributable sources removed or replaced; method/audit memo and AI-use log (PDF/A); and a data-access statement. Deposit in the institutional data repository (for example, UQ eSpace data collections or the university's RDA-linked repository [U]) with a DOI and a restricted-access tier where needed.
- **[I]** Indigenous-data subset: deposit metadata only, with access mediated by Indigenous governance, consistent with CARE "Authority to control".
- **[I]** Copyright in the thesis: quoting government documents falls under fair dealing (research or study, and criticism or review [U]) or CC BY attribution. Reproducing full figures, maps or images from third-party consultant reports may need permission before open deposit. The Library's copyright service should check this before final lodgement.

## 9. How does the consultant behave: project set-up, workflows, checklists, audit trails and compliance reviews?

### Takeaway
The consultant works in phases: inception, intake, analysis, milestone and deposit. At each phase they use a checklist, produce a dated artefact, and set escalation triggers, so the thesis can show compliance and analytic dependability to examiners. Workflows are inferred from the frameworks in sections 2–8, not from a published standard.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** DMPs are "strongly encouraged" from the outset under the Code; researchers must keep clear, accurate, secure and complete records (R22). — [UNSW summary of Code](http://climate-cms.wikis.unsw.edu.au/Australian_Code_for_the_Responsible_Conduct_of_Research)
- **[V]** ATLAS.ti asks for consent before AI uploads and offers an organisation-level switch to disable OpenAI integration. NVivo 15 AI can be disabled by registry key. — [ATLAS.ti AI security](https://atlasti.com/ai-data-security-and-privacy); [NVivo AI Assistant help](https://community.lumivero.com/s/article/NV15Win-Content-ai-ai-assistant?language=en_US)
- **[V]** NVivo AI Cloud requires the researcher to approve AI suggestions and traces AI actions back to source data. — [NVivo AI Cloud](https://lumivero.com/products/nvivo/)
- **[V]** QDPX transfers lose information and are not a cross-version format. — [ATLAS.ti QDPX](https://atlasti.com/features/project-export-in-qdpx-format); [MAXQDA REFI help](https://www.maxqda.com/help/report-and-export/export-and-import-refi-qda-projects)

### Inferences
**Phase 0, Inception (months 0–3): "compliance baseline" checklist [I]**
1. DMP drafted in the university system (for example UQRDM at UQ [U]). It covers data types (public documents, RTI/FOI, Indigenous-data subset), storage, backup, access, retention trigger (QDAN 601: 5 years after last action), deposit intentions, AI-use stance, and roles (candidate, principal advisor, associate advisor).
2. Ethics determination requested in writing: not human research, exempt, or lower risk, citing the National Statement 2025.
3. Indigenous data screening: contact the Indigenous research office and record a decision on the applicability of AIATSIS, CARE and Maiam nayri Wingara.
4. CAQDAS choice recorded (NVivo 15.6.x or ATLAS.ti 26.x) with build numbers. Check licence (university site licence). AI features *off by default*, with ATLAS.ti privacy mode on and NVivo AI disabled until approved.
5. Storage provisioned on approved infrastructure; folder skeleton created (`00_admin`, `01_register`, `02_sources/{public,rti_foi_restricted,indigenous_restricted}`, `03_caqdas/{master,snapshots,exports}`, `04_memos`, `05_outputs`, `06_deposit`).
6. Document register template and naming convention signed off by supervisors.
7. CAQDAS architecture specified:
   - **Files**: one per DocID.
   - **Cases**: organisations or actors (Queensland Government departments, Games delivery bodies, local councils, IOC, Organising Committee, Traditional Owner bodies [I]) and/or policy instruments.
   - **Classifications and attributes**: on files, DocType, Issuer, Jurisdiction, PubDate, Games-lifecycle phase (bid, establishment, planning, delivery), FormalVsImplementation (for means–ends decoupling evidence), TourismLegacyRelevance, IndigenousData flag, AccessRoute, Licence. On cases, sector, governance level, statutory basis.
   - **Codes**: a top-level split between a priori theory codes (institutional logics, legitimacy claims, decoupling types such as policy–practice and means–ends, leverage mechanisms) and inductive codes, following the RTA or document-analysis persona's advice.
   - **Memos**: one Method/Audit Log, one AI-Use Log, one Reflexive Journal, plus per-document analytic memos.
   - **Sets**: for example "QAO reports", "RTI tier", "Indigenous subset".
   - **Framework matrices**: case × theme summaries for cross-case comparison.

**Phase 1, Document intake (continuous): per-document checklist [I]**
- Is the source authoritative (issuer's site, legislation site, disclosure log)? Record URL, retrieval timestamp, Wayback/Trove capture, SHA-256, licence, and the version relationship.
- Sensitivity tier and Indigenous flag assigned; AI-permitted flag set.
- Imported into CAQDAS with attributes populated from the register; never edited inside CAQDAS. Annotate rather than alter.
- Weekly reconciliation: register row count = CAQDAS file count, and any differences are explained.

**Phase 2, Analysis (ongoing): audit-trail discipline [I]**
- Every analytic session ends with a dated entry in the Method/Audit memo: what was coded, codebook changes and why, queries run (saved queries with parameters), and decisions.
- Codebook versioning: export the codebook (.qdc/DOCX) at each structural change, with a change log (added, merged, split, retired codes, and why).
- AI-use protocol (only if approved): record feature, software build, model or provider if disclosed, date, DocIDs sent (tier 1 only), prompt or intent text, outputs, and what was accepted, modified or rejected and why. Keep AI-generated codes in a separate, visibly labelled branch until the researcher re-codes or validates them.
- Snapshots: close the project, then make a dated copy weekly. Export QDPX monthly and at each milestone, and test-open the QDPX once in a second tool or a fresh project to confirm it can be restored.

**Phase 3, Milestone compliance reviews (confirmation, mid-candidature, pre-submission) [I]**
- Re-verify the regulatory baseline: National Statement version, university AI policy, QPP obligations, any new Indigenous-data guidance. Re-run checksums. Check that all register links and archived copies resolve. Confirm that the backup restores. Update the DMP. Supervisors sign off.
- **Event-driven reviews** are triggered by: adding own-RTI/FOI material; adding Indigenous-specific material; enabling any AI feature; changing CAQDAS version or tool (migration plan); adding a co-coder or collaborator (the cloud collaboration residency question); a data incident (university breach procedure under IP Act Chapter 3A [I]); planning publication of datasets.

**Phase 4, Pre-submission and deposit [I]**
- Copyright clearance of figures; licence check on documents being deposited; embargo decision; final export bundle (section 8); DMP closure with retention trigger date; destruction plan for restricted copies at the end of retention, according to schedule.

### Gaps
- No published Australian standard checklist for CAQDAS-based document studies was found. These checklists are synthesised and should be checked by the university Library's RDM team.

## 11. What are the red flags and failure modes?

### Takeaway
The biggest risks are: assuming "public means unrestricted" (ethics, privacy, Indigenous data); switching on CAQDAS AI features that send material offshore without approval; losing provenance (link rot, unrecorded versions, renamed files); single-copy or sync-corrupted projects; trusting lossy QDPX migrations; and citing superseded rules, especially the National Statement 2023 after 23 June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **[V]** Public availability does not imply permission for research use. — [National Statement 2023](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2023.pdf)
- **[V]** The National Statement 2025 has been in force since 23 June 2026. — [NHMRC FAQs](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/ethics/national-statement-ethical-conduct-human-research/2025-update-faqs)
- **[V]** NVivo desktop AI goes to OpenAI with zero retention, and no region option was documented. ATLAS.ti AI goes to OpenAI under SCCs. — [Lumivero blog](https://lumivero.com/resources/blog/qualitative-data-analysis-reimagined-ai-reflexivity-and-memoing-with-nvivo-15/); [ATLAS.ti AI security](https://atlasti.com/ai-data-security-and-privacy)
- **[V]** At least one university (UiO) has refused to approve NVivo Collaboration Cloud on security and privacy grounds. — [UiO](https://www.uio.no/english/services/it/research/data-collection-and-analysis/nvivo/help/collaboration-in-nvivo.html)
- **[V]** QDPX loses information; variables and attributes may drop out. — [MAXQDA support](https://help.maxqda.com/en/support/solutions/articles/80001135550-can-i-import-nvivo-or-atlas-ti-projects-into-maxqda-); [GoTranscript guide](https://gotranscript.com/public/guia-para-migrar-proyectos-qda-con-refi-qda-qdpx)
- **[V]** Disclosure logs are incomplete and late: in 2026, 20 of 30 agencies missed the s 11C deadline. — [OAIC desktop review 2026](https://www.oaic.gov.au/freedom-of-information/information-commissioner-decisions-and-reports/foi-reports/disclosure-log-desktop-review-2026)
- **[V]** Vendor AI claims (for example "up to 90%" time savings) are marketing. — [ATLAS.ti features](https://atlasti.com/features)
- **[V]** Vendor documentation is internally inconsistent on release dates (ATLAS.ti 25.0.0) and hosting regions (NVivo AI Cloud, four vs five regions). — [ATLAS.ti updates](https://atlasti.com/updates); [Lumivero FAQ](https://lumivero.com/resources/test-test-test/)

### Inferences
**Red flags [I]**
1. No written ethics or exemption determination on file ("it's only documents").
2. AI Assistant, AI Coding or Conversational AI used on RTI/FOI material containing personal information, or on Indigenous-specific material, without Privacy Office or IT approval and Indigenous governance agreement.
3. AI-generated codes merged into the main codebook without a log, or presented as "themes" without researcher interpretation. This is a dependability and reflexivity failure.
4. Documents with no retrieval date or archived copy. Government web pages, Games websites and dashboards change or disappear.
5. Multiple versions of the same plan or strategy coded as one source; amendments not tracked (`supersedes`). This is especially damaging when the thesis argues *change over time* or decoupling between formal versions and implementation.
6. Files renamed or replaced after CAQDAS import; register and project out of sync; checksums not re-verified.
7. Live .nvp/.nvpx or .atlproj files opened from OneDrive or Dropbox sync folders; one copy only; no restore test.
8. Migrating between tools or major versions via QDPX without a validation check (counts of files, codes, coded references, attributes before and after).
9. Inconsistent attribute values (free-text "Qld Govt" vs "Queensland Government"). Use controlled vocabularies.
10. Citing the National Statement 2023 for determinations made after 23 June 2026; citing pre-IPOLA IPPs or NPPs for Queensland after 1 July 2025; citing the Public Records Act 2002 after 5 December 2024.
11. Depositing third-party copyrighted documents (IOC, consultants, media) in an open dataset under the belief that fair dealing covers redistribution.
12. Treating CC BY as covering embedded logos, images, third-party material or ICIP.
13. Indigenous-data subset deposited openly, or tagged with deficit-framed metadata.
14. "Inter-rater reliability" statistics added to an RTA design to look rigorous. This is a methodological mismatch to raise with the RTA persona.
15. Embargo not considered for material from the candidate's own RTI applications, or for information received in confidence.
16. Retention trigger not recorded, so data is destroyed too early or kept indefinitely without a reason.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical studies, in the time available, on failure rates of CAQDAS project corruption or QDPX migration.

## 12. How does this persona interface with other personas (RTA consultant, document-analysis expert, supervisors and others)?

### Takeaway
The consultant is the panel's infrastructure and compliance hub. They turn methodological choices (RTA, document analysis, neo-institutional coding) into project architecture and audit artefacts. ### Inferences
- **[I] RTA consultant:** The consultant sets up the reflexive journal, the memo system and the codebook versioning that RTA needs. They raise the question of whether AI coding fits reflexive TA and log any use. They avoid reliability theatre (no kappa unless the design calls for it). Shared artefacts: the reflexive memo, codebook change log, AI-use log. Possible tension: RTA prefers fluid, evolving codes, while compliance prefers frozen, versioned states. Resolve it with snapshot exports rather than freezing the codes.
- **[I] Document-analysis expert:** This persona supplies the source-criticism fields (authenticity, credibility, representativeness, meaning; document genre; intended audience) that become register columns and CAQDAS attributes. They also decide how versions and amendments are treated analytically, and the consultant implements those decisions as `supersedes` links.
- **[I] Neo-institutional theory / decoupling expert:** This persona supplies the a priori code families and the attribute logic (for example, FormalVsImplementation, and the stated end vs actual means for means–ends decoupling).
