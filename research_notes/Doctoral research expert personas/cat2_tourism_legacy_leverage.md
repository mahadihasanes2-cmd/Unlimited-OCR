## Q1. How are legacy and leverage conceptualised (classic frameworks plus 2020–2026 developments)?

### Takeaway
The field separates **legacy** from **leverage**:
- **Legacy** is an outcome or structural change that lasts beyond the event and is judged ex post against a counterfactual (Preuss).
- **Leverage** is a *strategy*: deliberate, planned action to use the event as a resource for host objectives before, during and after it (Chalip; O'Brien).

The strategist persona treats any thesis or policy text that uses "legacy" for both a hoped-for outcome and the activity that is supposed to produce it as a conceptual error. It also flags this as a possible sign of means–ends decoupling.

### Cited Findings

**Preuss: the legacy cube and later frameworks**
- **[CITATION]** Preuss, H. (2007). The conceptualisation and measurement of mega sport event legacies. *Journal of Sport & Tourism*, 12(3–4), 207–228. https://doi.org/10.1080/14775080701736957
  - Legacy is defined (as commonly quoted) as "all planned and unplanned, positive and negative, tangible and intangible structures created for and by a sport event that remain longer than the event itself", irrespective of the time of production and space.
  - The **"legacy cube"** has three axes: planned/unplanned, positive/negative and tangible/intangible. Time and space are added dimensions.
  - Preuss also lists event-created "structures": infrastructure; knowledge/skills/education; image; emotions; networks; culture. (The list is as recalled; check the original.)
  - He argues for a "top-down" (macro, counterfactual) versus "bottom-up" (benchmark of event-induced structures) measurement logic.
- **[CITATION]** Gratton, C., & Preuss, H. (2008). Maximizing Olympic impacts by building up legacies. *The International Journal of the History of Sport*, 25(14), 1922–1938. https://doi.org/10.1080/09523360802439023
  - Restates the cube. Argues that legacy must be measured net of what would have happened without the Games (gross vs net legacy) and over long horizons, and that positive legacies must be "built up" through planning.
- **[CITATION]** Preuss, H. (2015). A framework for identifying the legacies of a mega sport event. *Leisure Studies*, 34(6), 643–664. https://doi.org/10.1080/02614367.2014.994552
  - Reframes legacy as event-induced **changes to a host's "location factors"**, which produce legacies when stakeholders experience them.
  - As recalled, six change categories: urban development; environmental enhancement; policies and governance; human development; intellectual property; social development. Verify the exact labels.
- **[CITATION]** Preuss, H. (2019). Event legacy framework and measurement. *International Journal of Sport Policy and Politics*, 11(1), 103–118. https://doi.org/10.1080/19406940.2018.1490336
  - Operationalises measurement around (i) identifying event-caused changes, (ii) who is affected and how, and (iii) comparison with a no-event counterfactual and a baseline, over time. (Paraphrased from prior knowledge.)
- **[CITATION]** Preuss, H. (2005). The economic impact of visitors at major multi-sport events. *European Sport Management Quarterly*, 5(3), 281–301.
  - Gives the **visitor typology** behind displacement accounting: home-stayers, runaways and changers among residents; event visitors, casuals, time-switchers, extentioners and avoiders among non-residents.
  - Only some types bring genuinely additional ("new") money.

**Systematic reviews**
- **[CITATION]** Thomson, A., Cuskelly, G., Toohey, K., Kennelly, M., Burton, P., & Fredline, L. (2019). Sport event legacy: A systematic quantitative review of literature. *Sport Management Review*, 22(3), 295–321. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smr.2018.06.011

**Chalip and O'Brien: leverage**
- **[CITATION]** Chalip, L. (2004). Beyond impact: A general model for sport event leverage. In B. W. Ritchie & D. Adair (Eds.), *Sport tourism: Interrelationships, impacts and issues* (pp. 226–252). Channel View.
  - The **general model of event leverage** runs from leverageable resource to opportunity to strategic objectives to means.
  - Resource 1, *event visitors and trade*. Opportunities: entice visitor spending, lengthen visitor stays, retain event expenditures, use the event to build regional business relationships. Objective: optimise total trade and revenue.
  - Resource 2, *event media*. Opportunities: showcase the destination via event advertising and reporting, and use the event in destination advertising and promotions. Objective: enhance the destination image.
  - Core claim: impacts are not automatic. They must be produced by strategy, which shifts the focus from ex post impact to ex ante leverage.
- **[CITATION]** Chalip, L. (2006). Towards social leverage of sport events. *Journal of Sport & Tourism*, 11(2), 109–127. https://doi.org/10.1080/14775080601155126
  - Social leverage builds on **liminality and communitas** produced by event celebration.
  - Means include enabling sociability, creating event-related social spaces, producing ancillary events, theming, and showcasing celebration.
  - Its significance here is that tourism and social leverage share means.
- **[CITATION]** O'Brien, D. (2006). Event business leveraging: The Sydney 2000 Olympic Games. *Annals of Tourism Research*, 33(1), 240–261. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annals.2005.10.011
  - Studies the Sydney 2000 business-leveraging initiatives (notably Business Club Australia).
  - Shows that leverage depends on purpose-built organisational vehicles, networks and the timing of activity before the Games, and that benefits are not spontaneous.
- **[CITATION]** O'Brien, D., & Chalip, L. (2008). Sport events and strategic leveraging: Pushing towards the triple bottom line. In A. G. Woodside & D. Martin (Eds.), *Tourism management: Analysis, behaviour and strategy* (pp. 318–338). CABI.
  - Extends the leverage model to economic, social and environmental (triple-bottom-line) objectives.
- **[CITATION]** Chalip, L. (2014). From legacy to leverage. In J. Grix (Ed.), *Leveraging legacies from sports mega-events: Concepts and cases* (pp. 2–12). Palgrave Macmillan.
- **[CITATION]** Chalip, L. (2017). Trading legacy for leverage. In I. Brittain, J. Bocarro, T. Byers & K. Swart (Eds.), *Legacies and mega events: Fact or fairy tales?* (pp. 25–41). Routledge.

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The evaluative crux for this persona is that **legacy is an ex post state and leverage is an ex ante plan with means.** A strategy document listing legacy aspirations without leverage means (owners, budgets, programmes, KPIs, timing) is exactly the configuration that neo-institutional theory reads as potential **means–ends decoupling**. The policy is adopted and activity may occur, but the causal link to the stated end is unspecified (Bromley & Powell 2012; see Q5). Smith's (2014) "convenient justification" critique is the tourism-studies analogue of ceremonial conformity.
- **[INFERENCE]** Preuss's 2015 and 2019 shift from "structures" to "changes in location factors that affect stakeholders" gives a document-based thesis a coding frame. For each claimed legacy, code (i) the change, (ii) the affected stakeholders, (iii) the counterfactual stated or implied, and (iv) the leverage means attached.
- **[INFERENCE]** Ziakas's portfolio lens is the right yardstick for Queensland's "events capital" ambition (Destination 2045; TEQ 2025–29, see Q4). The question becomes whether 2032 is embedded in a portfolio with cross-leverage or treated as a singular spike.

## Q2. What is the evidence on Olympic tourism outcomes (Weed; Fourie & Santana-Gallego; Solberg & Preuss; crowding-out; Sydney, London, Rio, Tokyo, Paris)?

### Takeaway
Ex post evidence shows **Games-time tourism is frequently flat or displaced.** London 2012 Q3 visits fell 4.2% year on year. Paris 2024 saw iconic attractions down 22–29% and largely domestic visitors. Spend per visitor can rise, and longer-run arrival effects are modest, conditional and highly dependent on leverage, season and capacity.

Headline forecasts made before the Games (e.g., "a million extra people" for London) are routinely overstated. The strategist treats any thesis or policy claim of automatic tourism legacy as unsupported.

Effects vary by event type, by whether the event is held in peak or off-peak season (larger off-peak), and by participating versus non-participating origin countries, with crowding-out of the latter. ## Q3. How do the main economic evaluation methods compare (I–O, CGE, CBA), and what do the IOC's OGI and later legacy reporting add?

### Takeaway
- **Input–output (I–O) multiplier studies** measure gross *activity* and systematically overstate benefits. They assume no resource constraints, no crowding-out, and no opportunity cost of public funds.
- **CGE** models net *economy-wide* effects (GSP/GDP, consumption) once resource reallocation and price effects are allowed for.
- **Cost–benefit analysis (CBA)** asks whether residents are better off (welfare, net present value), which is the question public investment should answer.

Ex post studies (Sydney CGE retrospective; Paris Cour des comptes) are consistently less favourable than ex ante, commissioned studies.

### Cited Findings

## Q4. What targets and frameworks would the strategist measure against (national, Queensland, Brisbane 2032, regional), and which are current or superseded?

### Takeaway
The target architecture is **multi-level, multi-metric and time-misaligned**:
- **THRIVE 2030 (national, March 2022):** $230bn by 2030, since redefined to *exclude* long-stay international students. The regional target is now reported as $95bn, down from $100bn.
- **Towards Tourism 2032 (Qld, Nov 2022):** $44bn overnight visitor expenditure (OVE) by 2032, interim $34bn by 2027. Appears superseded.
- **Destination 2045 (Qld, June 2025):** double visitor expenditure to $84bn by 2045.
- **Elevate 2042 (Brisbane 2032 Legacy Strategy, 10 Nov 2023):** four themes including "Economy" (in some sources "economy of the future").
- **KPMG (2021):** $8.1bn Qld and about $17.6bn national quantified benefits over 20 years, including $4.6bn Qld tourism and trade.

Each uses a different metric, baseline and horizon, so the strategist's first task is to reconcile them before testing any finding.

## Q5. How do strategists judge a thesis's practical and policy relevance (additionality, counterfactuals, time horizons, regional dispersal, and the stated-goal versus delivery-means gap)?

### Takeaway
The strategist judges relevance by whether the thesis can tell decision-makers (DETSI, TEQ, BEDA, the OCOG, councils) **which stated tourism and legacy ends lack credible delivery means, and why**. That means testing every legacy claim for:
- additionality (net of deadweight, displacement, leakage and substitution);
- an explicit counterfactual;
- a dated horizon and metric;
- spatial distribution (regional dispersal);
- owned, resourced leverage means.

The last test is where a means–ends decoupling lens adds distinctive value.

### Cited Findings

**Decoupling theory**
- **[CITATION]** Meyer, J. W., & Rowan, B. (1977). Institutionalized organizations: Formal structure as myth and ceremony. *American Journal of Sociology*, 83(2), 340–363. https://doi.org/10.1086/226550
  - Organisations adopt formal structures (here, legacy strategies and targets) for legitimacy, which can be decoupled from technical activity.
- **[CITATION]** Bromley, P., & Powell, W. W. (2012). From smoke and mirrors to walking the talk: Decoupling in the contemporary world. *Academy of Management Annals*, 6(1), 483–530. https://doi.org/10.5465/19416520.2012.684462
  - Distinguishes **policy–practice decoupling** (policies not implemented) from **means–ends decoupling**: policies are implemented, but the activities are loosely linked to the goals they are meant to achieve, which is common where outcomes are opaque and evaluation is weak.
- **[CITATION]** Wijen, F. (2014). Means versus ends in opaque institutional fields: Trading off compliance and achievement in sustainability standard adoption. *Academy of Management Review*, 39(3), 302–323. https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2012.0218
  - In opaque fields, rigid compliance with prescribed means can crowd out achievement of ends. This is directly analogous to venue delivery compliance versus tourism-legacy achievement.

**Leverage, legacy and audit evidence for this test**
- **[CITATION]** Chalip (2004), O'Brien (2006) and O'Brien & Chalip (2008), as in Q1: leverage requires strategic objectives, specific means and organisational vehicles. Without them, legacy is unlikely.
- **[CITATION]** Smith (2014), as in Q1: leverage rhetoric can be a "convenient justification".
- **[CITATION]** Preuss (2019) and Gratton & Preuss (2008), as in Q1: legacy must be assessed against a counterfactual and over time.
- **[VERIFIED-WEB]** The Cour des comptes warned that commissioned Games-impact studies "tend to overestimate". — [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/pour-la-cour-des-comptes-paris-2024-eu-un-impact-economique-limite)
- **[VERIFIED-WEB]** Gold Coast 2018 shows that ex post evaluation can contradict ex ante assumptions in both directions: domestic spend about half of forecast, regional spillover higher. — [GC2018 economic impacts report](https://library.olympics.com/fiba/digitalCollection/DigitalCollectionAttachmentDownloadHandler.ashx?parentDocumentId=3023688&documentId=3023689&skipWatermark=true&skipCopyright=true)

**Trickle-down and spillover evidence**
- **[CITATION]** Misener, L., Taks, M., Chalip, L., & Green, B. C. (2015). The elusive "trickle-down effect" of sport events: Assumptions and missed opportunities. *Managing Sport and Leisure*, 20(2), 135–156.
  - Assumed spillovers (e.g., participation, small business) rarely materialise without deliberate leverage.
- **[CITATION]** Beesley, L. G., & Chalip, L. (2011). Seeking (and not seeking) to leverage mega-sport events in non-host destinations: The case of Shanghai and the Beijing Olympics. *Journal of Sport & Tourism*, 16(4), 323–344.
  - Relevant to regional (non-host) Queensland destinations: leverage by non-host regions is a strategic choice, not an automatic spillover.

### Inferences
- **[INFERENCE]** The persona's **policy-relevance test** for the thesis:
  1. **Additionality:** does the thesis distinguish gross from net, and identify deadweight (what would occur anyway, e.g., trend growth toward THRIVE targets), displacement (crowding-out, time-switching), leakage (spend leaving the region or state) and substitution?
  2. **Counterfactual:** does it state what the "no Games" or "Games without leverage" world looks like? A qualitative thesis can do this with a reasoned counterfactual or comparative cases (GC2018, Sydney 2000, Paris 2024).
  3. **Horizon:** are claims dated to pre-Games (2021–31), Games-time (2032) or post-Games (2033–42 and 2045)? Are they aligned to the horizon of the relevant target?
  4. **Spatial distribution:** does the analysis test SEQ concentration against stated dispersal ends (the VPA's 50%, THRIVE's regional share, Destination 2045 regional events)?
  5. **Means–ends:** for each stated tourism or economic end, does a delivery means exist? That means an owner agency, budget line, programme, KPI and reporting cycle. Is the causal theory linking the means to the end stated, plausible and evidenced?
  6. **Actionability:** are the findings framed so agencies can act (e.g., "assign a leverage owner and outcome KPI to the regional-dispersal goal")?
- **[INFERENCE]** **Where decoupling is likely in this case.** The OCOG and GIICA hold delivery mandates focused on venues, operations and budget compliance (the *means* that are monitored). Tourism and economic legacy *ends* are dispersed across DETSI, TEQ, BEDA, councils and Austrade, and appear in strategies with differing metrics and horizons. Opaque outcomes plus diffuse ownership is the classic means–ends decoupling condition identified by Bromley & Powell (2012) and Wijen (2014).
- **[INFERENCE]** The persona counts it as a *positive* sign if the thesis shows **recoupling mechanisms**: independent outcome monitoring (an OGI-like indicator panel), published leverage plans with budgets, ex post CBA commitments, and lessons transferred from the GC2018 evaluation.

### Gaps
- I found no verified public Brisbane 2032 tourism *leverage plan* with named owners, budgets and KPIs. Its absence is itself a finding to test, not an assumption.
- I found no verified independent ex post monitoring regime announced for Brisbane 2032 tourism outcomes.

## Q6. Persona specification: role, behaviour, evaluation questions, evidence standards, skills, red flags and interfaces (objective items a, b, d, e, f)

### Takeaway
The **Tourism Legacy and Leverage Strategist** works like a visitor-economy policy evaluator and destination strategist with event-economics literacy. They read the thesis's governance and decoupling findings against dated macro targets (THRIVE 2030, Destination 2045) and regional and Games frameworks (TEQ, Elevate 2042, KPMG). They insist on gross-versus-net discipline, separate legacy from leverage, and translate decoupling findings into consequences for tourism outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- **[CITATION]** Getz, D., & Page, S. J. (2016). Progress and prospects for event tourism research. *Tourism Management*, 52, 593–631. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tourman.2015.03.007
  - A field map of event tourism policy, evaluation and portfolio management.
- **[CITATION]** Jago, L., Dwyer, L., Lipman, G., van Lill, D., & Vorster, S. (2010). Optimising the potential of mega-events: An overview. *International Journal of Event and Festival Management*, 1(3), 220–237.
- **[CITATION]** Crompton (1995, 2006); Késenne (2005); Dwyer et al. (2004–2006); Preuss (2005, 2007, 2015, 2019); Chalip (2004, 2006); Smith (2014); Bromley & Powell (2012), as above.
- **[VERIFIED-WEB]** Institutional anchors: [Austrade THRIVE 2030](https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/how-we-can-help-you/programs-and-services/thrive-2030-strategy); [DETSI Destination 2045](https://www.detsi.qld.gov.au/tourism/destination-2045/20-year-tourism-plan); [TEQ Strategic Plan 2025–29](https://teq.queensland.com/content/dam/teq/corporate/corporate-searchable-assets/industry/strategies/industry-strategic-plans/TEQ-Strategic-Plan-2025-2029.pdf); [Elevate 2042 summary](https://q2032-public-assets.s3.ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/documents/ELEVATE%202042%20Legacy%20Strategy_SUMMARY.pdf); [Qld 2032 reports (KPMG/VPA)](https://www.premiers.qld.gov.au/publications/categories/reports/2032-olympic-and-paralympic-games.aspx)

### Inferences (persona design; all [INFERENCE] built on the sources above)

**(a) Role**
- Tests whether the thesis's findings matter for *tourism and economic legacy and leverage* outcomes, and whether its handling of tourism and economic evidence in documents is technically sound.
- Acts as a bridge between governance theory and visitor-economy policy practice.
- Asks "so what for the visitor economy?" of every governance finding.

**(b) Behaviour: how they test findings**
1. **Target audit:** builds the reconciliation table (Q4), dating each instrument and flagging superseded ones (TT32, VPA) and redefined ones (THRIVE).
2. **Claim classification:** codes each document claim as legacy (outcome) or leverage (means); planned or unplanned; positive or negative; tangible or intangible (Preuss cube); and by time and space.
3. **Number provenance check:** for every figure, records method (I–O, CGE or CBA), ex ante or ex post, gross or net, metric (spend, OVE, GSP, welfare), geography (Brisbane, SEQ, Qld, Australia), horizon, discounting, commissioner and independence.
4. **Displacement stress test:** applies Preuss's (2005) visitor typology, seasonality (a winter Games against the peak domestic winter market), capacity (QTIC rooms) and the London and Paris precedents.
5. **Means–ends trace:** for each end, looks for owner, budget, programme, KPI and reporting, and the causal theory linking them.
6. **Comparative benchmark:** Sydney 2000 (Giesecke & Madden; O'Brien), GC2018 (post-Games evaluation), London 2012 (ONS), Paris 2024 (OECD; Cour des comptes).
7. **Relevance verdict:** what a DETSI, TEQ, BEDA or OCOG reader could change because of the thesis.

**Evaluation questions they ask**
- Which target, at which date and in which metric, is this finding being tested against? Is that instrument still current?
- Is this a legacy claim or a leverage claim? If leverage, who owns it, what does it cost, and how will success be measured?
- Is this figure gross visitor expenditure, GSP or welfare? Who commissioned it? Was it ever re-estimated ex post?
- What is the counterfactual? How much of the THRIVE or Destination 2045 trajectory would happen without 2032 (deadweight)?
- Where do displaced visitors and runaway residents show up in the analysis?
- Does the regional-dispersal end (VPA's 50%; Destination 2045 regional events) have regional leverage means, or is it assumed "trickle-down" (Misener et al. 2015)?
- How does the thesis handle the 20-year horizons of KPMG and Elevate 2042 against national targets that end in 2030?
- Did 2032 planning learn from GC2018's evaluation (domestic spend about half of forecast)?
- Is image leverage evidenced through destination-image mechanisms (Kaplanidou) or merely asserted ("global showcase")?
- Does the thesis distinguish the event *signal* (the bid; Rose & Spiegel 2011) from *hosting* effects?

**Evidence standards**
- Primary, dated documents, with versions tracked.
- Independent or ex post evidence weighted above commissioned or ex ante evidence.
- CBA or CGE net measures preferred over I–O.
- Explicit counterfactuals and sensitivity ranges.
- Official statistics from Tourism Research Australia (NVS, IVS, tourism satellite accounts) and the ABS for monitoring claims.
- Qualitative claims supported by document triangulation and process tracing.
- Numbers are reported with their stated caveats ("preliminary", "not an impact analysis").

**(d) Skills**
- Visitor-economy statistics literacy: OVE, total spend, inbound versus domestic, day trips, satellite accounts, the student-exclusion definitional change.
- Event-economics literacy: I–O, CGE and CBA logic; multipliers; additionality (deadweight, displacement, leakage, substitution); NPV and discounting; job-years versus jobs.
- Leverage and portfolio strategy (Chalip; O'Brien; Ziakas).
- Destination image and marketing (Kaplanidou).
- Australian policy-instrument mapping across the Commonwealth (Austrade, TRA, Tourism Australia), state (DETSI, TEQ, Treasury) and local or regional (BEDA, councils, CoMSEQ) levels.
- Document forensics: version control, superseded-strategy detection, metric reconciliation.
- Translating decoupling findings into policy recommendations.

**(e) Red flags and failure modes the persona watches for**
1. Confusing **gross with net** impact, or citing the VPA's $20.2bn visitor spend as a "benefit".
2. Ignoring **displacement and crowding-out**, time-switchers, casuals and resident runaways (Preuss 2005; ONS 2012; Paris 2024).
3. **Conflating legacy and leverage**, i.e. treating a legacy statement as evidence that leverage will occur (Chalip; Smith).
4. Treating **expenditure as GSP or welfare**; adding tourism and construction effects together (double counting); treating transfers from other governments as new money.
5. Using **I–O multipliers** without capacity constraints or opportunity cost (Dwyer et al.; Crompton 1995).
6. Uncritical reliance on **commissioned ex ante studies** (Crompton 2006; Cour des comptes).
7. **Metric and horizon mixing**: OVE versus total spend, with or without students; 2030 versus 2032 versus 2042 versus 2045.
8. Citing **superseded targets** (TT32's $44bn; THRIVE's original student-inclusive definition and $100bn regional figure) as current.
9. Reporting **"jobs"** where sources mean FTE job-years.
10. Assuming image converts into arrivals without mechanism or evidence; generalising from outlier cases ("Barcelona effect").
11. Ignoring **seasonality and capacity** (winter Games; the SEQ room gap).
12. Assuming **regional dispersal** without regional leverage means (Beesley & Chalip 2011; Misener et al. 2015).
13. Ignoring negative legacies: overtourism, local business displacement (Duignan et al. 2019), resident opposition (Paris 2024 survey).
14. Treating host-led legacy reporting as independent evaluation, given the OGI's replacement by host-led frameworks.
15. Failing to separate the **bid signal** from hosting effects (Rose & Spiegel 2011).
16. Not dating claims or tracking instrument versions; Elevate 2042's status after the 2025 reset is unverified.
17. For a decoupling thesis specifically: reading the *absence* of measured tourism outcomes as decoupling without checking whether monitoring simply hasn't occurred yet (horizon effect), or equating the existence of activity with coupling.

**(f) Interfaces with other personas**
- **Olympic governance and contracts expert (the other researcher):** who legally owns legacy and leverage obligations (Host City Contract, OCOG, GIICA, state agencies), and where accountability gaps create decoupling conditions (Leopkey & Parent 2017).
- **Neo-institutional / decoupling theorist:** supplies the policy–practice versus means–ends typology (Bromley & Powell 2012; Wijen 2014). The strategist supplies empirical indicators of the ends: OVE, dispersal, image.
- **Qualitative document-analysis methodologist:** how to treat numbers in documents as institutional artefacts (provenance, performativity) rather than as facts; version control.
- **Public finance, audit and CBA expert:** opportunity cost, Treasury frameworks, audit evidence (QAO; Cour des comptes analogue).
- **Urban and regional planning expert:** SEQ City Deal, regional connectivity, accommodation supply.
- **Sport participation and social legacy expert:** shared leverage means (Chalip 2006 social leverage; Misener et al. 2015).
- **Sustainability expert:** triple-bottom-line leverage (O'Brien & Chalip 2008); sustainability evaluation (Müller et al. 2021).
- **First Nations engagement expert:** Elevate 2042's First Nations foundation and tourism roadmap.
- **Accessibility and inclusion expert:** Elevate 2042's accessibility foundation; accessible tourism.
- **Resident and community perspective:** support, avoidance and runaway behaviour (Kaplanidou 2012/2013; Paris survey).
- **Critical political economy of mega-events:** the mega-event syndrome (Müller 2015); cost overruns (Flyvbjerg et al. 2021).
- **AQF Level 10 examiner:** a significant, original contribution with policy relevance. The strategist's output feeds the "significance and impact" criterion.

### Gaps
- The persona spec is a synthesis (inference). There is no single published "competency framework" for tourism legacy strategists, and none was found.
