## Q1. Persona role and behaviour (objectives a and b): what the expert does, and how they build, appraise, read, document and question a corpus

### Takeaway
The persona owns the documentary method for the PhD. It decides which records exist and which are missing, how each record was produced and what it does, and how to read across linked genres (legislation → strategy → budget → report → audit) to infer latent meaning and means–ends decoupling. It never treats a document as a transparent window onto practice. Its working style combines source criticism (Scott; AACODS), structured extraction (READ; Bowen), systematic coding (Schreier/Mayring/directed QCA) and critical-interpretive reading (WPR, CDA, silences), all under an explicit audit trail.

### Cited Findings
- [V-web] Bowen defines document analysis as a qualitative procedure for reviewing and evaluating printed and electronic documents. He covers the nature and forms of documents, the method's advantages and limitations, and illustrates it with a grounded theory study. The paper targets research novices and is practical. — [Bowen 2009, QRJ 9(2):27–40, doi:10.3316/QRJ0902027](https://www.doi.org/10.3316/QRJ0902027)
- [V-cit] Bowen (2009) describes an iterative process of **skimming (superficial examination), reading (thorough examination) and interpretation**. It combines elements of content analysis and thematic analysis. He lists five functions of documents in research: context, questions to ask, supplementary data, tracking change and development, and verification/corroboration. He warns of "insufficient detail", "low retrievability" and "biased selectivity". — Bowen, G. A. (2009). Document analysis as a qualitative research method. *Qualitative Research Journal*, 9(2), 27–40. https://doi.org/10.3316/QRJ0902027
- [V-web] The READ approach has four steps: **R**eady your materials, **E**xtract data, **A**nalyse data, **D**istil findings. It addresses epistemological issues such as how documents are socially constructed and how they function in modern bureaucracies. It is illustrated with Pakistan and Niger case studies, with coding tools supplied as supplementary files. Stated uses: understanding policy content across time and geographies, documenting processes, and triangulating with interviews. — [Dalglish, Khalid & McMahon 2020, *Health Policy and Planning* 35(10):1424–1431 (PMC7886435)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7886435/)
- [V-web] Morgan (2022) notes that the literature on documentary research is scant. It sets out advantages and limitations of documents as data and strategies for selecting documents, argues that **reflexive thematic analysis suits document analysis**, and gives a hypothetical worked example. — [Morgan 2022, *The Qualitative Report* 27(1):64–77, doi:10.46743/2160-3715/2022.5044](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/tqr/vol27/iss1/4/); [USM repository record](https://aquila.usm.edu/fac_pubs/20228)
- [V-web] Wood and Sebar (TQR vol. 25, issue 2) apply a QDA framework to a case study of women entrepreneurs in rural Australia. They argue that rigour comes from **naming assumptions and being explicit about procedures**, and that QDA sits inside a conceptual framework integral to the credibility of the qualitative "story". — [The Qualitative Report 25(2), article 11](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/tqr/vol25/iss2/11)
- [V-web] Scott's four quality criteria, from the book's own table of contents: **Authenticity (soundness and authorship); Credibility (sincerity and accuracy); Representativeness (survival and availability); Meaning (literal and interpretative understanding).** — [Scott 1990, *A Matter of Record*, Polity: Wiley table of contents](https://gov.wiley.com/WileyCDA/WileyTitle/productCd-0745600700,descCd-tableOfContents.html); [OUP lecture transcript paraphrasing Scott on credibility](https://arc2.oup-arc.com/protected/files/content/file/1562166645454-Clip-4-Transcript.pdf)
- [V-web] Prior argues that social scientists have treated documents mainly as evidence and as containers of fixed content. He urges a shift from content to **what documents do**, treating them as active agents in dynamic networks. — [Prior 2008, *Sociology* 42(5):821–836 (SAGE study-site copy)](https://studysites.sagepub.com/gray3e/study/chapter19/Book%20chapters/Repositioning_Documents.pdf)
- [V-web] Practice-oriented document analysis (Asdal & Reinertsen) is built on six concepts: **document sites, tools, work, texts, issues and movements**. It includes guidance on digital documents and ethics and studies documents "in practice and as practice". — [SAGE book page](https://www.sagepub.com/en-us/cab/doing-document-analysis/book276193); [UiO TIK project page](https://sv.uio.no/tik/english/research/projects/sts-methods-lab/p-o-d-a.html)

### Inferences
- [INF] **Role statement for the persona:** "Methodological custodian of the documentary evidence base." The persona (1) maps the document universe for Brisbane 2032 governance and legacy; (2) builds and versions a corpus register; (3) appraises every source for authenticity, credibility, representativeness and meaning; (4) designs a theory-informed but open coding frame (neo-institutional constructs such as stated goals, means, ceremonial conformity, legitimacy talk, monitoring and recoupling); (5) reads for latent meaning, silences and intertextual borrowing; (6) builds temporal goal–means–activity–outcome chains to test for decoupling; (7) keeps an audit trail an examiner could re-trace.
- [INF] **Typical workflow** (synthesised from Bowen, READ, Altheide, Schreier and Scott):
  1. Fix the research questions and the role documents play in them (Bowen's five functions).
  2. Draw a "genre map" of issuing bodies and document types.
  3. Write a documented search strategy (parliament tabled papers, Hansard, budget papers, audit offices, IOC/OCOG publications, RTI disclosure logs, web archives).
  4. Set inclusion and exclusion rules.
  5. Register each document with provenance metadata (author or issuer, date, version, genre, audience, legal status, access route, URL or archive capture, file hash).
  6. Appraise (Scott, AACODS).
  7. Skim → read → interpret.
  8. Extract into a READ-style matrix.
  9. Pilot the coding frame, then do the main coding.
  10. Do interpretive passes (WPR/CDA/silences).
  11. Compare across documents and over time.
  12. Memo throughout.
  13. Triangulate with other data.
  14. Report with document IDs, quotation conventions and appendices.
- [INF] **Questions this persona habitually asks:**
  - Who wrote this, for whom, and under what mandate and constraints?
  - What genre conventions shape what can be said here (for example, audit "limited assurance" wording, or explanatory-note templates)?
  - Which version is this, and what changed from the last one?
  - What does this document *do*: bind, legitimate, defer, signal compliance, allocate?
  - What is absent that genre or theory would lead us to expect?
  - Where else does this wording appear (IOC templates, earlier Games, other jurisdictions)?
  - What would we expect to see in the documents if decoupling *were not* occurring?
  - Is this silence a product of secrecy rules (Cabinet confidentiality, RTI exemptions) rather than a substantive absence?

## Q2. Foundational and current methods texts for documentary research (Bowen, Scott, Prior, Atkinson & Coffey, Coffey 2014, Altheide, Morgan, Dalglish, Cardno, Wood et al., 2020–2026)

### Takeaway
The canon runs in three strands. **Source-critical** work (Scott 1990) supplies quality criteria. **Constructionist/"documentary realities"** work (Atkinson & Coffey; Coffey 2014; Prior 2003, 2008; Asdal & Reinertsen 2022) treats documents as social products and actors. **Procedural/applied** guides (Bowen 2009; Altheide's ECA; Cardno 2018; Dalglish et al. 2020; Wood et al. 2020; Morgan 2022) give step-by-step methods. Work from 2020–2026 adds structured health-policy protocols, practice-oriented analysis, Hansard-specific rigour, and early, contested LLM-assisted coding.

## Q3. Qualitative content analysis traditions on manifest and latent meaning (Mayring; Schreier; Hsieh & Shannon; Graneheim & Lundman; Elo & Kyngäs; Krippendorff)

### Takeaway
QCA traditions place manifest and latent meaning on a continuum of abstraction and interpretation. Manifest analysis describes what the text visibly says (meaning units → condensation → codes → categories). Latent analysis interprets underlying meaning (→ themes). The deeper the interpretation, the more the analyst must show the logic chain from text to theme. Directed (deductive) QCA suits theory-led coding of neo-institutional constructs. Summative QCA shows how keyword counts can open into latent interpretation.

### Cited Findings
- [V-web] Graneheim & Lundman (2004) define manifest and latent content, meaning units, condensation, abstraction and theme, and propose trustworthiness criteria (credibility, dependability, transferability). Their interpretive stance draws on Watzlawick et al. — [PubMed-derived record via science.gov](https://www.science.gov/topicpages/q/qualitative+content+analysis). Full citation [V-cit]: Graneheim, U. H., & Lundman, B. (2004). *Nurse Education Today*, 24(2), 105–112. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nedt.2003.10.001
- [V-web] Graneheim, Lindgren & Lundman (2017) contrast phenomenological description of **manifest** content with hermeneutic interpretation of **latent** content. They cover inductive, deductive and abductive approaches, and argue that **greater abstraction and interpretation makes credibility harder to show**, so authors must demonstrate the logic linking categories and themes to the aim. They distinguish QCA from thematic analysis mainly by whether counting is appropriate. — [Record (Nurse Educ Today 56:29–34)](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-28651100)
- [V-web] A 2020 follow-up by Lindgren, Lundman & Graneheim on abstraction and interpretation exists. — [course copy](https://m2.teluq.ca/pluginfile.php/556067/mod_folder/content/0/Module%205/Lindgren%20et%20al.%20%282020%29.pdf). Citation [V-cit]: Lindgren, B.-M., Lundman, B., & Graneheim, U. H. (2020). Abstraction and interpretation during the qualitative content analysis process. *International Journal of Nursing Studies*, 108, 103632.
- [V-web] Mayring's open-access monograph: *Qualitative content analysis: theoretical foundation, basic procedures and software solution* (Klagenfurt, 2014, 143 pp., CC BY-NC-ND), with companion free software QCAmap. — [SSOAR record 39517](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/handle/document/39517); [Klagenfurt QCA homepage](https://qualitative-content-analysis.uni-klu.ac.at)
- [V-cit] Mayring, P. (2000). Qualitative content analysis. *Forum Qualitative Sozialforschung / FQS*, 1(2), Art. 20. https://doi.org/10.17169/fqs-1.2.1089. Core procedures: **inductive category development** and **deductive category application**. Rule-guided, step models with defined units (coding, context, recording units), revision loops and intercoder checks. Basic techniques are summary, explication and structuring.
- [V-cit] Schreier, M. (2012). *Qualitative Content Analysis in Practice*. London: Sage. QCA is systematic, flexible and data-reducing. Its centre is the **coding frame**: main and subcategories, mixing concept-driven and data-driven categories. Steps: build the frame → segment → trial coding (pilot) → evaluate and modify the frame (consistency, validity) → main coding → present results. Categories should be one-dimensional, mutually exclusive and exhaustive.
- [V-cit] Hsieh, H.-F., & Shannon, S. E. (2005). Three approaches to qualitative content analysis. *Qualitative Health Research*, 15(9), 1277–1288. https://doi.org/10.1177/1049732305276687.
  - **Conventional**: categories derived inductively.
  - **Directed**: starts from existing theory or research for initial codes, extends or tests theory.
  - **Summative**: counts keywords/content, then interprets underlying (latent) context.
- [V-cit] Elo, S., & Kyngäs, H. (2008). The qualitative content analysis process. *Journal of Advanced Nursing*, 62(1), 107–115. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2648.2007.04569.x. Three phases (preparation, organising, reporting). Inductive route: open coding, grouping, categorisation, abstraction. Deductive route: a **categorisation matrix** (structured or unconstrained). Also discusses whether to analyse latent content (silence, sighs, posture) as well as manifest content.
- [V-cit] Krippendorff, K. (2019). *Content Analysis: An Introduction to Its Methodology* (4th ed.). Thousand Oaks, CA: Sage. Content analysis is a technique for making replicable and valid **inferences from texts to the contexts of their use**. Texts have no single meaning independent of readers. Analysts need an explicit analytical construct linking text to context. Reliability is assessed with Krippendorff's alpha.
- [V-cit] Potter, W. J., & Levine-Donnerstein, D. (1999). Rethinking validity and reliability in content analysis. *Journal of Applied Communication Research*, 27(3), 258–284. Distinguishes **manifest**, **latent-pattern** and **projective** content, which need different validity logics. This is useful for justifying latent coding.
- [V-cit] Kuckartz, U., & Rädiker, S. (2023). *Qualitative Content Analysis: Methods, Practice and Software* (2nd ed.). London: Sage. Thematic, evaluative and type-building QCA variants with CAQDAS support.
- [V-cit] Historians' tradition on latent meaning: Marwick's distinction between **"witting" and "unwitting" testimony**, and Bloch's "witnesses in spite of themselves". Documents disclose information their authors did not intend to convey. — Marwick, A. (2001). *The New Nature of History*. Basingstoke: Palgrave; Bloch, M. (1954). *The Historian's Craft*. Manchester University Press.

### Inferences
- [INF] **Recommended hybrid for the PhD:**
  1. A directed/deductive coding frame (Hsieh & Shannon; Schreier; Mayring's deductive application) operationalising neo-institutional constructs: formal goals/ends; means (resources, structures, KPIs); ceremonial/legitimacy language; monitoring/verification; coupling signals; recoupling.
  2. Inductive extension for tourism legacy and leverage themes not predicted by theory.
  3. A latent/interpretive pass (Graneheim & Lundman themes; WPR/CDA) on a theoretically sampled sub-corpus.
- [INF] Summative counting (for example, frequency of "legacy", "leverage", "sustainab*", "value for money" across versions of strategies and Hansard) is useful as a *signal* of rhetorical inflation or drift. It must not be read as importance or implementation. Latent interpretation should follow.
- [INF] Interface with the RTA persona: QCA's coding-frame reliability logic (intercoder agreement) conflicts with reflexive TA's rejection of coding reliability (Braun & Clarke). The thesis must choose explicitly, for example QCA for the manifest extraction layer and RTA for latent theme development, and justify the mix.

## Q4. Interpretive and critical approaches to policy and legislative texts (Bacchi's WPR; Fairclough's CDA; intertextuality; silences and absences; reading against the grain)

### Takeaway
WPR treats proposed solutions as revealing implicit "problem representations" and asks directly where the silences lie. CDA links textual features to discursive and social practice, and through *practical argumentation* (Fairclough & Fairclough) gives a ready grammar for reconstructing goal–means claims in policy texts. That makes it the most direct bridge to means–ends decoupling. Reading for silences (Trouillot) and against or along the grain (Benjamin; Stoler) turns absence into data. Absence must still be distinguished from secrecy and from archival artefact.

### Cited Findings
- [V-web] WPR starts from the premise that what one proposes to do about something reveals what one thinks is problematic. Policies therefore contain implicit problem representations open to analysis. — [University of Adelaide WPR page](https://www.adelaide.edu.au/graduatecentre/career-development/online-training/resources-tools/the-wpr-approach)
- [V-web] WPR Question 4: "What is left unproblematic in this problem representation? Where are the silences? Can the 'problem' be thought about differently?" The approach also asks analysts to apply the questions to their own proposals (self-problematisation). — [Adelaide WPR summary PDF](https://www.adelaide.edu.au/graduate-research/ua/media/162/wpr-summary.pdf); [Bacchi-derived table, *Globalization and Health*](https://globalizationandhealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12992-018-0443-9/tables/1)
- [V-web] Primary source: Bacchi, C. (2009). *Analysing Policy: What's the Problem Represented to Be?* Frenchs Forest, NSW: Pearson Australia. — [VOCEDplus record](https://www.voced.edu.au/content/ngv%3A52583)
- [V-cit] The six WPR questions:
  1. What's the problem represented to be?
  2. What presuppositions or assumptions underlie it?
  3. How has this representation come about?
  4. What is left unproblematic / where are the silences?
  5. What effects are produced?
  6. How and where is it produced, disseminated and defended, and how could it be disrupted?
  
  Bacchi & Goodwin add a step applying the questions to one's own problem representations. — Bacchi, C., & Goodwin, S. (2016). *Poststructural Policy Analysis: A Guide to Practice*. New York: Palgrave Macmillan.
- [V-cit] Fairclough's three-dimensional CDA model analyses text (vocabulary, grammar, cohesion, structure), **discursive practice** (production, distribution, consumption, including intertextuality) and **social practice** (ideology, hegemony). It distinguishes **manifest intertextuality** (explicit presence of other texts) from **constitutive intertextuality / interdiscursivity** (mixing of genres and discourses). — Fairclough, N. (1992). *Discourse and Social Change*. Cambridge: Polity; Fairclough, N. (2003). *Analysing Discourse: Textual Analysis for Social Research*. London: Routledge.
- [V-cit] Fairclough & Fairclough's **practical argumentation** model reconstructs political texts as arguments from a **goal premise**, **circumstantial premise**, **value premise** and **means–goal premise** to a **claim for action**, then evaluates them critically, including by considering consequences and alternatives. — Fairclough, I., & Fairclough, N. (2012). *Political Discourse Analysis: A Method for Advanced Students*. London: Routledge.

## Q5. Genre-specific analysis: legislation and explanatory notes, Hansard, strategies, intergovernmental agreements, audit and performance audit reports, review reports, bid books and host contracts, RTI/FOI releases

## Q6. Corpus construction and appraisal (purposive/theoretical sampling, inclusion criteria, provenance and versioning, grey literature appraisal such as AACODS, audit trail)

### Takeaway
A defensible corpus is **designed, not found**. It needs an explicit document universe (genre map), purposive or theoretical sampling logic, written inclusion and exclusion rules, a provenance and version register, systematic appraisal (Scott's four criteria; AACODS for grey literature), and an audit trail recording every decision. Scott's "representativeness (survival and availability)" and Bowen's "biased selectivity" warn that the corpus is shaped by what organisations choose to produce, publish and keep.

### Cited Findings
- [V-web] AACODS (Tyndall, Flinders University, 2010) appraises grey literature on **Authority, Accuracy, Coverage, Objectivity, Date, Significance**. Example prompts: who is responsible and what are their credentials; if there is no named author, check the organisation's impartiality; is the item opinion or factual; does it have a stated methodology; was it peer-reviewed. Grey literature usually lacks peer-review quality checks. — [AACODS checklist PDF (Oxford Health copy)](https://oxfordhealth.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/01/AACODS_Checklist.pdf); [Monash grey literature guide](https://guides.lib.monash.edu/grey-literature/evaluatinggeyliterature). (Sources differ on dating, 2008 conference talk vs 2010 checklist; cite as Tyndall, J. (2010). *AACODS Checklist*. Flinders University.)
- [V-web] Scott's representativeness criterion turns on **survival and availability**; authenticity on soundness and authorship; credibility on sincerity and accuracy; meaning on literal and interpretative understanding. — [Wiley TOC](https://gov.wiley.com/WileyCDA/WileyTitle/productCd-0745600700,descCd-tableOfContents.html)
- [V-web] Leopkey & Parent's Olympic legacy study used multiple document types (bid documents, candidature files, final reports, websites) as primary data. This is a model of multi-genre corpus construction for mega-event institutionalisation. — [BISp SURF](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201305003815)

- [V-cit] Altheide's ECA recommends **theoretical sampling** of documents (sampling to develop emerging concepts, comparing extremes), alongside a revisable protocol. — Altheide (1987); Altheide & Schneider (2013).
- [V-cit] Audit trail and confirmability: Lincoln, Y. S., & Guba, E. G. (1985). *Naturalistic Inquiry*. Beverly Hills, CA: Sage.   - **Tier 1, constitutive/legal:** Arrangements Act 2021 and amendments, explanatory notes, statements of compatibility, committee reports on Bills, Host Contract (if public), IGAs.

## Q7. Detecting means–ends or policy–practice decoupling empirically through documents (stated goals vs allocated means vs reported activities vs audited outcomes over time), with published examples

### Takeaway
Bromley & Powell distinguish **policy–practice decoupling** (policies adopted but not implemented) from **means–ends decoupling** (policies implemented but loosely connected to the outcomes they are meant to achieve). In document analysis, decoupling becomes visible by building **longitudinal goal → means → activity → outcome chains** from different genres and different authors, then comparing them. Ends come from legislation and strategies; means from budgets, IGAs and KPIs; activities from reports and Hansard; outcomes from audits and evaluations. Published mega-event studies document "rhetoric vs reality" gaps in sustainability and legacy, but few explicitly operationalise decoupling through documents. This is a methodological contribution the thesis can claim.

### Cited Findings
**Theory anchors**
- [V-cit] Meyer, J. W., & Rowan, B. (1977). Institutionalized organizations: Formal structure as myth and ceremony. *American Journal of Sociology*, 83(2), 340–363. https://doi.org/10.1086/226550. Formal structures adopted for legitimacy can be decoupled from technical activity.
- [V-cit] Bromley, P., & Powell, W. W. (2012). From smoke and mirrors to walking the talk: Decoupling in the contemporary world. *Academy of Management Annals*, 6(1), 483–530. https://doi.org/10.5465/19416520.2012.684462. Introduces **means–ends decoupling** and argues it is becoming more prevalent than policy–practice decoupling as monitoring intensifies.
- [V-web] A 2025 case paper summarises means–ends decoupling as policies genuinely implemented and activity genuinely occurring, but disconnected from the intended outcome. It notes Bromley & Powell's argument that policy–practice gaps have become less common as monitoring has intensified. — [IJMSIT, "Managing the Stock, Not the Capability"](https://journal.lembagakita.com/ijmsit/article/view/8595)
- [V-cit] Further anchors:
  - Recoupling: Hallett, T. (2010). The myth incarnate: Recoupling processes, turmoil, and inhabited institutions in an urban elementary school. *American Sociological Review*, 75(1), 52–74.
  - Loose coupling: Weick, K. E. (1976). Educational organizations as loosely coupled systems. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 21(1), 1–19.

- [V-cit] Mega-event leverage claims can serve as "convenient justification" for hosting rather than as implemented strategies. This is a leverage-specific decoupling argument. — Smith, A. (2014). Leveraging sport mega-events: New model or convenient justification? *Journal of Policy Research in Tourism, Leisure and Events*, 6(1), 15–30.

### Inferences
- [INF] **Operational protocol, the "ends–means–activities–outcomes ledger".** For each stated legacy or governance goal *G*, build one row per time point (for example, 2021 Act; 2023 legacy strategy; 2024 QAO; 2025 review and amendments; annual budgets 2021–26), with columns for:
  1. **End** as stated (verbatim, Doc ID, author, date).
  2. **Means** allocated: funding line, accountable body, statutory power, KPI.
  3. **Activities** reported: annual report, Hansard, estimates.
  4. **Outcome/verification**: audit or evaluation findings, assurance level.
  5. **Coupling judgement** with an evidence grade.
  6. **Alternative explanations** considered.
- [INF] **Decoupling signatures to code for:**
  - (a) *Orphan ends*: goals with no owner, funding or KPI → policy–practice decoupling risk.
  - (b) *Output-for-outcome substitution*: KPIs measure activity counts (events held, plans produced) rather than the end (visitor yield, regional dispersal, accessibility) → means–ends decoupling.
  - (c) *Verification rituals*: audit or report text attests to the existence of plans, frameworks or governance structures (cf. "limited assurance" and "no evidence ... ineffective") rather than outcomes (Power).
  - (d) *Goal drift and re-labelling* across versions (for example, "legacy" redefined, targets softened, baselines changed) without explanation.
  - (e) *Recommendation recurrence*: the same audit recommendation repeated across reports, or accepted but not actioned (agency responses vs follow-up audits).
  - (f) *Template isomorphism*: legacy and sustainability wording copied from IOC or prior-host documents with no local means attached (Fairclough intertextuality).
  - (g) *Restructuring as recoupling or as ceremony*: new bodies (GIICA) and new plans after reviews. Code whether they changed means or only talk.
- [INF] **Falsification discipline:** for each signature, specify disconfirming evidence (for example, funded KPIs aligned to outcome measures, independent evaluation showing goal attainment) and look for it with equal effort. Distinguish **implementation lag** (normal for a 2032 project in 2026), **loose coupling** (designed flexibility) and **deliberate decoupling**. Documents alone rarely show intent, so triangulate with elite interviews or RTI material where possible (cf. the procurement counter-example).
- [INF] Fairclough & Fairclough's means–goal premise and Bacchi's Q4 together give a principled way to say *what* the ends and means claims are and *what is not problematised*. The ledger then tests them over time.

Search did not surface the term "decoupling" in mega-event document studies. This is likely a real gap and a thesis opportunity [INF], but search coverage was limited.

## Q8. Ethics of documents-only research (public availability, NHMRC National Statement exemptions, RTI-released material)

### Takeaway
In Australia, documents-only research using publicly available, non-identifiable records is usually **lower risk and potentially exempt** from HREC review under the National Statement (2023). Exemption is an institutional decision made *before* research starts, not a researcher's self-determination. Research using **personal information without consent cannot be exempted**. RTI releases, inquiry submissions from private individuals and documents naming junior officials raise privacy, reputational and representational issues that ethics review or a documented ethics rationale should address.

### Cited Findings
- [V-web] National Statement (2023) **para 5.1.17**: lower-risk research that satisfies one or more of conditions (a)–(d) may be exempt. The conditions cover (among others) collections where personal identifiers were removed before researchers received them and researchers commit not to re-identify, surveys/observation of public behaviour, training programs, and **publicly available information**. Paragraph 5.1.16 states that research using personal information **without consent cannot be granted an exemption**, because a consent waiver requires an ethics review body. — [Griffith University exemption guidance](https://www.griffith.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0034/1949074/Work-that-is-exempt-from-research-ethics-review-at-Griffith-University-v1.2.pdf); [RMIT exemption template](https://researchethics.rmit.edu.au/Personalisation/DownloadTemplate/86); [St Andrew's Hospital exemption form (Jun 2024)](https://sah.org.au/media/documents/research/apply-to-be-exempt-from-ethical-review-v5,-jun-2024.pdf). Check the verbatim wording on the NHMRC site, reportedly pp. 87–88 of the 2023 edition.)

### Inferences
- [INF] **Practical ethics position for the thesis:**
  1. Apply for a formal exemption or low-risk determination *before* data collection, citing 5.1.17's publicly available information condition.
  2. Document the ethics reasoning in the methods chapter even if exempt.
  3. Name senior public officials and ministers acting in official roles. Consider not naming junior officers and private submitters to inquiries.
  4. Do not attempt to re-identify redacted individuals in RTI releases.
  5. Store RTI correspondence and releases securely, with provenance.
  6. Note copyright and licence status: many Queensland government documents carry Creative Commons licences [UNC]. Research quotation is generally covered by fair dealing for research or study under the *Copyright Act 1968* (Cth) [V-cit: s 40].
- [INF] If the researcher *files* RTI requests, the requests themselves are an intervention ("data production" per Walby & Larsen). Reflexive notes should record how request wording may shape what is released, and the potential "Hawthorne-like" effect on agencies.
- [INF] Pairing documents with interviews takes the project out of exemption territory and requires full HREC review.

**(e) Red flags and failure modes the persona watches for**
1. [INF] **Transparent-window fallacy:** inferring practice directly from policy text (Atkinson & Coffey; Prior). *Fix:* treat documents as social products and actors, and triangulate.
2. [INF] **Provenance and purpose blindness:** ignoring authorship, audience and genre constraints. For example, reading a ministerial media release, an OCOG annual report and a QAO report as equivalent "evidence". *Fix:* Scott/AACODS appraisal recorded per document.
3. [INF] **Selection, survival and availability bias:** only publicly posted, "good news" or surviving documents (Scott's representativeness; Bowen's "biased selectivity"). *Fix:* genre map, RTI, comparators, explicit statement of what could not be obtained.
4. [INF] **Ignoring silences, or over-reading them:** failing to ask what is missing (Bacchi Q4; Trouillot), or treating every absence as meaningful when it reflects Cabinet confidentiality, RTI exemptions or timing. *Fix:* expectation baselines and source-of-silence coding.
5. [INF] **Anachronism and version confusion:** quoting superseded venue plans, budgets or Acts; reading 2026 KPIs back onto 2021 goals. *Fix:* capture-and-hash register; time-stamped ledger.
6. [INF] **Legal-status errors:** treating a Bill, draft strategy or consultation paper as enacted or adopted policy.
7. [INF] **Hansard-as-fact:** quoting parliamentary assertions as evidence of outcomes rather than as claims.
8. [INF] **Misreading audit genre:** treating "limited assurance" or "no evidence ... ineffective" as a positive finding of effectiveness; treating acceptance of recommendations as implementation; skipping scope exclusions and agency responses.
9. [INF] **Counting as meaning:** equating keyword frequency (summative QCA) with importance or implementation.
10. [INF] **Decontextualised coding:** fragmenting documents so that genre, sequence and intertextual chains are lost (cf. Prior's emphasis on function and networks).
11. [INF] **Confirmation bias toward decoupling:** finding decoupling everywhere; not distinguishing implementation lag, loose coupling, recoupling and deliberate ceremony; no search for disconfirming evidence.
12. [INF] **Conflating decoupling types:** mixing policy–practice with means–ends decoupling (Bromley & Powell).
13. [INF] **Secondary-source substitution:** relying on media summaries of audit or review reports instead of the primary report (a risk visible in this very session, where the VAGO material came via news).
14. [INF] **Multimodal neglect:** ignoring maps, renders, infographics and tables in bid and legacy documents, which carry much of the persuasive load.
15. [INF] **Paradigm muddle:** blending QCA reliability logics with RTA's reflexive stance, or realist decoupling claims with poststructural WPR, without positioning.
16. [INF] **Uncritical LLM use:** undisclosed or unvalidated automated coding of latent meaning.
17. [INF] **Ethics complacency:** assuming "public = no ethics", naming private submitters or junior officers, or attempting to de-redact RTI material.
18. [INF] **Moving-target risk:** Brisbane 2032 is ongoing, so findings must be bounded by a documented cut-off date, with interpretation framed as "decoupling *to date*".
