## Q1. Persona role, behaviours and skills (objectives a, b, d)

### Takeaway
The Mega-Event Governance Expert is the panel's "delivery realist". They make sure abstract institutional theorising (e.g., means–ends decoupling) is anchored in specific documents, parties, dates, contract clauses, statutes, budgets and audit findings. They ask of every claim: *which document, which version, which party is bound, who bears the risk, and is this a promise, a plan, or an outcome?*

### Cited Findings
**Why this persona is needed.** The empirical object is a dense, multi-party contractual and statutory web:
- The Brisbane 2032 Olympic Host Contract was executed in Tokyo on 21 July 2021 by four parties: the IOC (Thomas Bach, Ser Miang Ng), Brisbane City Council (Cr Adrian Schrinner), the State of Queensland (Annastacia Palaszczuk) and the Australian Olympic Committee (John Coates) **[V]** — [BCC tabled document, 11 Feb 2025](https://docs.brisbane.qld.gov.au/Council%20and%20Committees/2025/02-Feb/11%20February%202025/Council/Tabled%20document%201.pdf); [IOC, Addendum No. 1 to the Olympic Host Contract](https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/Olympic-Games/Brisbane-2032/Host-Contract/Addendum-N-1-Olympic-Host-Contract.pdf).
- The Commonwealth of Australia is **not** a party to the Host Contract. Its role sits in a separate Commonwealth–Queensland intergovernmental agreement (IGA) **[V]** — [Brisbane Olympics 2032 Intergovernmental Agreement (federation.gov.au)](https://federation.gov.au/sites/default/files/about/agreements/brisbane-olympics-2032-intergovernmental-agreement.pdf).
- The Brisbane Organising Committee for the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games became a full party to the Host Contract through a joinder agreement dated 19 May 2022 **[V]** — [IOC Addendum No. 1](https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/Olympic-Games/Brisbane-2032/Host-Contract/Addendum-N-1-Olympic-Host-Contract.pdf).
- The Queensland Audit Office (QAO) found that the responsible entities had not built a consolidated budget capturing all estimated Games delivery costs. It also found the state government "carries the greatest contractual and financial exposure" and has underwritten the Organising Committee's financial risk **[V]** — [QAO, *Preparing for the Brisbane Games*, Report 5: 2024–25, tabled 13 Dec 2024](https://www.qao.qld.gov.au/reports-resources/reports-parliament/preparing-brisbane-games); [tabled PDF](https://www.parliament.qld.gov.au/Work-of-the-Assembly/Tabled-Papers/docs/5824T0282/5824t282.pdf).

**What the persona checks in a thesis.** These are modelled on how auditors, inquiry committees and governance scholars work; see the sources under Q3, Q4 and Q6.
1. **Promise / plan / outcome discipline.** Bid-stage claims (Future Host questionnaire, masterplan) must be kept apart from contractual obligations (Host Contract), funded plans (IGA, budget papers, the 2032 Delivery Plan) and observed outcomes (audits, post-Games reviews). The Victorian Auditor-General's finding that the 2026 Commonwealth Games budget was "unrealistically low" while the later $6.9bn estimate was "overstated" shows that even official figures are claims to be tested **[V]** — [VAGO, *Withdrawal from the 2026 Commonwealth Games*, 20 Mar 2024](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/withdrawal-commonwealth-games-2026/).
2. **Contract hierarchy and parties.** Which instrument binds whom: Olympic Charter → Host Contract (Principles, Operational Requirements, addenda and joinder) → IGA → state enabling legislation → delivery-body charters and project agreements **[I, built on the [V] facts above]**.
3. **Accountability mapping.** Each function has a body: IOC (rights-holder), Coordination Commission (monitoring), OCOG (Games operations), GIICA (venue delivery), Queensland Government (underwriter, legislator), BCC (Host Contract party), Commonwealth (funder via IGA). The persona checks the thesis assigns responsibility to the right one **[I]**.
4. **Dating and versioning.** Brisbane's venue plan has changed at least three times (2021 bid → Feb 2023 Gabba rebuild → Mar 2024 QSAC plan → Mar 2025 Victoria Park plan). Every fact needs a date stamp and a "superseded by" flag **[V; see Q5]**.
5. **Budget scope.** The persona checks which cost envelope is meant: OCOG operating budget, the $7.1bn venue program, transport and other "Games-related" infrastructure, or security. Paris shows how totals swing with scope **[V; see Q4]**.
6. **Source independence.** Audit (QAO, VAGO, NAO, Cour des comptes) is ranked above government-commissioned review (Quirk 2024, 100 Day Review 2025), which is ranked above ministerial media statements and IOC press releases **[I]**.
7. **Political-cycle reading.** Decisions are read against elections and leadership changes: Queensland election 26 Oct 2024; IOC presidency change in June 2025 **[V-cit; see Q2, Q5]**.

**How the persona reads political and administrative context.**
- Reviews and "resets" are often triggered by changes of government or leader, and the persona treats them as political as well as technical events. In March 2025 Premier Crisafulli apologised for breaking his 2024 election promise that no new stadium would be built **[V]** — [ABC News, 25 Mar 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-03-25/brisbane-olympics-olympic-games-2032-stadium-premier-plans/105062348).
- The persona watches for "rule of exception" law. In 2025 Queensland legislated to let GIICA override 15 planning-related laws, including the Planning Act, Environmental Protection Act and Queensland Heritage Act **[V]** — [ABC News, 1 May 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-05-01/qld-parliament-to-override-planning-laws-2032-olympic-venues/105236370).

### Inferences
- **Core skills [I]:**
  - contract reading: incorporation by reference, joinder, addenda, termination and guarantee clauses;
  - statutory interpretation of enabling and amending Acts, explanatory notes and statements of compatibility;
  - public-finance literacy: capital vs operating, contingency, P50/P90, "funding envelope" vs cost estimate;
  - intergovernmental-relations literacy in Australian federalism;
  - audit literacy: "reasonable" vs "limited" assurance, scope limits;
  - chronology building and version control of plans;
  - knowing access routes for documents: tabled papers, RTI disclosure logs, Senate estimates.
- The persona should push the PhD to treat each document genre as an *institutional performance* with its own audience. Bid books persuade the IOC; Hansard justifies to voters; audits hold to account. That fits a decoupling analysis, because formal structures and statements are the "ceremonial" layer to compare against means and outcomes **[I]**.

## Q2. How is the IOC governance architecture built? (Charter; Host Contract; Agenda 2020 / 2020+5; New Norm; 2019 host-election reform; 2025–26 Coventry-era changes)

### Takeaway
The IOC governs host delivery through a layered, IOC-drafted contract under the Olympic Charter. Financial risk falls on host-side public parties. Since 2014 the IOC has reformed the *front end*: reduced bid costs, the "New Norm" cost-cutting measures, and a 2019 move from competitive bidding to non-public dialogue with a "preferred host". Under President Kirsty Coventry (in office from June 2025) the host-selection process was paused and redesigned. The new design adds a "Strategic Dialogue" stage and targets mid-2029 for electing the 2036 host. The basic risk allocation in the Host Contract has not visibly changed.

### Cited Findings
**Olympic Charter**
- The Charter codifies the Fundamental Principles, Rules and Bye-laws. Chapter 5 deals with the Olympic Games: celebration, election of the host (Rule 33 and its Bye-law), organising committee, liabilities, and so on **[V-cit]** — IOC, *Olympic Charter* (current edition at olympics.com; confirm the edition in force, as it is revised at most Sessions).
- The liabilities rule (historically Rule 36, "Liabilities – Withdrawal of the organisation of the Olympic Games") makes the NOC, OCOG and host jointly and severally liable for Games commitments. It also states the IOC bears no financial responsibility **[V-cit; U on the current rule number and wording]**. *A governance expert would check this clause in the current Charter and in Host Contract Principles before relying on it.*

**Host Contract architecture**
- From the 2024 edition onward the IOC split the Host City Contract into:
  - **HCC Principles** (the legal agreement: parties' rights, obligations, finance, termination, dispute resolution);
  - **HCC Operational Requirements** (technical and service-level requirements by functional area);
  - the Olympic Charter, incorporated by reference;
  - later directives and addenda.

  **[V-cit]**. For 2032 the instrument is styled the "Olympic Host Contract", reflecting that a "host" can now be more than one city, a region or a country (2019 reform) **[V-cit]**. Addendum No. 1 exists **[V]** — [IOC Addendum No. 1](https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/Olympic-Games/Brisbane-2032/Host-Contract/Addendum-N-1-Olympic-Host-Contract.pdf).
- The 2032 Host Contract parties and the 19 May 2022 OCOG joinder **[V]** — see Q1.
- **Risk allocation, Brisbane:** the state government underwrites the OCOG and carries the greatest contractual and financial exposure **[V]** — [QAO 2024](https://www.qao.qld.gov.au/reports-resources/reports-parliament/preparing-brisbane-games).
- **Human-rights, anti-corruption and labour clauses** were added to Host Contract Principles from the 2024 edition **[V-cit; U on the precise clause numbers]**.
- **Dispute resolution** clauses historically refer to arbitration (Court of Arbitration for Sport) under Swiss law **[U; confirm in the 2032 Principles]**.

**Olympic Agenda 2020 (2014)**
- 40 recommendations approved at the 127th IOC Session, Monaco, 8–9 December 2014. Bidding was recast as an "invitation", the evaluation stressed risks and opportunities, bid costs were cut, sustainability was built in, and the Host City Contract was to be published **[V-cit]** — IOC, *Olympic Agenda 2020: 20+20 Recommendations* (olympics.com).

**The "New Norm" (2018)**
- 118 measures to cut Games delivery cost and complexity, approved by the IOC Session in PyeongChang in February 2018 **[V-cit]**. The IOC's projected savings ("up to around USD 1bn" for Summer and about USD 0.5bn for Winter) are **IOC self-reported claims**, not independently audited outcomes **[U on exact figures]** — IOC, *Olympic Agenda 2020 – Olympic Games: The New Norm* (olympics.com).

**Olympic Agenda 2020+5 (2021)**
- 15 recommendations approved at the 137th IOC Session (virtual), 12 March 2021 **[V-cit]** — IOC, *Olympic Agenda 2020+5: 15 Recommendations* (olympics.com).

**2019 new approach to host election**
- Approved at the 134th Session, Lausanne, June 2019. It amended Olympic Charter Rule 33 and its Bye-law **[V-cit]**:
  - two Future Host Commissions (Summer, Winter) created as permanent bodies;
  - two dialogue stages: "continuous dialogue" (non-committal, ongoing) and "targeted dialogue" with one or more "preferred hosts";
  - no fixed seven-year election timetable;
  - hosts may be several cities, regions or countries.
- **Brisbane was the first Summer host selected this way** **[V-cit]**:
  - IOC Executive Board named it preferred host on 24 February 2021;
  - elected at the 138th Session in Tokyo on 21 July 2021 (72 yes, 5 no) **[V-cit; confirm vote count]**;
  - Host Contract signed the same day **[V]**.

**2025–2026 changes under Kirsty Coventry**
- Coventry was elected IOC President at the 144th Session (Costa Navarino, Greece) on 20 March 2025 and took office on 23 June 2025 **[V-cit]**.
- One early decision was "pausing the host election process for future Games, with a working group established to review the timeline and member engagement". The "Pause and Reflect" consultation was folded into a "Fit for the Future" strategy **[V]** — [The Rahnuma Daily / wire report](https://therahnuma.com/a-new-chapter-and-a-shift-in-tone-mark-coventrys-debut-as-ioc-president/); [Prokerala (same wire)](https://www.prokerala.com/news/articles/a1710930.html); [Inside the Games, "Coventry urges sport to adapt to new era"](https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/coventry-urges-sport-to-adapt-to-new-era).
- The IOC announced that the host selection process had been "made fit for the future" **[V; headline and summary]** — [IOC news](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/olympic-games-host-selection-process-made-fit-for-the-future); [Inside the Games, "IOC reshapes Olympic programme and host selection"](https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/ioc-reshapes-olympic-programme-and-host-selection). The announcement covered:
  - a new transitional stage called **"Strategic Dialogue"**;
  - a target to elect the **2036 host in mid-2029**;
  - Interested Parties to be shortlisted for Strategic Dialogue by the Executive Board in **March 2027**.
- GamesBids reported that the IOC named **seven applicants** for 2036, set a deadline after which no more Interested Parties would be considered (reported as 31 October [2026]), and scheduled presentations to the Future Host Commission for November **[V; secondary]** — [GamesBids](https://gamesbids.com/eng/summer-olympic-bids/is-it-enough-ioc-names-seven-applicants-and-deadline-for-2036-olympic-bid-but-what-comes-next-is-critical-to-restore-trust-and-transparency/). The same piece frames the reform as needing to "restore trust and transparency".
- **2030 Youth Olympic Games:** the election set for the 146th Session (June 2026) was put on hold by the Executive Board in May 2026 so that YOG objectives could be re-evaluated under Fit for the Future **[V; secondary]** — [Wikipedia, Bids for the 2030 Summer YOG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2030_Summer_Youth_Olympics).
- **Conflict:** sources disagree on whether the host-selection reform was approved at the 145th Session (Milan, Feb 2026) or the 146th (June 2026) **[U]**.
- **IOC Annual Report 2025, "Passing the Torch"** — primary source for the transition year **[V; existence]** — [IOC Annual Report 2025 PDF](https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/International-Olympic-Committee/Annual-report/IOC-Annual-Report-2025.pdf).
- **IOC Coordination Commission for Brisbane 2032:** Coventry chaired it until May 2025, when Mikaela (Mikee) Cojuangco Jaworski took over during the first in-country meeting **[V]** — [ABC News, 20 May 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-05-20/international-olympic-committee-members-brisbane-meeting-2032/105310080).

### Inferences
- **[I]** The 2019 reform shifted host selection from public, competitive candidature files to private, negotiated dialogue. The document trail moved accordingly: fewer public bid books, more IOC-authored Future Host Commission reports and preferred-host questionnaires. Coventry's "Strategic Dialogue" adds another stage that is largely non-public. For a document-based PhD this is itself a governance finding about transparency, and it limits the bid-era corpus available for 2036.
- **[I]** In institutional terms, Agenda 2020 and the New Norm are *formal structures* adopted by the IOC and imitated in host documents ("existing and temporary venues", "climate positive"). Means–ends decoupling can be tested by setting those *ends* against host-side *means* (venue decisions, budgets, procurement), not only against policy adoption. Brisbane's 2025 decision to build a new 63,000-seat stadium is an obvious test case.
- **[I]** The Host Contract's risk allocation (IOC as rights-holder with no financial liability; state underwriter) did not change with the reforms. A governance expert would say the reforms acted on cost *drivers* and *selection*, not on *who pays when costs overrun*.

### Gaps
- The text of the 2032 Host Contract Principles and Operational Requirements could not be retrieved. This leaves unconfirmed: the IOC financial contribution to the Brisbane OCOG, surplus-sharing, termination triggers, guarantee schedules, and the dispute-resolution clause.
- The exact Session numbers and dates of the Fit for the Future host-selection votes, and whether any changes reach the 2032 Host Contract (none were found), remain open.
- The current Olympic Charter edition and rule numbering need confirming.

## Q3. What does the core scholarship say (including 2022–2026 work)?

### Takeaway
The literature agrees broadly that Olympic delivery is structurally prone to cost overruns with fat-tailed risk. It also finds:
- hosts take on public risk under conditions of exception;
- legacy and sustainability claims run ahead of outcomes;
- the IOC has weak leverage to enforce its own reform goals.

Two lines of work give the decoupling lens its sharpest tools: governance and stakeholder research (Parent, Leopkey, Chappelet, Geeraert), and project and risk research (Flyvbjerg, Jennings).

### Cited Findings
**Cost and risk**
- **Flyvbjerg, B., Budzier, A., & Lunn, D. (2021).** Regression to the tail: Why the Olympics blow up. *Environment and Planning A: Economy and Space*, 53(2), 233–260. https://doi.org/10.1177/0308518X20958724
  - Average real cost overrun 172%; overruns found in every Games with data since 1960; overruns follow a fat-tailed (power-law) distribution, so the "average" is unstable and extreme blow-outs should be expected **[V-cit]**.
  - Governance implication: lock in budgets late, use reference-class forecasting and large contingencies, and do not believe "this time is different".
- **Flyvbjerg, B., Stewart, A., & Budzier, A. (2016).** *The Oxford Olympics Study 2016: Cost and Cost Overrun at the Games.* Saïd Business School Working Paper 2016-20 **[V-cit]**.
- **Flyvbjerg, B., & Gardner, D. (2023).** *How Big Things Get Done.* Currency (2022–23). A popular synthesis of reference-class forecasting and "think slow, act fast" **[V-cit]**.
- **Jennings, W. (2012).** *Olympic Risks.* Palgrave Macmillan **[V-cit]**. Related work:
  - Jennings, W. (2012). Why costs overrun: risk, optimism and uncertainty in budgeting for the London 2012 Olympic Games. *Construction Management and Economics*, 30(6), 455–462 **[V-cit]**.

## Q4. How did comparative delivery models work (Sydney 2000, London 2012, Paris 2024, LA 2028, Milano Cortina 2026), and what lessons do governance experts draw?

### Takeaway
Most hosts use a two-body structure: a public infrastructure or delivery authority and an organising committee for operations, wrapped in state guarantees and often special legislation. Independent audit institutions supply the most reliable cost record. Recurring lessons:
- "privately funded OCOG" narratives hide large public spending;
- cost totals depend entirely on scope;
- multi-site, multi-level Games (Milano Cortina, Victoria 2026) magnify coordination and accountability failures.

### Inferences
- **[I] Lessons governance experts draw:**
  1. The delivery-authority / OCOG split (OCA/SOCOG, ODA/LOCOG, SOLIDEO/COJOP, GIICA/Brisbane 2032 OCOG) separates infrastructure accountability from operations. It also splits *visibility*: an OCOG surplus (Paris €75.7m) can coexist with billions in public cost.
  2. Public backstops persist whatever the funding model (LA's "privately funded" Games still carries a City backstop).
  3. Supreme audit institutions (NAO, Cour des comptes, QAO, VAGO) are the decisive sources for "means vs ends". The Paris and Milano Cortina figures show that totals are contestable constructs.
  4. Legal exceptionalism is standard (London 2006 Act, Paris 2018 and 2023 laws, Brisbane 2025 planning overrides).
  5. Contested legal status of organising bodies (Milano Cortina) hides how public-law accountability is avoided. This is a decoupling mechanism in its own right.
- **[I]** For Brisbane the closest analogue is Sydney 2000: the same Westminster federal system, a state government underwriting the Games, and a strong Minister. London 2012 is the closest analogue for GIICA as a delivery authority.

## Q6. What does the 2023 cancellation of Victoria's 2026 Commonwealth Games, and its audit and inquiry reports, teach governance experts?

### Takeaway
Victoria 2026 is the Australian cautionary case of a regional, multi-hub event. It was committed to on an inadequate business case and an unrealistically low budget, then cancelled on an overstated estimate. Agencies failed to give frank, timely advice, and executive control obstructed scrutiny. The case shows how bid promises, cost estimates and withdrawal justifications are all political artefacts, and how several accountability forums with different powers produce different "truths".

### Cited Findings
- Premier Daniel Andrews withdrew in July 2023, citing costs that had risen to nearly $7bn. Victoria paid $380m in compensation to the Commonwealth Games bodies **[V]** — [Canberra Times](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/8341672/comm-games-could-be-pushed-to-2027-to-find-new-host/); [Government News](https://www.governmentnews.com.au/senior-victorian-bureaucrats-to-be-grilled-over-dumped-commonwealth-games/); [SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/commonwealth-games-inquiry-hears-of-cost-doubts-ignored-regions-and-wish-list-overlook/yh345jode).
- **VAGO, *Withdrawal from the 2026 Commonwealth Games*** (Independent assurance report to Parliament 2023–24: 13, 20 Mar 2024) **[V]** — [VAGO report page](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/withdrawal-commonwealth-games-2026/); [PDF](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-03/20240320_Withdrawal-from-2026-Commonwealth-Games.pdf); [Sports Technology summary](https://www.sportstechnology.ausleisure.com.au/news/victorias-auditor-general-finds-2026-commonwealth-games-cancellation-cost-the-state-more-than-589-million/). Findings:
  - the cost of bidding, planning and withdrawing was over **$589m**;
  - the business case was inadequate;
  - agencies did not always give government timely, full advice;
  - "the original Games budget was unrealistically low, but the $6.9 billion cost estimate the government publicly released when it withdrew from the Games was overstated";
  - the waste could have been avoided had agencies worked together to give frank and full advice.
- **Victorian Legislative Council Select Committee on the 2026 Commonwealth Games Bid**, final report (tabled around April 2025) **[V]** — [Parliament of Victoria PDF](https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/4a24c6/globalassets/tabled-paper-documents/tabled-paper-9090/lc-comm-games-bid-60-03_final-report.pdf); [APO](https://apo.org.au/node/330282); [ABC, 1 Apr 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-04-01/vic-victorian-2026-commonwealth-games-inquiry-findings/105123366).
  - **62 findings and 6 recommendations.**
  - The multi-city model was "overly ambitious and not feasible" in the time available.
  - It named Andrews, Jacinta Allan, Martin Pakula and Tim Pallas as sharing responsibility, and accused the government of impeding evidence-gathering.
  - It nonetheless concluded that withdrawing was the correct decision at that time.
  - Note: one search summary called this a Legislative *Assembly* committee. The URL ("lc-…") and Senate committee references confirm it is a Legislative **Council** committee.
- **Senate RRAT References Committee** (inquiry referred March 2023; chair Sen. Matt Canavan) **[V]** — [APH Appendix 3](https://aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Rural_and_Regional_Affairs_and_Transport/PreparednessforGames/Second_interim_report/Appendix_3_-_Committee_views_and_recommendations_from_the_first_interim_report); [SwimSwam](https://swimswam.com/senate-committee-recommends-australia-salvage-2026-commonwealth-games/); [Bunbury Herald](https://www.bunburyherald.com.au/news/interim-report-into-cancelled-2026-commonwealth-games-calls-for-urgent-intervention-from-federal-government-c-11902926).
  - Its interim report recommended the Australian Government take a facilitation and coordination role to "salvage" the Games.
  - Andrews and Allan declined to give evidence.
  - It urged Victoria to cooperate with the Legislative Council inquiry.
- Glasgow staged a scaled-down 2026 Commonwealth Games (July–August 2026) **[V-cit; not re-checked]**.

### Inferences
- **[I] Lessons for the governance persona:**
  1. *Budgets are political artefacts at both ends.* An underestimate wins the event, and an overestimate justifies exit. A thesis must never take either at face value.
  2. *Frank and fearless advice* is a measurable governance variable. Audit evidence on advice quality is direct evidence of whether formal processes (business cases, gateway reviews) were ceremonial.
  3. *Triangulate accountability forums:*
     - the auditor (independent, limited by scope and cabinet access);
     - an upper-house select committee (partisan, but with compulsory powers);
     - the Senate (federal, with weak reach into state cabinets).

     Each yields a different account.
  4. *Exit costs are contractual.* The $380m settlement shows the host-contract risk allocation working in practice. The Brisbane Host Contract would allocate termination risk in a similar way, but its clauses were not checked **[U]**.
  5. The same Senate committee examined both Victoria 2026 and Brisbane 2032 preparedness. That gives the PhD a federal document thread that links the two cases.
- **[I]** Victoria 2026 is the reference case for "regional dispersal" claims. Brisbane's statewide 17-venue program (Toowoomba, Gold Coast, Redlands and others) raises the same coordination risks.

### Gaps
- The Senate RRAT committee's *final* report and its relevant 2024–2026 findings on Brisbane could not be confirmed. Any Victorian Government response to the Legislative Council recommendations was not located.

## Q8. Red flags, failure modes, and interfaces with other personas (objectives e, f)

### Takeaway
The governance persona's main job on the panel is to stop category errors:
- treating promises as outcomes;
- treating the IOC as a delivery body;
- treating the Commonwealth as a Host Contract party;
- treating a funding envelope as a cost;
- treating a superseded plan as current.

It hands theory, legacy and method questions to the relevant specialist personas.

### Cited Findings
These are the factual anchors for the red flags. All are **[V]** with sources given in Q1–Q7.
- The Commonwealth is not a Host Contract party; the OCOG joined only in May 2022.
- The state underwrites the OCOG and carries the greatest exposure; there was no consolidated Games budget as of Dec 2024 (QAO).
- Venue plans were superseded in Mar 2024 and Mar 2025, and the Brisbane Arena was dropped in Mar 2025.
- Stadium estimates conflict ($3.4bn / $3.785bn / ~$3.6bn).
- Paris public-cost totals vary by scope and date (€3bn / about €6bn).
- VAGO showed both the low and the high Victoria 2026 estimates were wrong.
- 11 of 17 Brisbane venues were still in business case at 30 June 2025 (QAO Dec 2025).

### Inferences
**Red flags and failure modes [I]**

**Treating bid promises as outcomes.** For example, citing the bid's existing-venue share or "climate positive" as achieved. The persona responds: classify each statement as promise, plan or outcome, and require audit or outcome evidence before the PhD claims an "end" has (or has not) been met.

**Ignoring contract hierarchy and parties.** For example, saying the "IOC requires Australia to…"; in fact the obligations bind the IOC, Queensland, BCC, the AOC and the OCOG, and the Commonwealth only through the IGA. The persona would also ask whether the thesis has read the Host Contract Principles at all, rather than relying on press summaries.

**Misreading accountability.** For example:
- blaming the OCOG for venue overruns, when GIICA has delivered venues since 2025;
- treating Coordination Commission praise as assurance;
- crediting or blaming "Brisbane" when the decisions were the State's.

**Envelope = cost fallacy.** The $7.1bn is a funding envelope whose contents changed; it is not a measured cost. OCOG budgets, the venue envelope, transport and security must be kept separate.

**Superseded-arrangement errors.** For example, writing about the Gabba rebuild, QSAC athletics, the Brisbane Arena, or a pre-GIICA delivery authority as current. Every Brisbane fact needs a date and a status.

**Snapshot fallacy.** Analysing a moving project as if frozen. Set a corpus cut-off and an update protocol.

**Audit misuse.** For example, quoting QAO "limited assurance" conclusions as definitive, or ignoring audit scope limits and entity responses.

**Review naivety.** Taking the Quirk or 100 Day Review findings at face value without their terms of reference, timeframes, independence and political context. The 100 Day Review was run by GIICA, which then delivers the program.

**Decoupling inflation.** Labelling every change or overrun as "decoupling". Means–ends decoupling requires two things: evidence that formal policies were *adopted and implemented* (means), and evidence that the outcomes (ends) do not follow (Bromley & Powell 2012; Wijen 2014). Plain non-implementation is policy–practice decoupling, and reversal after an election may simply be political change. This is a joint check with the theory persona.

**IOC self-reporting as evidence.** For example, citing New Norm savings or Agenda 2020 "success" from IOC documents alone. Cross-check against independent work (Geeraert & Gauthier 2018; Müller et al. 2021; audits).

**Media figure laundering.** Repeating news-reported costs without tracing them to budget papers or audits.

**Overlooking the federal and multi-level dimension.** Ignoring the IGA, Senate scrutiny and council-delivered venues (e.g., the reported Gold Coast shortfall). The case is multi-level (Parent et al. 2011, 2017).

**Overlooking rule-of-exception law and rights.** Not analysing the 2025 planning overrides and the removal of appeal rights, or Human Rights Act compatibility statements (Müller 2015; Boykoff 2014).

**Interfaces with other personas [I]**

**Neo-institutional theory / decoupling expert.** Governance supplies concrete formal structures (Charter, Host Contract, New Norm, envelope) and evidence of means (procurement, budgets). Theory supplies the decoupling typology (policy–practice vs means–ends), institutional logics and legitimacy mechanisms, and checks against "decoupling inflation".

**Tourism legacy and leverage expert (covered by another researcher).** Governance hands over:
- which body owns legacy (the OCOG legacy function, the state's Elevate 2042 legacy strategy [U on title and date], and tourism agencies);
- the legacy and leverage commitments in the Host Contract and IGA.

It takes back evidence on whether tourism "ends" were resourced (means).

**Qualitative document-analysis methodologist.** Joint ground: corpus design (the genre table above), sampling and cut-off, document authenticity and versions, coding layers (formal / means / ends), and how to handle redacted RTI and conflicting figures.

**Public administration and Australian federalism / intergovernmental-relations expert.** IGAs, Commonwealth–state fiscal relations, statutory-authority design (GIICA), ministerial responsibility and cabinet confidentiality.

**Public-law and contracts expert.** Host Contract clause analysis (guarantees, termination, CAS/Swiss law), planning-override validity and Human Rights Act compatibility.

**Megaproject / cost-risk expert (Flyvbjerg tradition).** Reference-class forecasting, contingency, P50/P90 and the interpretation of overrun statistics.

**Urban planning and critical urban geography expert.** Victoria Park (a heritage-listed parkland and contested site), Müller's syndrome and Boykoff's celebration capitalism, and community contestation.

**Sustainability and climate governance expert.** The "climate positive" commitment, QAO's review of early climate planning, and Müller et al. (2021).

**First Nations / Indigenous perspectives expert.** Reconciliation and Indigenous engagement commitments in Games planning and how they are delivered [U on specific Brisbane 2032 documents].

**Political science / media and discourse expert.** Election-cycle analysis (2024), Hansard discourse and media framing.

**Research ethics and data-access / RTI expert.** RTI strategy, ethical handling of leaked or redacted material, and elite-interview supplements if any.

**Examiner / AQF Level 10 quality persona.** Originality claims (e.g., the first decoupling analysis of the 2032 Host Contract and the GIICA reset) and limits of currency.
